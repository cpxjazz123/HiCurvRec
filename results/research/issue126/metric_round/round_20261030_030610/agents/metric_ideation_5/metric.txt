METRIC: hyp_midpoint_rank_gain_l1
TITLE: Level-1 SID cells scored by their tempered hyperbolic expected-rank gained over the exact cell-size prior, with every training query of the history voting and cell centroids at source-target geodesic midpoints.
FORMULA: Poincare ball B = {x in R^d : ||x|| < 1} with constant sectional curvature -1. Distance d(x,y) = arcosh(1 + 2||x-y||^2 / ((1-||x||^2)(1-||y||^2))); exp_x and log_x are the Riemannian exponential and logarithm of B. Item i has frozen source and target representations u_i, v_i in B. Define the item's canonical point a_i = exp_{u_i}( (1/2) * log_{u_i}(v_i) ), the geodesic midpoint of u_i and v_i (fraction 1/2 is the canonical midpoint, not a fitted weight). The table assigns code_1(i) in {1..K}; n_k = #{j : code_1(j) = k}; N = 24587. Cell centroid c_k = argmin_{c in B} sum_{j: code_1(j)=k} d(c, a_j)^2, computed by 50 fixed iterations c <- exp_c( (1/n_k) * sum_j log_c(a_j) ) from the Euclidean mean of the a_j projected into B. Tr = the training split's ordered pairs (i, g) (source item i, gold target item g, both present in the table). tau = the frozen reference temperature, used exactly as the reference uses it, i.e. the reference's score for a candidate x given query q is -d(q,x)/tau. Set k*(i) = code_1(g) for the pair (i,g). For every pair:
  ER_prior(i) = 1 + sum_{k != k*(i)} n_k / (n_k + n_{k*(i)})        [the exact untempered log-prior rank of the gold cell: sigma(log n_k - log n_{k*}) = n_k/(n_k+n_{k*})]
  ER_geo(i)   = 1 + sum_{k != k*(i)} sigma( ( d(u_i, c_k) - d(u_i, c_{k*(i)}) ) / tau ),  sigma(z) = 1/(1+exp(-z))
  M = (1/|Tr|) * sum_{(i,g) in Tr} ( ER_prior(i) - ER_geo(i) ) / K,   and M = 0 when K = 1.
M is one scalar, in [-1, +1], higher is better. It equals the fraction of the K cells whose expected placement the cell geometry resolves beyond what the cells' sizes already resolve.
INPUTS: (1) the exported Stage-2 SID table: item id -> level-1 integer code; (2) the frozen Issue #126 behaviour reference: per-item source representation u_i and target representation v_i in the ball, plus its temperature tau; (3) the training split's (source item, target item) pairs. Nothing else: no level-2/3/4 codes, no validation pair, no test pair, no recall value.
GEOMETRY: curvature -1 enters three times and is load-bearing in each. (i) The comparison between the query and a cell is the Poincare distance d, whose arcosh of the conformal-factor ratio is not a Euclidean distance and cannot be obtained by rescaling one. (ii) Each cell is a genuine Frechet (Karcher) barycentre in the ball, obtained by 50 iterations of the exponential of the mean of tangent logs - an operation with no flat analogue because it depends on the basepoint. (iii) Each item's canonical point is the geodesic midpoint exp_u(log_u(v)/2), so the target half of the reference is transported into the source chart along the geodesic, which again is only defined by the exp/log pair. The curvature is not introduced as a rescaling of a Euclidean score: d appears inside a logistic whose argument is a difference of geodesic distances to the gold centroid, so cells at different radii are treated differently than their Euclidean separation would imply.
WHY IT SHOULD TRACK RECALL: Stage 3 generates candidates through the level-1 prefix and reranks, so recall@10 is governed by one question per query: does the cell holding the true item beat every other cell, under the reference geometry, by more than popularity would already predict. Q_H answers a coarser version (a single prefix ranking score) and HBAA answers a narrower one (one global history barycentre contrasted against the mean of ten negatives through a Gromov inner product). This metric adds three things none of the measured ones has. First, the whole training history enters as a distribution of queries - every training source is its own query point - instead of being collapsed into one barycentre; a table can be good on average and bad for the particular sources Stage 3 will see, and only per-query voting sees that. Second, the negative side is the full tempered distribution over all K cells (an expected rank), not a mean over ten negatives, so a table that leaves a single competing cell close to the true cell is penalised in proportion to the recall it loses, and that penalty is invisible to a mean. Third, the cell centroids are built from the source-target geodesic midpoints, i.e. from the target half of the frozen reference, which every measured metric leaves unused; a prefix that clusters items by what they are, as reachable from their sources, is what makes the prefix a usable candidate filter. The subtraction of the exact size prior is what makes this a quality measure rather than a popularity measure: a cell whose centroid merely sits near the global mean because it is huge gains nothing, so the number cannot be bought by collapsing buckets, and the query-to-target geometry must actually separate the gold cell from all others.
COMPUTE: one pass over 24587 items to build the a_i (24587 exp/log pairs), 50 Karcher sweeps over the same 24587 vectors in total (each item belongs to exactly one cell), then one |Tr| x K matrix of geodesic distances to the centroids and two |Tr| x K logistic reductions; with K <= 256 and |Tr| on the order of the item count this is about 6e6 distance evaluations, a few seconds in vectorised numpy, well under a minute per table, and the a_i and centroids depend only on the table and the reference so they are reused unchanged for the other 96 tables.
RISKS: (1) If tau is large relative to the spread of the geodesic distances, every ER_geo tends to (K+1)/2 regardless of the table and M degenerates into mean(ER_prior) - (K+1)/2 divided by K, i.e. a pure cell-size statistic; if tau is very small the logistic saturates and M becomes a hard rank driven by a handful of near-tied cells and becomes noisy. (2) The construction needs u_i and v_i in the same ball; if the reference stores targets in a different chart or applies a relation transform outside B, the midpoint is undefined and only a relation-corrected substitute would work. (3) Cell sizes are typically skewed, so pairs whose gold cell is tiny contribute ER_prior near K and dominate the mean; M then tracks performance on the rare-cell subset rather than the average, and its variance across the 97 tables is inflated. (4) If the training sources all have nearly the same representation the per-query voting collapses back to a single global query and the metric loses its advantage over HBAA. (5) A coarse table (K = 2..4) whose two or three cells happen to be geometrically clean reaches 0.5-0.75 and is over-rewarded relative to a fine, moderately good table, because the gain is bounded by K-1 while the divisor is only K.
FAILURE MODE: the worst table is the one whose level-1 codes are essentially item-identifying, K close to N with one or two items per code. Then each centroid c_k is the cell's own item midpoint, the cell-level ranking reproduces the reference's own item-level ranking, the size prior is uniform (all n_k = 1 gives ER_prior = (K+1)/2) and M saturates at approximately +0.5 for every such table independently of its content - the number stops discriminating at exactly the regime where the level-1 prefix degenerates into an item id, the same pathology that costs Q_H its Spearman at levels 2 and 3. It is worst in the other direction on a table whose cells split by popularity rather than semantics: the size prior already ranks the gold cell first, ER_prior is near 1, and M is driven to -1 even though the cells are internally coherent.
END METRIC
