METRIC: hyp_horosphere_bucket_lift
TITLE: Horospherical lift of level-1 bucket barycentres against the history's boundary direction, standardised over all buckets.
FORMULA: Fix curvature c=1 (K=-1). Work in the Poincare ball B^d={x in R^d: ||x||<1} with d_B(x,y)=arcosh(1+2||x-y||^2/((1-||x||^2)(1-||y||^2))). Let b(i) in {1..K} be the level-1 integer code of item i, N=24587. Let x_i in B^d be the frozen reference SOURCE representation of item i. For a finite set S, let bar(S)=argmin_{m in B^d} sum_{j in S} d_B(m,x_j)^2, computed by Karcher iteration m <- exp_m((1/|S|) sum_j log_m(x_j)), 10 steps, initialised at the Euclidean mean rescaled to norm 0.9; log/exp are the Poincare ball maps. For each training history h (an item sequence) with next item i+(h): u_h = bar({x_j : j in h}); xi_h = u_h/||u_h|| in boundary S^{d-1} (skip h if ||u_h||<1e-6). Busemann function at boundary point xi: b_xi(x) = log( (1-||x||^2) / ||x-xi||^2 ) (curvature -1; for curvature -c divide by 2*sqrt(c) and replace x by sqrt(c)x). For each level-1 bucket k=1..K let m_k = bar({x_i : b(i)=k}). For each h compute y_k(h)=b_{xi_h}(m_k), then z_k(h)=(y_k(h)-mean_{k'} y_{k'}(h))/(std_{k'} y_{k'}(h)+1e-12). The metric is M = (1/|H|) sum_{h in H} tanh( z_{b(i+(h))}(h) ). H is a deterministic subset of the training histories of size min(|H_train|, 2048), selected by the 2048 smallest 64-bit hashes of the history's item-id tuple. Constants: c=1 (fixed negative curvature), 10 Karcher steps, 2048=2^11 <= N/12 (a fixed compute cap, not tuned), 1e-12 numerical floor; no parameter is fitted to the 97 recalls.
INPUTS: (1) the exported SID table's level-1 integer code per item; (2) the frozen Issue #126 reference's per-item SOURCE representation x_i (training-pairs-only model, no target side, no interaction scores); (3) the TRAINING split histories and their next items. Nothing else: no validation pair, no test pair, no recall, no Stage-3 output.
GEOMETRY: Curvature enters twice. (a) The Karcher barycentre bar(.) is the Frechet mean in the fixed-curvature -1 Poincare ball; its position, not a Euclidean average, defines both the bucket point m_k and the history direction xi_h. (b) The scoring function is the Busemann function of a horosphere, b_xi(x)=log((1-||x||^2)/||x-xi||^2). Horospheres and Busemann functions are a purely negatively-curved object: they are the level sets of the asymptotic "height" toward a boundary point, and the formula uses the Poincare ball's conformal factor. In Euclidean space the same expression has no horosphere meaning, and b_xi is not a distance; it linearises the Gromov product only because of negative curvature. Thus the comparison of a bucket's hyperbolic position against the history's boundary direction is meaningful only through the map.
WHY IT SHOULD TRACK RECALL: For each training history, the frozen reference places the true next item in a particular region of the ball; Stage-3 recall is high when the SID table's level-1 bucket occupies that same region, because beam search then spends its level-1 branch on the right subtree. HBAA measures this with the finite Gromov product (u_h | m_b), i.e. proximity to the history point, and with a mean over ten sampled negatives. This metric instead projects every bucket barycentre onto the horosphere foliation centred at the history's asymptotic direction xi_h and asks whether the true bucket is higher than the table's other buckets, standardised over ALL K-1 negative buckets. It therefore (i) uses the whole bucket through its hyperbolic barycentre, not just a bucket label, (ii) uses the full negative distribution over buckets rather than ten negatives, which removes negative-sampling variance, and (iii) separates "depth toward the predicted future" (Busemann height) from "nearness to the observed history point" (Gromov product): two tables can have the same Gromov alignment yet different horospherical ordering, and the latter is what the beam search consumes. Because z is standardised within each history and the metric is a mean of tanh, a collapsed table (K=1, or all bucket barycentres on one horosphere) returns exactly 0, matching the observed collapse failure of plain path likelihood.
COMPUTE: K<=256 buckets, d is the reference dimension (order 64), |H|<=2048, N=24587. The bucket barycentres m_k are computed once: O(K*N*d*10) in the worst case but only over items, about 4e7 float ops. Each history needs one Karcher mean over its own short sequence and K Busemann evaluations of K*d dot products: 2048*256*64 ~ 3.4e7 float ops. Total well under one minute, no N-by-N matrix and no per-pair Stage-2/3 work.
RISKS: If x_i cluster near the origin, every ||u_h|| is small, xi_h is unstable and b_xi is nearly flat, so z is noise; the 1e-6 and 1e-12 floors only avoid division by zero. If a table has very few buckets, std_k y_k is small and z is volatile; tanh bounds the damage but does not remove it. If the reference's source ball uses a different curvature or scaling than c=1, the Busemann values are rescaled (monotone per history, so z is invariant) but the Karcher means are not; the reference's own curvature must be used as c if it is not 1. Histories longer than the training window are truncated exactly as in the training split. A table that assigns codes by item frequency rather than semantics can still produce nonzero z by chance; the mean over 2048 histories makes this small but not exactly zero.
FAILURE MODE: Worst on a one-code table (K=1: z is 0 by construction, M=0), on a uniformly random relabelling of the codes (E[M]=0), and on any table whose level-1 buckets all have the same Busemann height at the histories' directions (all m_k on a common horosphere, e.g. all bucket barycentres at the origin, giving std_k y_k ~0 and M~0). It is also weak on a table with two enormous buckets split by a direction orthogonal to every xi_h.
END METRIC
