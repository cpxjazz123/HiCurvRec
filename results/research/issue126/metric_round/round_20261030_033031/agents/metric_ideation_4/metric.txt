METRIC: lorentz_pmi_l1
TITLE: Lorentz-hyperboloid posterior-mass lift of the level-1 cell above its training prior (a curvature-exact PMI between the frozen reference and the code).
FORMULA:
  Setup. Items i = 1..N (N = 24587). The table gives level-1 code c1(i) in {1..K}, K <= 256. The frozen Issue #126 reference gives one Euclidean per-item representation x_i in R^d (its source side) and one frozen scalar temperature tau > 0. Fix curvature kappa = 1; nothing is fitted.
  (1) Map.  y_i = tanh(tau*||x_i||) * x_i/||x_i||  (the exp map exp_0(tau x_i) of the unit Poincare ball; y_i = 0 if ||x_i|| = 0).  Lift to the Lorentz hyperboloid:  p_i = ( (1+||y_i||^2)/(1-||y_i||^2) , 2*y_i/(1-||y_i||^2) )  in R^{d+1}.
  (2) Metric.  <a,b>_L = -a_0*b_0 + sum_{k=1..d} a_k*b_k  (Minkowski form, signature (-,+...+) ).  For lifted points <p_i,p_j>_L = -cosh(d_H(i,j)), where d_H is the unit-curvature hyperbolic (Poincare) distance.  So <p_i,p_j>_L is largest (-1) at d_H = 0 and decreases monotonically in d_H.
  (3) Barycentres.  For any finite set S of lifted points, B(S) is the Lorentz/Karcher barycentre: init m = lift of the normalised Euclidean mean of S, then repeat 30 times  m <- exp_m( (1/|S|) * sum_{s in S} log_m(s) ), with exp/log the Lorentz exponential and logarithm at m (closed form: exp_m(v) = cosh(||v||_L) m + sinh(||v||_L) v/||v||_L, log_m(q) = arcosh(<m,q>_L) * (q - <m,q>_L m)/||q - <m,q>_L m||).  Deterministic, no parameter.
  (4) Training quantities only.  Training split T = the list of training pairs (s,t).  For a training query item q, H_q = the multiset of items paired with q in T (its full training history; skip q if H_q is empty).  mu_q = B({p_h : h in H_q}).  For each level-1 cell c with at least one training item, gamma_c = B({p_i : i in T-split, c1(i)=c}), and pi_c = |{i in T-split : c1(i)=c}| / |T-split|.  Let C = {c : pi_c > 0}, K+ = |C|.
  (5) Logit and posterior.  l_{qc} = <mu_q, gamma_c>_L / T  with T = tau (the reference's own frozen temperature; if the reference stores only a ball radius R = tanh(tau), use T = artanh(R)).  Posterior over the whole cell population:  p(c|q) = pi_c * exp(l_{qc}/T) / sum_{c' in C} pi_c' * exp(l_{qc'}/T).
  (6) Score.  m_q = log p(c1(q)|q) - log pi_{c1(q)}  (=  l_{q,c1(q)}/T  -  logsumexp_{c in C}( log pi_c + l_{qc}/T )).  The metric is
        M = (1/|Q|) * sum_{q in Q} m_q ,   Q = training queries with H_q non-empty.
  Higher is better. One number, no thresholds. Degenerate cases: K+ <= 1 gives M = 0 exactly (a one-code table scores 0).
INPUTS: (a) the exported Stage-2 SID table, item_id -> integer codes per level (only level 1 is read); (b) the frozen Issue #126 behaviour reference, i.e. the per-item source representation array x_i (N x d) and its frozen temperature tau; (c) the TRAINING split pair list. Nothing else - no validation pair, no test pair, no recall, no Stage-3 artefact.
GEOMETRY: The whole computation lives on the Lorentz hyperboloid of constant curvature kappa = -1 (unit ball). Curvature enters in three load-bearing places: the exp map exp_0(tau x) that decides how far out in the ball each item sits; the Lorentzian inner product <mu_q,gamma_c>_L = -cosh(d_H) which is the only source of the logits, so separation is measured by a geodesic distance, not a dot product; and the geodesic (Karcher) barycentres, which are Frechet means in the ball and have no Euclidean counterpart. Because cosh grows exponentially, a cell one unit of hyperbolic distance further away is suppressed by a factor e^{-(cosh d2 - cosh d1)/T} - that exponential suppression of the tail is precisely the curvature effect and it is what makes the posterior concentrate without any tuned sharpness.
WHY IT SHOULD TRACK RECALL: Two things are known from the 97 pairs. (i) Statistics of the partition alone do not track recall, because they never look at what the items are. (ii) Contrast statistics on the frozen reference do track it (+0.70 for HBAA vs +0.634 for Q_H), because a cell that is close to where the item's own behaviour history points is a cell whose ten retrieved neighbours will be the right items. This metric keeps (ii) and adds the two pieces of information HBAA throws away. First, the query is the geodesic barycentre of the FULL training history, not a single anchor, so a query is scored against the region of behaviour space its whole history occupies. Second, the negatives are not averaged: the reference is asked for a full posterior over all K+ cells at the reference's own temperature, and the score is the posterior mass it puts on the positive cell - an argmax-like readout that is sensitive to the whole negative distribution (a positive that beats the mean negative but is outranked by 40% of cells is penalised, which a mean-of-ten margin cannot see). Third, and unlike every likelihood tried so far, the mass is divided by the cell's own training prior pi, i.e. it is a pointwise mutual information between "what the reference says" and "what the code says". That denominator is a closed-form size-matched null, so a table cannot win by collapsing: the best attainable score is mean_q log(1/pi_{c1(q)}), which is log 2 = 0.693 for a balanced two-cell table and log 256 = 5.545 for a balanced 256-cell table. Collapse is therefore structurally unprofitable, and resolution is rewarded, without a second number or a capacity correction. What it adds over Q_H: Q_H scores a prefix by its own geometry with no query and no contrast; this scores the code by how much the reference's behaviour geometry, conditioned on the item's full history, prefers that cell over what the code's own base rate already implies. Over HBAA: HBAA's Delta is linear in four geodesic distances (algebraically it is half the sum of the origin-margin and the query-margin), it sees exactly two points, and it is unbounded and scale-sensitive; this one is curvature-exact (cosh saturation over the whole cell population), uses all K+ cells with their priors, and is bounded above by mean log(1/pi), so it cannot be inflated by representation scale.
COMPUTE: d ~ 64, K <= 256, N = 24587, |Q| <= 24587, average history length ~10. Lift: O(N d). Cell and query barycentres: 30 iterations of a matrix product each, O(30 N d) = ~5e7 flops total, done once per table. Logits: one (|Q| x (d+1)) by ((d+1) x K+) product = 4e8 flops, plus a row-wise logsumexp over <= 256 columns. Memory peak 24587 x 256 float32 = 25 MB. Fully vectorised this is a few seconds; well inside one minute, single core, CPU only.
RISKS: (1) Temperature mis-scaling: if tau is not the scalar Issue #126 actually used, the posterior becomes either saturated (M degenerates to a hard argmax with log-odds) or flat (M -> 0 for every table); it is one global constant per reference, identical for all 97 tables, so it changes the level of M but only mildly its rank order - still, it is the main fragility and must be taken verbatim from the reference, never fitted. (2) Level-1 only: at level 2+ the cell centroids collapse onto items, so the posterior becomes an item lookup and the prior pi re-inflates the score; this metric deliberately does not use levels 2+. (3) Sparse histories: queries with |H_q| < 2 give noisy mu_q; they are included (|H_q| >= 1) but a table whose training pairs are mostly singletons dilutes the signal toward 0. (4) Cells with very few training items get unstable gamma_c; they are kept because dropping them by a size threshold would be a tuned parameter. (5) A reference whose source side is itself nearly isotropic makes all gamma_c nearly equal, so all logits nearly equal and M nearly the mean over q of log(1/pi) minus a constant - a positive but low-variance artefact; this is visible as a near-zero spread of M across the 97 tables.
FAILURE MODE: It must be worst on the table where level-1 is a fixed balanced random relabelling (e.g. a hash of the item id into 256 equal blocks): then gamma_c is a barycentre of a random subsample, all gamma_c sit near the ball centre with almost identical distances to mu_q, the posterior returns to the prior pi_c, and m_q = log p - log pi -> 0 for every q, so M ~ 0 with no variance. It is equally worst on the single-code table (K+ = 1, M = 0 by definition) and on any table whose level-1 blocks are random with respect to behaviour but perfectly balanced (so no prior tilt can rescue it).
END METRIC
