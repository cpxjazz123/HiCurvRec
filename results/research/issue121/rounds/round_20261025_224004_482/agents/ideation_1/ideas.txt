IDEA: cohort_ball_anchors
TITLE: Place first-level prototypes at training-only successor-cohort anchors
MOTIVATION: A 256-way first token is too expensive for a width-20 beam, but merging items arbitrarily loses behavioral utility. Items repeatedly reached from the same training-history cohort should share one of a small set of behaviorally meaningful first-level regions; later levels retain item discrimination.
NOVELTY: This moves behavior into the locations of the first-level hyperbolic prototypes, not into an auxiliary prefix-occupancy loss or an item-specific curvature. Unlike a codebook-size-only change, the small codebook is initialized and continuously pulled by cohort-conditioned curved anchors. Use 32 effective L1 prototypes; L2/L3 have 256 each and L4 remains deterministic collision extension.
CHANGE: From training-only directed transitions, compute a soft successor-cohort vector for every item and its cohort anchor in the Poincare ball. During L1 quantization, score each prototype by Poincare distance from the encoded item to a behavior-conditioned prototype location, and update that location toward the weighted cohort anchor; quantize residuals at subsequent levels as usual. Capacity budget: L1 32 groups (at most 5 bits); L2/L3 carry discrimination (up to 8 bits each); T4 is near-constant, only collision extension (target entropy <= 0.1 nat).
SPECIFICATION: Let A[i,j] be train transition counts from history item i to next item j, with rows normalized after log-popularity correction. Define b[j]=normalize(sum_i A[i,j] e_i), where e_i is the one-hot training source-item ID; project b[j] through a learned low-rank map to u[j] in the tangent space. For each cohort k, soft membership q[j,k]=softmax_k(-||u[j]-a[k]||^2/tau_b). Maintain anchor h[k]=Proj(sum_j q[j,k] z[j]/sum_j q[j,k]) in the ball. Let c[k] be L1 codewords mapped by exp_0; use score[j,k]=d_P(Exp_0(z[j]), Exp_0(c[k])+_P eta Log_{Exp_0(c[k])}(h[k]))^2, and assign argmin_k score. Here +_P denotes Poincare Mobius addition, and Proj clips to radius (1-eps)/sqrt(c). Train with ordinary straight-through RQ reconstruction/commitment gradients plus a small anchor update from these assignments; do not add an independent behavior loss.
COST: Store sparse transition rows and 32 anchors. Per step at frozen scale, compute O(24,556*32*d) first-level curved scores plus sparse transition aggregation amortized over the epoch; roughly one 32-way distance pass, expected <10% Stage2 wall-time overhead over Euclidean 256-way assignment (measure on four cards). Across 24 steps/epoch and 3000 epochs, 73,668,000 item-step assignments; no extra Stage3 cost.
HYPOTHESIS: Shared successor cohorts occupy compact curved neighborhoods, letting L1 be both useful and beam-cheap. Keep only if, versus both ablations, L1 Hard ER rises from 1 toward a useful nontrivial range (roughly 0.75-0.95 on canonical 52,855 pairs with ten fixed negatives), depth-1 gold-prefix survival improves, depth-2/3 survival remains high, depth-4 survival does not regress, T4 NLL stays approximately baseline/near-constant, and frozen Stage3 test_R@10 exceeds 0.065.
RISKS: Cohorts can be popularity proxies; noisy or diffuse incoming transition profiles can collapse anchors; the small L1 capacity can merge semantically incompatible items; Mobius updates near the boundary are unstable; a cohort estimate based on sparse training histories may not generalize.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Directed, popularity-adjusted training-only incoming successor cohort of each item.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: From training split sequences only, count directed adjacent transitions i->j; weight each occurrence by 1/log(2+outdegree(i)) and 1/sqrt(1+popularity(j)), aggregate a sparse incoming source-item profile per target j, normalize, then compute soft cohort memberships and anchors. No validation/test histories, transitions, or item co-occurrence are read.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: Freeze the sparse transition matrix, cohort projection and anchors from training interactions before SID export. Validation/test are used only in the prescribed later evaluation, never for anchor construction, tuning, or assignment.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Poincare exp/log maps, Mobius addition and squared Poincare geodesic distance at fixed negative curvature -c, c=1 (learnable c may be omitted in first implementation).
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: L1 prototype placement and assignment in Stage2 training and final SID quantization; L2/L3 retain the existing residual quantization path.
COUPLING PATH: Training transition profiles determine q[j,k] and h[k]; h[k] moves the actual curved L1 prototype before its geodesic assignment score is computed. Removing behavior makes h[k] zero; replacing Poincare operations with Euclidean operations changes the same score and prototype update.
COUPLING EQUATION: score(j,k)=d_P(Exp_0(z_j), Exp_0(c_k)+_P eta Log_{Exp_0(c_k)}(h_k))^2, h_k=Proj(sum_j q_beh(j,k) Exp_0(z_j)/sum_j q_beh(j,k)); assignment(j)=argmin_k score(j,k).
OBSERVABLES: Report L1 Hard ER on canonical 52,855 pairs with ten fixed negatives, gold-prefix survival at depths 1-4, T4 NLL, and Stage3 test_R@10. Both-on should reduce effective L1 branching versus 256-way arms without sacrificing depth-2/3 target survival; desired test_R@10 > 0.065.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Set q_beh uniform and all h[k]=0; retain 32 L1 codes and the identical Poincare distance, training schedule, and downstream levels.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Replace exp/log, Mobius addition and d_P with identity/addition and squared Euclidean distance; keep the exact training-derived q_beh, anchor updates and 32-code capacity.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Train with the above training-only q_beh-driven anchor placement and fixed-curvature Poincare assignment; use frozen Stage3 protocol and no early stopping or test-time tuning.
SPEC EQUATIONS: A_ij=count_train(i->j)/((log(2+outdeg_train(i)))*sqrt(1+pop_train(j))); p_j=normalize(A[:,j]); q_j=softmax(-||W p_j-a_k||^2/tau_b); h_k=Proj(sum_j q_jk Exp_0(z_j)/max(eps,sum_j q_jk)); v_k=Proj(Exp_0(c_k)+_P eta Log_{Exp_0(c_k)}(h_k)); l1_j=argmin_k d_P(Exp_0(z_j),v_k)^2. Use straight-through selected code and ordinary residual quantization for L2/L3.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs are training-only sparse directed transitions, encoder item vectors z, 32 tangent-space code vectors c_k, and learned cohort projection W/a_k. Outputs are L1 assignments, curved prototype vectors v_k and residuals passed to L2/L3.
SAFEGUARDS: Normalize sparse profiles with eps; cap transition weights and anchor movement eta<=0.1; clamp curvature positive; project ball points to radius (1-1e-5)/sqrt(c); clamp acosh arguments to >=1+1e-6; clamp norms/divisors; detach cohort membership for each assignment pass and update anchors by EMA; deterministic fixed tie-breaking; persist train-only provenance.
END IDEA

IDEA: cohort_transport_sinkhorn
TITLE: Route successor demand through capacity-balanced hyperbolic first-level assignment
MOTIVATION: A beam loses targets when first-level assignments fragment the same next-item cohorts or overload a few prefix branches. Balance a small first-level vocabulary by training successor demand while retaining curved, item-dependent geodesic preferences, rather than trusting unconstrained nearest-code assignment.
NOVELTY: The behavior signal is a sparse target-demand distribution that enters the transport cost matrix itself, and a capacity-constrained Sinkhorn projection jointly selects curved assignments. This is not a detached popularity/prefix-mass penalty and is not merely reducing codebook size; behavior changes the per-item curved ranking and the global assignment competition. Use 32 L1 groups, L2/L3 for discrimination, and deterministic T4 near-constant collision extension.
CHANGE: At each Stage2 assignment step, compute a training-only successor-demand affinity between each item and each L1 group from transitions among the current batch's training items. Add that affinity directly to the negative-curvature geodesic cost before entropic Sinkhorn with uniform 32-group capacity, then use the resulting soft assignment for the straight-through L1 code and residual levels. Capacity budget: L1 32 balanced groups (~5 bits); L2/L3 hold remaining useful information; T4 target <=0.1 nat as collision extension only.
SPECIFICATION: For training transitions define directed neighbor weights P(j|i) from popularity-adjusted counts. Give each L1 group k a trainable successor-profile prototype r_k over a fixed low-rank random-feature embedding phi(j) of target IDs. For source item i, s_ik=cosine(sum_j P(j|i)phi(j),r_k). Let D_ik=d_L(-c)(Exp_0(z_i), Exp_0(c_k))^2 - lambda*s_ik. In each minibatch solve Q=Sinkhorn(exp(-D/tau), row_mass=1, col_mass=B/32) for batch size B; assignment is argmax_k Q_ik, with soft Q used in straight-through gradients. Update r_k only from training transition profiles assigned to k.
COST: O(B*32*d) Lorentz/Poincare cost plus Sinkhorn O(I*B*32) per minibatch, where I=20 fixed iterations; transition affinity via sparse neighbor gather (cap 64 neighbors/item) adds O(B*64*r). At frozen scale B=24,556 per step, this is ~786k curved pair scores and ~15.7m simple Sinkhorn scalar updates per step; 24 steps/epoch x 3000 epochs = 73,668,000 item-step rows, memory chunked by minibatch to avoid full Bx32 materialization. Expect moderate overhead; benchmark wall time against baseline, no early stopping.
HYPOTHESIS: Demand-aware capacity balancing can align the 32 feasible prefix branches with predictable training successors and prevent a few branches from dominating. Keep only if both-on has improved depth-1 survival and depth-2/3 survival relative to behavior-off and Euclidean controls, useful L1 Hard ER (not trivially 1.0), near-constant T4 NLL, and Stage3 test_R@10 > 0.065 under frozen protocol.
RISKS: Sinkhorn's equal capacity can conflict with naturally skewed demand; target-profile affinity may overfit frequent transitions; entropic assignments may be too soft for useful discrete SIDs; 20 iterations and distance computation add cost; batch-local balancing may introduce assignment instability.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Popularity-adjusted directed outgoing successor distributions P(j|i) for each training item i.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: Count adjacent item transitions only in training sequences. Normalize each source row after inverse-log source-frequency and inverse-square-root target-popularity correction; retain at most the top 64 training successors per source, renormalize, and map successors into fixed random features phi(j). Validation/test sequences are excluded.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: Transition rows, item popularity, feature map and group profiles are constructed/fitted using training interactions only and frozen by SID export. Do not calculate transitions or adjust features/thresholds from validation or test; Stage3 evaluation remains the fixed downstream protocol.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Fixed-curvature c=1 Lorentz-hyperboloid geodesic distance (negative sectional curvature -c) between lifted encoder vectors and first-level codewords; it is the base assignment cost inside Sinkhorn.
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: First-level Stage2 assignment cost matrix before capacity-constrained Sinkhorn and discrete SID extraction.
COUPLING PATH: Training successor affinity modifies each item's geodesic cost D_ik, and Sinkhorn transports assignment mass according to that same behavior-modified curved matrix. With behavior off the cost ranking changes; with geometry off the cost matrix's curved term is replaced, affecting the same transport solution.
COUPLING EQUATION: D_ik=d_L(-c)(Exp_0(z_i),Exp_0(c_k))^2 - lambda*s_beh(i,k); Q=Sinkhorn(exp(-D/tau), row=1, col=B/32); assignment_i=argmax_k Q_ik.
OBSERVABLES: Measure L1 Hard ER on canonical 52,855 pairs with ten fixed negatives, gold-prefix survival at depths 1,2,3,4, T4 NLL, and Stage3 test_R@10. Expected direction: depth-1 survival higher than unconstrained 256-way behavior arms, depth-2/3 retention higher than geometry-only capacity control, T4 NLL stays low, and test_R@10 exceeds 0.065. ER should be nontrivial below 1 and reflect ten-negative discrimination.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Set lambda=0 and remove successor profiles while retaining the same 32-way Lorentz cost and identical Sinkhorn capacities/iterations.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Replace Lorentz geodesic distance with squared Euclidean distance in D_ik; retain the training-only affinity, lambda, 32 groups and same Sinkhorn.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Use the training-only successor affinity plus fixed-curvature Lorentz cost in one D matrix, with 32-way capacity Sinkhorn; all other training and Stage3 settings frozen.
SPEC EQUATIONS: P_ij=normalize_j(count_train(i->j)/(log(2+outdeg(i))*sqrt(1+pop(j)))); u_i=normalize(sum_j P_ij phi(j)); s_beh(i,k)=u_i dot r_k; D_ik=d_L(-1)(Exp_0(z_i),Exp_0(c_k))^2-lambda*s_beh(i,k); K_ik=exp(-D_ik/tau); Q=diag(a)Kdiag(b), iterate a=1/(Kb), b=(B/32)/(K^T a) for 20 iterations; hard code argmax_k Q_ik; residual quantize downstream.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs are training-only sparse transitions/popularity, training item embeddings, fixed phi feature vectors, 32 Lorentz/tangent codewords and trainable r_k. Outputs are a Bx32 soft transport assignment, discrete L1 tokens and residuals for L2/L3.
SAFEGUARDS: Compute transport in log space with stabilized logsumexp; clamp D and log-kernel; use positive tau and lambda caps; normalize profiles with eps; cap neighbors at 64; chunk the Bx32 matrix if needed; project Lorentz points to the valid sheet after exp-map; clamp acosh argument; ensure batch size is divisible by 32 or use exact remainder column masses summing to B; stop Sinkhorn on fixed 20 iterations (no validation-driven tuning); deterministic tie-breaking.
END IDEA
