IDEA: successor_transport_code
TITLE: Parallel-transported successor displacement as a latent prediction target
MOTIVATION: An item’s next-item behaviour depends on which successor is likely, not only on its static content or neighbourhood. Training the latent to represent the typical direction from an item to its successors may make quantized IDs useful for next-item prediction.
NOVELTY: The behaviour signal supervises a tangent displacement between the source and successor representations, rather than clustering successors, modifying quantizer assignments, or adding an independent geodesic-cohesion loss.
CHANGE: Map encoder latents to the Poincaré ball and train each source latent to point along the ball’s Fréchet-mean successor direction after parallel transport to the origin. The transported direction and hyperbolic distance define a predicted successor point, which is compared to the actual successor using Poincaré distance before the ordinary RQ-VAE quantization and reconstruction losses.
SPECIFICATION: For each training pair (i,j), let x_i,x_j be the ball-mapped encoder outputs and μ_i the Poincaré Fréchet mean of the observed successors of i. Set v_i = PT_{x_i→0}(log_{x_i}(μ_i)); set u_ij = PT_{x_i→0}(log_{x_i}(x_j)). Regress v_i toward the behavior-weighted mean of u_ij using a Huber loss in the origin tangent space. Use the training-pair frequency as the weight, normalized to mean one; multiply this loss by the baseline RQ-VAE commitment-loss coefficient. The behaviour-off switch sets the loss to zero; geometry-off uses Euclidean means, differences, and distances; both-on uses the specified hyperbolic operations. Train only the both-on candidate.
COST: At most 24,556 source-item successor summaries per epoch, computed once from training data and cached; each minibatch step adds one batched log/parallel-transport calculation and a vector regression over its sampled items. This is O(Bd) per step at batch size B and latent dimension d, versus the baseline’s O(BKd) quantizer assignment cost for K codewords. Negligible summary cost amortized over 3,000 epochs; one 24-step epoch adds 24 such minibatch calculations.
HYPOTHESIS: If successor directions contain useful information not captured by static item embeddings, the coupled objective will change encoder latents and their quantized SIDs in ways that improve next-item prediction; retain only if the single frozen Stage 3 test Recall@10 exceeds 0.065.
RISKS: High-degree sources may have diffuse or multimodal successor sets, making their Fréchet mean unrepresentative. Rare sources may have unstable means; the regression may also compete with reconstruction and reduce content fidelity.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Directed training-only item transitions and their empirical successor distribution.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: From training-split user sequences only, count adjacent pairs i→j. For each source i, compute the normalized successor frequencies and their weighted Fréchet mean in the current ball representation; summaries are refreshed on each training step for the sampled sources.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: Build the transition counts exclusively from the Stage 2 training split. No validation or test interactions, successors, or statistics are read when constructing counts or means; Stage 3 evaluation uses the frozen trained SID model.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Poincaré log and exp maps, the Poincaré Fréchet mean, and parallel transport in curvature −1.
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: In the Stage 2 encoder objective, before residual quantization; the predicted successor direction updates the encoder representation that the quantizer subsequently consumes.
COUPLING PATH: Training transitions determine the successor mean and displacement target; their tangent vectors are parallel-transported in the Poincaré ball and regress the encoder latent’s transported direction. Removing the transitions removes the target; replacing the ball operations with Euclidean operations changes the target and gradient path.
COUPLING EQUATION: L_succ = Σ_i w_i Huber(PT_{x_i→0}[log_{x_i}(μ_i)] − Σ_j p(j|i) PT_{x_i→0}[log_{x_i}(x_j)]); x_i=exp_0(z_i), μ_i=Frechet_{−1}({x_j,p(j|i)}), and w_i is normalized training successor mass.
OBSERVABLES: With coupling on, the encoder latent and resulting SID assignments should respond to successor-direction structure; the decisive observable is Stage 3 test Recall@10 > 0.065. Record transition-prediction diagnostics, but do not use them as admission gates.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Set all transition-derived targets and weights to zero, leaving the canonical baseline objective and quantizer unchanged. This switch is implemented for comparison only; do not train this arm.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Replace the Fréchet mean, log/exp maps, and parallel transport with Euclidean means and differences, retaining the same training transitions and objective structure. This switch is implemented for comparison only; do not train this arm.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Use training-only successor transitions and the curvature −1 Poincaré operations in the coupled objective above. Train this single candidate under the frozen budget and judge it only by the frozen Stage 3 test Recall@10.
SPEC EQUATIONS: p(j|i)=count_train(i→j)/Σ_k count_train(i→k); μ_i=Frechet_{−1}({x_j,p(j|i)}); a_i=Σ_j p(j|i) PT_{x_i→0}(log_{x_i}(x_j)); b_i=PT_{x_i→0}(log_{x_i}(μ_i)); L_succ=Σ_i w_i Huber(b_i−a_i). Add L_succ with the baseline commitment-loss coefficient. Poincaré maps and transport use curvature −1; Euclidean-off uses the corresponding arithmetic mean and vector differences.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs are minibatch encoder latents, cached training-only transition counts, and successor latents for sampled sources. Output is the scalar successor-direction loss and gradients to the encoder; the standard RQ-VAE path produces SIDs.
SAFEGUARDS: Clamp Poincaré norms below 1−ε before log/exp/distance calculations; use ε=1e−5 in the model dtype. Normalize transition probabilities with a denominator floor; skip sources with no observed successors. Accumulate Fréchet iterations in float32, stop at the fixed numerical tolerance 1e−5 or 10 iterations, and detach the mean’s iterative solver if needed for memory while retaining gradients through the sampled successor vectors.
END IDEA

IDEA: branch_conditioned_codebook_temperature
TITLE: Branching-dependent hyperbolic soft assignment temperature
MOTIVATION: Items with many distinct observed continuations may need more flexible semantic-code assignment than items with a concentrated successor set. A training-only branching signal can modulate how sharply each item chooses a code, with hyperbolic distance determining that choice.
NOVELTY: Unlike behaviour-weighted Euclidean assignment or a separate behaviour loss, this uses successor-set branching to control the temperature of the actual negatively curved code-assignment distribution that produces the quantized representation.
CHANGE: Compute a scale-free successor branching score from training transitions, then use it to set each item’s soft-assignment temperature over Poincaré codewords. Use the resulting probabilities for the existing straight-through hard code selection and commitment path; do not add an independent behaviour regularizer.
SPECIFICATION: For source item i, compute successor entropy H_i=−Σ_j p(j|i)log p(j|i) and normalize by log(max(2,n_i)), where n_i is its number of distinct training successors; set h_i=H_i/log(max(2,n_i)), clipped to [0,1]. For each quantization level ℓ, let d_{iℓk} be the squared Poincaré distance from the current residual point to codeword k. Set τ_i=median_batch(d)/(median_train(h)+ε)·(ε+h_i), using fixed training-wide median values and ε=1e−5; compute q_{iℓk}=softmax_k(−d_{iℓk}/τ_i). Use the ordinary hard argmin for the forward quantized code and q for its existing straight-through gradient. The behaviour-off switch sets h_i to its training-wide median; geometry-off replaces Poincaré distances by squared Euclidean distances; both-on uses the specified entropy and Poincaré distances. Train only both-on.
COST: Transition counts and normalized entropies are computed once from training interactions, O(number of training transitions), then cached. Each minibatch step adds O(BK) to compute per-item temperatures and probabilities, which is the baseline assignment softmax’s order of work; the Poincaré distance is likewise evaluated across B×K items and codewords. At the frozen scale this runs for 24 steps per epoch over 3,000 epochs, with no additional full-data pass per epoch.
HYPOTHESIS: If successor branching should affect how much code-choice uncertainty an item retains, per-item temperatures may encode useful variation into the quantized identifiers and improve the single Stage 3 test Recall@10 beyond 0.065. Branching and assignment diagnostics are recorded, never used as admission gates.
RISKS: The baseline straight-through estimator may not benefit from itemwise temperature variation; diffuse successors may indicate noise rather than meaningful uncertainty. The global median scale could be poorly calibrated to code distances, and temperature differences may have little effect on hard assignments.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Normalized entropy of each item’s observed directed successor distribution.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: Count adjacent item transitions within the Stage 2 training-split user sequences; compute p(j|i), distinct successor count n_i, and normalized entropy h_i from those counts only.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: Transition counts, entropy values, and training-wide normalization statistics are derived solely from the Stage 2 training split. No validation or test interactions enter assignment temperatures, codebook updates, or SID construction.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Squared Poincaré distance at fixed curvature −1, used inside the per-item softmax over codewords.
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: The RQ-VAE quantizer’s code-assignment computation at each residual level, before straight-through hard selection.
COUPLING PATH: Successor entropy determines each item’s temperature; temperature scales the Poincaré codeword distances in the soft-assignment logits; those probabilities provide the straight-through gradient that updates the encoder and codebooks, and the resulting hard choices form the SID.
COUPLING EQUATION: q_{iℓk}=softmax_k(−d_{−1}(x_{iℓ},c_{ℓk})²/τ_i), with τ_i=median_batch(d²)(ε+h_i)/(ε+median_train(h)); the behaviour term h_i and curved term d_{−1} meet inside the same assignment logits.
OBSERVABLES: The coupled pathway should alter per-item assignment probabilities, gradients, and potentially hard SID choices as a function of successor branching. Record these changes and the behavioural diagnostics; decide solely by whether Stage 3 test Recall@10 exceeds 0.065.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Replace h_i by its training-wide median for all items, removing item-specific behaviour modulation while keeping the Poincaré code assignment. This switch is implemented for comparison only; do not train this arm.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Replace squared Poincaré distance with squared Euclidean distance in assignment logits, retaining the same training-derived h_i and temperature rule. This switch is implemented for comparison only; do not train this arm.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Use the item-specific training-only normalized successor entropy and squared Poincaré codeword distances in the temperature-scaled assignment softmax. Train this single candidate under the frozen budget and judge it only by Stage 3 test Recall@10.
SPEC EQUATIONS: Let n_i=|{j:count_train(i→j)>0}| and p(j|i)=count_train(i→j)/Σ_k count_train(i→k). Let h_i=H(p(·|i))/log(max(2,n_i)). Let m_d=median of squared codeword distances over the current minibatch’s assignments and m_h=median_train(h). Let τ_i=m_d(ε+h_i)/(ε+m_h), ε=1e−5. For every quantization level ℓ, q_{iℓk}=softmax_k(−d_{−1}(x_{iℓ},c_{ℓk})²/τ_i); take the usual hard argmin and use q in the baseline straight-through update.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs are residual encoder vectors per quantization level, Poincaré codebook vectors, cached training-only successor entropy h_i, and fixed training-wide median m_h. Outputs are per-level assignment probabilities, hard code indices, quantized vectors, and the ordinary RQ-VAE loss/gradients.
SAFEGUARDS: Project ball points and codewords to norm ≤1−1e−5; compute distances and softmax logits in float32. Clamp τ_i below by ε times the minibatch median distance plus a dtype-safe floor; if all distances are zero, use the dtype-safe floor. For n_i≤1 set h_i=0; use a documented zero-successor fallback of the training-wide median h. Detach the minibatch median scale so it stabilizes logits without introducing cross-example gradients.
END IDEA
