IDEA: branch_tangent_codes
TITLE: User-branch tangent prototypes for residual code assignment
MOTIVATION: A single item embedding may participate in distinct next-item contexts; ordinary residual quantization can average those contexts and obscure which continuation a prefix implies.
NOVELTY: Unlike a graph-denoised residual or behavior-only SID suffix, this changes the actual residual codebook assignment metric by transporting a train-only, context-conditioned direction through a negatively curved latent space.
CHANGE: Build a sparse distribution over next-item contexts for each item from training sequences, map its context centroid to the Poincare ball, and use that point to define a context-dependent tangent displacement before every residual quantizer assignment.
SPECIFICATION: Let x_i be the frozen item embedding and e_i its encoder output. For each item i, compute p_i(j) proportional to count(i->j)^0.75 over outgoing training transitions, retaining top 16 successors; let h_i be their mean frozen embeddings. Map z_i=exp^c_0(W e_i) and b_i=exp^c_0(U h_i) into the ball of curvature -c (c=0.5). Define v_i=log^c_{z_i}(b_i), and transformed residual target y_i=log^c_0(exp^c_{z_i}(alpha v_i)), alpha=0.15. At quantizer level l choose k=argmin_k d_c(y_i-r_i^l, q_lk)^2, then r_i^(l+1)=r_i^l+q_lk in tangent coordinates; retain baseline straight-through update and reconstruction loss. Apply behavior through v_i only for items with at least two observed successors; otherwise set v_i=0.
COST: Precompute sparse transition summaries once; per epoch batch cost is O(B*16*d + B*L*K*d) with the extra O(B*d) map operations; codebook comparison already dominates. At frozen scale (24,556 items, 24 steps/epoch, 3,000 epochs), this adds 24*3,000*B*16*d arithmetic for context aggregation (or cache h_i and pay O(24,556*16*d) once), plus O(24*3,000*B*L*d) curved maps; no extra model passes. Expect roughly 5-15% step time depending on batch and codebook sizes.
HYPOTHESIS: Items with branching successor distributions need a context-aware residual direction; the coupled assignment should improve Stage3 test Recall@10, targeting >0.065, and make leading SID prefixes more predictive of the training successor distribution. Keep only if both-on beats baseline and both ablations.
RISKS: Sparse or noisy transitions may overfit; frequent successors may dominate; curvature/maps can destabilize mixed precision; the context adjustment may hurt semantic reconstruction and create worse prefixes.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Directed next-item transition distribution, preserving branching rather than reducing each item to one neighbor.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: From training-only user sequences in timestamp order, count adjacent item pairs (i,j); discard self-loops, retain top 16 outgoing successors by count, apply count^0.75 normalization, and average corresponding frozen item embeddings to form h_i.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: Construct all counts from the training interaction split only, freeze h_i before RQ-VAE fitting, and never read validation/test sequences when creating transition statistics or SIDs.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Poincare-ball exp^c and log^c maps plus geodesic distance, fixed sectional curvature -0.5.
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: The behavior-conditioned displacement is formed in the Poincare tangent space before each RQ-VAE residual quantizer assignment.
COUPLING PATH: Training transition counts make h_i; h_i determines b_i; the curved log map from z_i to b_i determines y_i; y_i changes the distances and chosen residual code at every level.
COUPLING EQUATION: y_i=log^c_0(exp^c_{z_i}(alpha*log^c_{z_i}(b_i))); k_i^l=argmin_k d_c(y_i-r_i^l,q_lk)^2, where b_i=exp^c_0(U*sum_j p_i(j)x_j).
OBSERVABLES: Changed code assignments and encoder/codebook gradients for branching items; compare successor-distribution predictability from SID prefixes and Stage3 test Recall@10.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Set h_i=0 and hence v_i=0 while retaining identical curved quantization and all other settings.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Replace exp/log/d_c with identity maps and squared Euclidean distance, preserving h_i and the behavior-dependent displacement formula in Euclidean space.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Use training-derived h_i and the curvature -0.5 Poincare displacement and assignment as specified.
SPEC EQUATIONS: p_i(j)=count_train(i,j)^0.75/sum_{u in Top16(i)}count_train(i,u)^0.75; h_i=sum_j p_i(j)x_j; z_i=exp^c_0(W e_i); b_i=exp^c_0(U h_i); y_i=log^c_0(exp^c_{z_i}(alpha log^c_{z_i}(b_i))); k_i^l=argmin_k d_c(y_i-r_i^l,q_lk)^2. Use baseline STE and reconstruction/commitment losses; do not add a separate behavior-only objective.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs: frozen item embeddings, training-split sequences, encoder outputs, residual codebooks. Output: per-item residual code indices/SIDs and the unchanged reconstruction loss path.
SAFEGUARDS: Clip tangent norms to 3/sqrt(c), project ball points to norm <=(1-1e-5)/sqrt(c), compute maps/distances in float32 with eps=1e-6, normalize only nonempty transition rows, use zero context for unsupported items, and clamp distance arguments to valid domains.
END IDEA
IDEA: transition_geodesic_codebook
TITLE: Geodesic transition barycenters as residual-code targets
MOTIVATION: Next-item prediction depends on local directed transitions, but ordinary codebook centroids are optimized to reconstruct item embeddings rather than to preserve the geometry of likely continuations.
NOVELTY: Rather than attach graph regularization to an otherwise standard quantizer, this uses the training-only transition graph to construct geodesic barycentric targets that directly determine the RQ-VAE codebook assignment and its gradient.
CHANGE: Maintain a slowly updated Poincare prototype for each item’s observed next-item neighborhood and interpolate the item latent toward that prototype along the geodesic before residual quantization; the interpolation weight is the item’s empirical transition entropy, so highly branching items receive more continuation structure.
SPECIFICATION: From training sequences count outgoing pairs, compute normalized p_i(j), entropy H_i=-sum_j p_i(j)log(p_i(j)+eps), and gate g_i=clip(H_i/log(max(2,degree_i)),0,1) (set 0 for degree<2). Given encoded z_i=exp^c_0(W e_i), compute neighborhood prototype m_i as the 3-step weighted Frechet mean initialized at weighted Euclidean mean of exp^c_0(W x_j): m <- exp^c_m(eta*sum_j p_i(j)log^c_m(z_j)); eta=0.25. Use target t_i=exp^c_{z_i}(beta*g_i*log^c_{z_i}(m_i)), beta=0.2, then assign first residual code by argmin_k d_c(t_i,q_1k)^2; later levels quantize tangent residual log^c_0(t_i)-sum_{s<l}q_sk. Backpropagate through t_i and maps; update codebooks with baseline commitment/reconstruction losses.
COST: Precompute sparse neighbor lists and entropy once. Cache item z_j and m_i once per epoch (one batched 3-iteration neighborhood aggregation, O(24,556*16*d) map work); per training step add O(B*L*d) maps and no additional codebook scan beyond existing comparisons. Across 3,000 epochs/24 steps, cached neighbor update is 3,000*24,556*16*d scalar operations, while step overhead is about O(24*3,000*B*L*d), approximately 5-12% wall-clock if the codebook search dominates.
HYPOTHESIS: High-entropy transition neighborhoods are better represented by a curved local continuation barycenter than by a Euclidean reconstruction-only code; the effect should improve next-item prefix utility and Stage3 test Recall@10, with target above 0.065.
RISKS: Neighborhood barycenters can collapse unrelated branches; popularity bias may pull prototypes toward hubs; stale cached embeddings can make means inconsistent; small item support makes entropy unreliable.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Per-item directed next-item neighborhood distribution and its normalized branching entropy.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: On training-only chronological user sequences, count each adjacent pair; form p_i over top 16 successors using add-one smoothing followed by renormalization; compute normalized entropy and weighted successor embedding mean.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: Training interactions alone produce graph, probabilities, entropy and neighbor lists; validation/test interactions are excluded from statistics, prototype initialization, training, and SID export.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Poincare geodesic interpolation via log^c_z, exp^c_z and distance d_c, with fixed curvature -0.5.
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: A transition-neighborhood Frechet mean and entropy-gated geodesic target shift are computed inside the quantization forward path before the first residual assignment.
COUPLING PATH: The directed transition distribution defines the curved Frechet mean; its entropy gates a geodesic move from each encoded item toward that mean; the moved point is then directly quantized, changing code indices and gradients.
COUPLING EQUATION: m_i=FrechetMean_c({z_j,p_i(j)}); t_i=exp^c_{z_i}(beta*g_i*log^c_{z_i}(m_i)); k_i^1=argmin_k d_c(t_i,q_1k)^2, with p_i and g_i computed from training transitions.
OBSERVABLES: Assignment changes should concentrate among high-entropy items; test whether SID-prefix next-item ranking and Stage3 Recall@10 improve, and report assignment/gradient changes relative to baseline.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Set p_i uniform-independent by setting g_i=0 and disable transition-derived m_i; retain curved quantization and baseline target z_i.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Retain training p_i and g_i but replace the Frechet/geodesic operations with weighted Euclidean neighbor mean and linear interpolation and squared Euclidean assignment.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Use training transition probabilities, entropy gate, curved Frechet mean, geodesic interpolation and Poincare assignment with curvature -0.5.
SPEC EQUATIONS: p_i(j) from training counts; H_i=-sum_j p_i(j)log(p_i(j)+eps); g_i=H_i/log(max(2,degree_i)); m_i <- exp^c_{m_i}(eta sum_j p_i(j)log^c_{m_i}(z_j)) for 3 iterations; t_i=exp^c_{z_i}(beta*g_i*log^c_{z_i}(m_i)); quantize t_i by nearest geodesic code then quantize tangent residuals.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs: train-split sequences, frozen item embeddings, current encoder outputs and residual codebooks. Outputs: curved neighborhood targets, residual code assignments/SIDs, and gradients through the target path.
SAFEGUARDS: Float32 map calculations; clamp ball norms below (1-1e-5)/sqrt(c), tangent norms below 3/sqrt(c), normalize probabilities with eps, skip unsupported/degree-one nodes (g=0), use three damped mean steps eta=0.25, and fall back to z_i if a mean becomes nonfinite.
END IDEA
