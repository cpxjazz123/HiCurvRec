IDEA: TRANSITION_TANGENT_FINE
TITLE: Transition-directed tangent residuals for fine-level assignment
MOTIVATION: Items with similar content can have different next-item roles. Fine SID levels should distinguish their observed transition futures without forcing unrelated items into the same coarse cluster.
NOVELTY: Rather than changing first-level assignments or adding a separate transition loss, this uses training transitions to steer the residual presented to each fine quantizer, with that steering performed by a hyperbolic exp map.
CHANGE: For each item, compute a popularity-adjusted mean tangent direction toward its training successors. At each fine RQ level, move the current residual’s hyperbolic representation a confidence-scaled amount along that direction before choosing its code.
SPECIFICATION: Encode item \(i\) as \(z_i\), map to the Poincaré ball \(x_i=\exp_0^c(z_i)\), and form \(u_i=\log_{x_i}^c(\sum_j p(j\mid i)x_j)\), where \(p(j\mid i)\propto n_{ij}/(1+n_j)\) over training transitions. For fine level \(\ell\), map residual \(r_i^\ell\) to \(x_i^\ell\), use \(\tilde x_i^\ell=\exp_{x_i^\ell}^c(\alpha_i u_i)\), assign \(k=\arg\min_q d_c(\tilde x_i^\ell,e_{\ell q})^2\), and subtract the selected code in the existing residual update. Derive \(\alpha_i\) as the ratio of the norm of the unscaled tangent step to the median nonzero training-item tangent norm, clipped at 1.
COST: Precompute transition directions once from training data. Per training step, one tangent-vector combination, exp map and code-distance calculation per fine level; no additional pairwise item comparisons. At the frozen scale (24 steps/epoch × 3000 epochs), this adds those operations for 72,000 steps; expected wall-clock overhead is dominated by the per-item/per-level exp maps and should be measured against baseline.
HYPOTHESIS: The mechanism is worth keeping if training transition direction predicts held-out-within-training successor identity and the fine-level assignments preserve distinctions among items with different successor profiles; judge it by frozen-protocol Stage3 test Recall@10, with 0.065 as the target.
RISKS: Sparse transitions make the direction noisy; popularity adjustment can overcorrect rare successors; tangent transport or residual-coordinate mismatch may steer items inconsistently across levels.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Directed, popularity-adjusted item transition distribution.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: Count consecutive item pairs in the training interaction sequences only. Set \(p(j\mid i)\propto n_{ij}/(1+n_j)\), normalized over observed successors, and use its weighted successor embedding barycenter. Confidence \(\alpha_i\) is zero for items without observed successors.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: Build counts, successor distributions and directions exclusively from the training split; freeze them before Stage2 optimization and SID export. Validation and test interactions are never read by this computation.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Poincaré-ball logarithmic and exponential maps and distance, with fixed negative curvature \(c=-1\).
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: The residual-to-code assignment input at every fine RQ level; the coarse level remains unchanged.
COUPLING PATH: Training transitions determine a successor tangent direction; the exp map applies it to each fine-level residual representation; Poincaré code distances then determine the fine code index and residual update.
COUPLING EQUATION: \(k_i^\ell=\arg\min_q d_{-1}^2(\exp_{x_i^\ell}(\alpha_i u_i),e_{\ell q})\), with \(u_i=\log_{x_i}(\sum_j p(j\mid i)x_j)\). Setting behavior off makes \(\alpha_i=0\); setting geometry off replaces the maps and distance with Euclidean residual addition and distance.
OBSERVABLES: Fine-level code assignments and their gradients should change with the transition direction; report assignment changes, training-only successor predictability from fine prefixes, and Stage3 test Recall@10.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Set \(\alpha_i=0\), retaining the curved fine-level assignment.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Replace log/exp maps and Poincaré distance with Euclidean tangent addition and squared Euclidean code distance, retaining the transition direction.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Use the training-derived direction, exp-map residual steering and Poincaré assignment as specified; train this candidate once only.
SPEC EQUATIONS: \(x_i=\exp_0^{-1}(z_i)\); \(m_i=\sum_j p(j\mid i)x_j\); \(u_i=\log_{x_i}^{-1}(m_i)\); \(x_i^\ell=\exp_0^{-1}(r_i^\ell)\); \(\tilde x_i^\ell=\exp_{x_i^\ell}^{-1}(\alpha_i u_i)\); \(k_i^\ell=\arg\min_q d_{-1}^2(\tilde x_i^\ell,e_{\ell q})\). Use the existing RQ residual update with the selected code. Superscript \(-1\) denotes curvature \(c=-1\), not an inverse map.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs are encoded item vectors, current fine-level residuals/codebooks, and frozen training-only successor probabilities and directions. Outputs are fine-level code indices, quantized vectors and updated residuals.
SAFEGUARDS: Use stable Möbius/Poincaré implementations; clamp ball norms to \(1-\epsilon\), normalize successor weights with an epsilon denominator, replace empty successor distributions by zero direction, and reject non-finite distances. Derive tangent-step scaling from the stated median-norm ratio, not a sweep.
END IDEA

IDEA: TRANSITION_CURVATURE_MARGIN
TITLE: Transition-pair geodesic margins on fine code assignments
MOTIVATION: A fine SID level can erase distinctions between items that lead to different next items, even when those distinctions matter for next-item generation. Train-only transition pairs can signal which residual representations should stay apart in the curved space used by fine quantization.
NOVELTY: This applies a behaviour-conditioned geodesic margin directly to the fine residual representation and its selected code, rather than steering residuals toward successors or reweighting assignments by a successor distribution.
CHANGE: At each fine level, use training transition pairs to impose a popularity-adjusted geodesic separation margin on residuals of items with distinct successor profiles. The separation term acts on the same residual coordinates used for that level’s code assignment.
SPECIFICATION: Build a sparse successor distribution \(p_i(j)\) from training transitions. For item pairs \((i,h)\) sampled from training items, define \(b_{ih}=1-\sum_j\min(p_i(j),p_h(j))\), with \(b_{ih}=0\) if either profile is empty. At each fine level, let \(x_i^\ell=\exp_0^{-1}(r_i^\ell)\). Add \(b_{ih}\max(0,m-d_{-1}(x_i^\ell,x_h^\ell))^2\) to the level’s residual quantization objective, for pairs in the current minibatch. Derive margin \(m\) as the median nonzero pairwise geodesic distance of a fixed, deterministic sample of training residuals at initialization.
COST: Precompute sparse successor profiles. Per step, add pairwise distances for sampled in-batch item pairs at each fine level; cost is quadratic in minibatch size unless pairs are deterministically subsampled, so use one fixed cyclic pairing of the minibatch indices (at most one pair per item). At 24 steps × 3000 epochs, this adds one geodesic distance per item per fine level per step: 72,000 steps, with cost proportional to batch size × number of fine levels.
HYPOTHESIS: This is worth keeping if items with different training successor profiles are less likely to share fine-level residual neighborhoods and the resulting SIDs improve Stage3 test Recall@10 over the frozen baseline. Training-only successor-profile agreement and fine-level geodesic distances are diagnostics, not admission criteria.
RISKS: Different successor profiles need not imply useful recommendation distinctions; sparse profiles may make the overlap signal unreliable; the margin could compete with reconstruction and reduce quantizer fit.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Popularity-adjusted similarity of directed item successor distributions.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: Count adjacent item transitions in training sequences only; normalize each item’s successor counts after dividing by \(1+n_j\). For a pair of items, use one minus the overlap \(\sum_j\min(p_i(j),p_h(j))\); empty profiles give zero weight.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: Successor profiles and pair weights are computed only from training interactions and frozen before Stage2 training. No validation or test sequence is used for profiles, pair selection, margin estimation or SID export.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Poincaré exponential map and geodesic distance at fixed negative curvature \(c=-1\).
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: A pairwise margin term on the residual representations at each fine quantization level, integrated into that level’s quantization objective.
COUPLING PATH: Training successor-profile disagreement weights a geodesic separation penalty on fine residuals; that penalty updates the encoder/codebook path that produces the same fine assignments.
COUPLING EQUATION: \(L_\ell^{pair}=\operatorname{mean}_{(i,h)} b_{ih}\,[\max(0,m-d_{-1}(\exp_0^{-1}(r_i^\ell),\exp_0^{-1}(r_h^\ell)))]^2\). Behavior off sets \(b_{ih}=0\); geometry off replaces the maps and geodesic distance with Euclidean coordinates and distance.
OBSERVABLES: Fine residuals and code assignments should separate more for items with dissimilar training successor profiles; report that distance relation, fine-prefix successor predictability, and Stage3 test Recall@10.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Set \(b_{ih}=0\), leaving the curved residual quantization objective.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Use Euclidean residual vectors and Euclidean pair distances, keeping the same training-derived \(b_{ih}\) and margin procedure.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Add the weighted Poincaré geodesic margin at each fine level. Only the both-on candidate is trained in the round; the off switches are implementation controls, not trained arms.
SPEC EQUATIONS: \(p_i(j)\propto n_{ij}/(1+n_j)\); \(b_{ih}=1-\sum_j\min(p_i(j),p_h(j))\); \(x_i^\ell=\exp_0^{-1}(r_i^\ell)\); \(L_\ell=L_\ell^{RQ}+\operatorname{mean}_{(i,h)}b_{ih}[\max(0,m-d_{-1}(x_i^\ell,x_h^\ell))]^2\). Estimate \(m\) once from the median nonzero pair distance over a fixed deterministic training-item sample at initialization.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs are minibatch fine residuals, frozen training successor profiles, codebooks and initialized residual-distance margin. Outputs are the total quantization objective, gradients and fine-level SID assignments.
SAFEGUARDS: Use deterministic item/pair sampling; clamp Poincaré norms below \(1-\epsilon\), clamp distance arguments away from zero where required, stabilize profile normalization, omit empty-profile pairs, and check losses/gradients for finiteness. Fix the margin from the prescribed initialization statistic; do not tune it.
END IDEA
