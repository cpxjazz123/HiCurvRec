IDEA: geodesic_flow_macro
TITLE: Flow-defined macro-codes with hyperbolic within-macro assignment
MOTIVATION: Many items share a broad next-step role even when their content embeddings differ; forcing the first residual level to preserve content separation can waste its capacity on distinctions not useful for continuation.
NOVELTY: The training-transition graph directly defines a small first-level macro partition, while hyperbolic geometry—not Euclidean nearest-centroid distance—assigns items within each behavior macro. This is not a behavior-weighted modification of the unchanged global quantizer: the candidate set itself is macro-conditioned and its geometry controls assignment.
CHANGE: Build deterministic behavior macros from outgoing-transition profiles, initialize one hyperbolic prototype per macro, and replace first-level quantization by a macro-restricted Poincare assignment; quantize the resulting tangent residual with the ordinary second and third RQ levels so item identity remains available.
SPECIFICATION: Let A_ij be train-only directed transition counts. Define p_i(j)=(A_ij+1)/(sum_j A_ij+M), and h_i=log(p_i) centered by its train-item mean. Spherical k-means on h_i with K1=256 gives macro m_i. For encoded item x_i, map u_i=exp_0^c(Wx_i) in the curvature -c ball, c=1/(mean_i ||Wx_i||^2+epsilon). For each macro k, initialize prototype a_k as the normalized Euclidean mean of its training u vectors, projected to radius (1-epsilon)/sqrt(c). Select q1(i)=argmin_{k=m_i} d_c(u_i,a_k)^2; r_i=log_{a_mi}^c(u_i). Feed r_i through the unchanged level-2 and level-3 residual quantizers, retaining their ordinary commitment and reconstruction terms. Assignment is fixed by behavior macro; the selected prototype and encoder receive gradients through d_c and the straight-through quantized path.
COST: Behavior preprocessing is one sparse pass over training transitions plus spherical k-means, once. Per training step at the frozen scale (24556 items, 24 steps/epoch, 3000 epochs): one O(B*d) exp/log-map and Poincare distance per batch item plus an O(B*K1) macro lookup (constant-time indexed); no all-item pairwise distances or additional epochs. Expected small overhead over baseline quantization; 72,000 steps unchanged.
HYPOTHESIS: Transition-profile macro membership captures continuation role, and curved within-macro distances allocate the first code to geometrically appropriate members instead of preserving fine Euclidean distinctions. This is worth keeping only if the both-on single run exceeds Stage3 test Recall@10 0.065; record macro/code assignments, prefix behavior predictability and Stage3 recall as diagnostics, not gates.
RISKS: Hard macro membership can strand items in a poor cluster; sparse outgoing profiles can be uninformative; fixed macro labels constrain first-level gradients. Curved distances may compress useful variation, while residual levels may fail to reconstruct details removed by the coarse assignment.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Directed next-item transition profile for each training item.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: From training split only, count consecutive item transitions A_ij within each user's time-ordered training events, including only adjacent events; add-one smoothing over the M training-vocabulary destinations and normalize each row. Cluster centered log-profiles into K1=256 spherical k-means macros. Items without outgoing edges receive the smoothed uniform profile.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: Construct A, profile normalizers, clustering, and prototypes only from training interactions. Validation/test events are never consulted; all items are encoded from the frozen item embeddings, and unseen/no-outgoing items use the uniform-profile fallback and deterministic nearest macro centroid based on their smoothed profile.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Poincare-ball exponential/logarithmic maps and squared Poincare distance d_c at fixed negative curvature -c, with c derived from mean squared projected training-vector norm.
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: First-level code assignment and residual construction during Stage2 training and the identical deterministic Stage2 SID export path.
COUPLING PATH: Train-only transition profiles determine macro m_i and thus the admissible first-level prototype; the Poincare distance to that prototype determines code assignment, encoder gradient, and the residual passed to levels 2 and 3. Removing either macro behavior or curved distance changes this same first-level path.
COUPLING EQUATION: m_i=Cluster(h_i); q1(i)=argmin_{k=m_i} d_c(exp_0^c(Wx_i),a_k)^2; r_i=log_{a_mi}^c(exp_0^c(Wx_i)); q2,q3=RQ(r_i). Here h_i is the explicit behavior term and d_c/exp/log are the curved terms.
OBSERVABLES: First-level codes are restricted to behavior-derived macros, behavior predictability of the coarse code should rise, and the hyperbolic assignment changes the selected code/prototype gradients relative to Euclidean distance. Third-level distinct triples and item uniqueness should remain substantial because levels 2-3 encode the within-macro residual. Judge only by Stage3 test Recall@10 > 0.065.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Set macro assignment to unrestricted global first-level assignment (all 256 prototypes admissible); keep Poincare distance, maps, initialization rule, and residual levels. Diagnostic comparison only; do not train this arm.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Replace exp/log maps and d_c with identity and squared Euclidean distance on Wx_i, retaining the exact same behavior macros, admissibility, and residual quantizers. Diagnostic comparison only; do not train this arm.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Use training-transition-derived m_i, macro-restricted Poincare distance and maps, and the same ordinary residual levels; this is the sole candidate training run.
SPEC EQUATIONS: A_ij=sum_u,t 1[(i,j) adjacent in training sequence u]; p_i(j)=(A_ij+1)/(sum_j A_ij+M); h_i=log p_i-mean_train(log p). m_i=spherical_kmeans(h_i,K1=256). c=(mean_train||Wx_i||^2+1e-8)^-1. u_i=exp_0^c(Wx_i); q1=argmin_{k=m_i} d_c(u_i,a_k)^2; z1=a_{q1}; r_i=log_{z1}^c(u_i). Then apply the baseline level-2/3 RQ-VAE losses and straight-through estimator to r_i. Train objective remains baseline objective with first-level distance/commitment term computed as d_c^2; all constants are fixed or measured, no sweep.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs are training-only ordered interactions, all frozen item embeddings, K1=256, and baseline RQ-VAE. Outputs are per-item first-level macro/code, curved residual and full 3-level SID; export must use the same stored macros/prototypes/maps as training.
SAFEGUARDS: Use float32/float64 for transition normalization and curvature calculation; epsilon=1e-6 in row denominators and map formulas; project ball vectors to norm <=(1-1e-5)/sqrt(c); clamp acosh arguments to >=1+1e-7; clip gradients using the baseline rule. Empty profiles use uniform values; empty macro centroids fall back to the nearest nonempty centroid. Do not allow NaN/Inf assignments; use lowest-index tie-breaking.
END IDEA

IDEA: hyperbolic_behavior_tree
TITLE: Transition hierarchy as a hyperbolic first-code tree
MOTIVATION: A useful first SID level should group items by broad behavioral role, but ordinary flat codebooks can assign unrelated continuation contexts together and consume fine-level capacity separating them again.
NOVELTY: Instead of adding graph similarity as a loss or weighting global assignments, training behavior constructs a fixed binary hierarchy and its branch decisions are made by Poincare geodesic comparisons. Later RQ levels are deliberately left to encode item-specific detail; this changes where behavior and geometry meet relative to global Sinkhorn or transition-residual methods.
CHANGE: Build a balanced divisive tree of smoothed train-only transition signatures with 256 leaves. Associate each internal node with two trainable Poincare prototypes; route each item down the tree by geodesic distance, use its leaf as the first SID code, and pass the tangent residual at that leaf to the unchanged second and third codebooks.
SPECIFICATION: Compute p_i as add-one-smoothed outgoing transition distribution from training sequences. At each node, split its assigned items into two by deterministic spherical k-means on centered log p_i; recurse until 256 leaves or singleton sets, then split singleton-heavy leaves by deterministic item-id order to obtain exactly 256 leaves. Encode x_i, map u_i=exp_0^c(Wx_i), and at each visited node n choose child b=argmin_{b in {0,1}} d_c(u_i,a_{n,b})^2. For training stability use soft branch probabilities softmax(-d_c^2/T) with T equal to median sibling squared-distance over the initialization batch; straight-through hard routing selects the leaf. Let z_i be its leaf prototype and r_i=log_{z_i}^c(u_i); standard residual quantization of r_i at levels 2 and 3 preserves distinctions. Tree prototypes update from the existing first-level commitment/reconstruction gradient; no extra behavioral loss is needed.
COST: One sparse graph/profile pass and tree construction once. Per batch item, 8 binary node comparisons to reach 256 leaves: O(8*d) Poincare operations plus soft branch probabilities; at frozen scale 24 steps/epoch x 3000 epochs = 72,000 steps, routing adds approximately eight distance evaluations per item per step. Residual levels and training schedule are unchanged; export repeats the same routing.
HYPOTHESIS: A behavior-derived hierarchy makes coarse prefixes predictive of next-item context, while geodesic branching lets nearby semantic vectors choose the appropriate branch without globally collapsing all items; third-level triples should remain informative from residual detail. Keep only if sole-run Stage3 test Recall@10 exceeds 0.065; prefix behavior predictability and SID statistics are recorded only as diagnostics.
RISKS: Tree errors near upper nodes are irreversible; low transition support can make branch labels noisy; a balanced tree need not align with embedding geometry. The deterministic singleton fallback may produce arbitrary branches. Soft routing temperature derived from initialization can still be poorly calibrated.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Per-item outgoing transition distribution and its recursively clustered binary branch labels.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: From training split only, count ordered adjacent item pairs per user, smooth each row by one count per destination, normalize, center log probabilities, and recursively bisect node members using deterministic spherical two-means. No validation/test interactions enter tree structure or labels.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: Tree, profiles, and prototypes are constructed from training sequences and frozen embeddings only. Validation/test transitions are not read for training, map fitting, branch thresholds, or SID export. For items with no training outgoing events, use uniform profile and deterministic tree traversal from that profile; Stage3 evaluation only consumes exported IDs.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Poincare exponential/logarithmic maps, fixed negative curvature -c measured from training projected-vector scale, and geodesic distance d_c for binary branch selection.
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: Repeated first-level tree routing and tangent residual formation in Stage2 forward pass and SID export.
COUPLING PATH: Training transition hierarchy defines the ordered internal branch prototypes and allowable route; at every branch, Poincare geodesic distance selects which behavior-defined child receives the item, with that route determining the first code and the residual seen by later quantizers.
COUPLING EQUATION: n_0=root; n_{t+1}=child(n_t,argmin_b d_c(exp_0^c(Wx_i),a_{n_t,b})^2), where tree children are derived from transition-profile behavior h_i; q1=leaf(n_8), r_i=log_{a_q1}^c(exp_0^c(Wx_i)), q2,q3=RQ(r_i). The behavior term is h_i/tree(h); the curved term is d_c and exp/log maps.
OBSERVABLES: Behavior-defined branch paths should make q1 more predictable from training transition context; curved branch decisions should change routing versus Euclidean comparisons; q2/q3 retain within-leaf detail, so distinct third-level triples should not collapse. Stage3 test Recall@10 > 0.065 is the sole judgement.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Replace the behavior-derived tree with a balanced deterministic tree over item IDs, leaving Poincare routing and all codebook sizes unchanged. This comparison exists in implementation but is not trained.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Replace Poincare maps and geodesic distance by identity and squared Euclidean distance, keeping the training-derived tree and residual levels unchanged. This comparison exists in implementation but is not trained.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Train once with train-transition-derived tree and Poincare geodesic routing; no off-switch arm is run.
SPEC EQUATIONS: Build A,p,h as in the preceding definition. Recursively partition h into two child groups until 256 leaves; persist tree and node IDs. Let c=(mean_train ||Wx||^2+1e-8)^-1, u=exp_0^c(Wx), branch logits s_b=-d_c(u,a_{n,b})^2/T_n, where T_n is the median sibling squared-distance measured once on training initialization vectors (floor 1e-6). Hard route by argmax s_b using straight-through softmax; follow exactly 8 levels. Set q1 to leaf ID and r=log^c_{a_q1}(u); quantize r with baseline codebooks at levels 2 and 3. Full loss is baseline reconstruction/commitment loss with first-level curved commitment distance; no additional loss coefficient.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Training ordered interactions, frozen item embeddings, baseline model dimensions and optimizer. Returns persisted behavior tree, trainable branch prototypes, first-level leaf code and 2-level residual SID suffix per item.
SAFEGUARDS: Use deterministic clustering/ties and persist tree; avoid empty-child nodes by deterministic median split on item ID within a behavior-tied node. Clamp probabilities, Poincare norms and acosh arguments as specified in IDEA geodesic_flow_macro; floor T_n at 1e-6; project prototypes inside the ball after updates; clip gradient using baseline rule. Use uniform profiles for zero-outdegree items and finite fallback route.
END IDEA
