IDEA: branch-tree-poincare-leaf
TITLE: Branch-derived coarse tree with Poincaré leaf assignment
MOTIVATION: First-level codes can be too discriminative for next-item prefix prediction; user interest branches provide a training-only way to make level 1 a useful coarse prefix while subsequent residual levels retain item identity.
NOVELTY: Unlike behaviour-profile-weighted assignment, behaviour builds a fixed coarse partition of the first-level codebook before optimization; hyperbolic geometry then assigns items only among the children of their behaviour-derived parent. It does not score co-consumption profiles against each code.
CHANGE: From training sequences, recursively split first-level code slots into K0/M parent groups using user-level successor-branch vectors; initialize each parent’s child prototypes from its assigned item embeddings. For each item, constrain the first-level choice to children of its behaviour-assigned parent and choose by fixed-curvature Poincaré distance; train levels 2–3 as the ordinary Euclidean residual quantizer.
SPECIFICATION: Build item branch vector b_i[a]=log(1+n_train(i→a)) over successor item clusters a (clusters made by deterministic spherical k-means on training-item input embeddings; cluster count M=min(64,ceil(sqrt(N)))). Set each user’s branch vector to the normalized sum of successor-cluster counts in that user’s training sequence. For each item, average the normalized branch vectors of training users who consumed it, yielding b_i (zero if none). Deterministically partition items into K0 parent groups by balanced k-means on b_i, with K0 the configured first-level codebook size divided by M, rounded down and at least 1; partition each parent’s K0/M child slots. At each quantizer update, map encoder output z_i to Poincaré ball via exp_0(s z_i), where s=1/median_i ||z_i|| (clamped finite); assign c1(i)=argmin_{k in children(parent(i))} d_c(exp_0(s z_i), exp_0(s e_k))². Output c1 then run the baseline residual quantizers for levels 2 and 3. Parent membership is frozen from training-only inputs; child prototypes e_k are trainable.
COST: One-time sparse successor-vector construction and balanced clustering O(E_train + N·M·d) once; per step adds O(B·(K0/M)·d) Poincaré distances, with B≈24556/24≈1024 items per step, plus negligible parent lookup. Across 24 steps/epoch × 3000 epochs: 72,000 steps and about 73.7M item-to-child distance evaluations (times K0/M), on top of baseline. This is a single run, no ablation arms or sweeps.
HYPOTHESIS: Users’ training successor branches induce genuinely reusable coarse item groups, and curved assignment within each group stabilizes first-token choices without collapsing residual identity. Observe changed first-level SID distribution/assignments, higher training-only successor-branch predictability from c1, retained distinct third-level triples, and ultimately Stage3 test Recall@10; retain only if Recall@10 exceeds 0.065.
RISKS: Popular items/users with sparse histories may have noisy branch vectors; hard parent constraints can force poor reconstructions; balanced clustering may not align with semantic embeddings; curvature may add no benefit over Euclidean distance. Residual levels may fail to recover identity if too many items share a parent.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Training-only item-to-successor-branch histogram, averaged over training users who consumed each item.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: Use only the Stage2 training interaction split. Form successor clusters from training-item input embeddings; count adjacent directed transitions within each user’s training sequence; aggregate normalized successor-cluster counts by consumed item. No validation/test transitions, users, or interaction counts are read.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: The branch vectors, item partitions, and parent-child map are materialized exclusively from the training split and frozen before SID export. Validation/test are used only by the frozen Stage3 protocol after SID construction.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Poincaré exponential map and squared geodesic distance with fixed negative curvature −c, c=1 (unit-curvature ball).
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: First-level RQ-VAE quantization; both the projected item latent and trainable child code vectors are compared in the Poincaré ball. Levels 2 and 3 retain baseline residual quantization.
COUPLING PATH: Training-only branch histogram determines item parent and hence the candidate child set; Poincaré distance selects a child within that set. Removing behaviour removes the parent constraint; replacing Poincaré distance removes the curved within-parent assignment, changing c1 and its residual input.
COUPLING EQUATION: parent(i)=g(b_i); c1(i)=argmin_{k∈children(parent(i))} d_{−1}(exp_0(s z_i), exp_0(s e_k))²; r1(i)=z_i−e_{c1(i)}.
OBSERVABLES: First-level assignments and their gradients change; measure SID assignment churn against the baseline, training-only successor-branch predictability from c1, distinct level-3 triples, and Stage3 test Recall@10. The behavioural diagnostics are recorded, not admission gates.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Mechanism switch sets parent(i) to the unrestricted all-child set for every item; retain Poincaré assignment. This is a specification/control only, not a trained arm.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Keep the same training-derived parents and children, but replace exp/log maps and Poincaré distance with squared Euclidean distance on z_i,e_k. This is a specification/control only, not a trained arm.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Use the frozen training-derived parent map and Poincaré distance inside its child set. This is the sole candidate trained once under the frozen budget.
SPEC EQUATIONS: b_i=normalize(Σ_{u∈trainers(i)} normalize(h_u)); h_u[a]=Σ_{t adjacent in train user u} 1[cluster(i_{t+1})=a]; p_i=g(b_i); x_i=exp_0(s z_i); c1(i)=argmin_{k∈C(p_i)} d_{−1}(x_i,exp_0(s e_k))²; r1=z_i−e_{c1}; (c2,c3)=baseline_RQ(r1). Zero history maps to the most-popular training-derived parent with ties by smallest index. g is deterministic balanced k-means on b_i and emits K0 parents; no hyperparameter is selected by outcome.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs are training-split sequences, training item embeddings, Stage2 encoder latents, configured first-level codebook size K0, trainable first-level prototypes, and baseline residual quantizers. Outputs are first-level code c1, residual latent r1, then baseline c2,c3 and exported three-level SID.
SAFEGUARDS: Normalize with epsilon 1e−12; exclude zero-count transitions; use FP32 distance accumulation; project ball points to norm ≤(1−1e−5)/sqrt(c); clamp atanh argument to 1−1e−6; set s from the training-latent median norm with epsilon floor; deterministic clustering/tie-breaking; empty-history fallback uses training popularity only. Do not allow a parent with no training items to receive a child prototype update.
END IDEA

IDEA: flow-mass-lorentz-quantizer
TITLE: Transition-flow hierarchy with Lorentzian coarse assignment
MOTIVATION: A useful first SID token should summarize where training-only user traffic flows, rather than isolate an item by reconstruction alone; keeping later residual tokens free lets the complete SID remain discriminative.
NOVELTY: Behaviour determines codebook topology through a directed flow partition, not an additive behavioural loss or a per-item behaviour-weighted distance. Hyperbolic geometry acts as the assignment metric within flow-defined coarse cells, a different position and signal use from profile-based region scoring.
CHANGE: Construct a directed item transition graph and partition its item nodes into exactly K0 first-level cells by minimizing cut flow while keeping each cell nonempty. Initialize one first-level prototype per cell and constrain each item’s curved quantization to its graph cell; leave the second and third residual codebooks and assignments at baseline.
SPECIFICATION: From training-only adjacent item pairs, compute directed counts n_ij, then edge weights w_ij=n_ij/sqrt((out_i+1)(in_j+1)) (popularity-adjusted directed transition mass). Deterministically run a balanced directed graph partitioner to assign every item to one of K0 cells; K0 is the existing first-level codebook size, not tuned. Initialize prototype e_k as the mean encoder input latent of cell k. Each update assigns item i to its cell’s sole first-level prototype by Lorentz distance, updates that prototype by its cell’s mean latent with the baseline optimizer, computes Euclidean residual z_i−e_{cell(i)}, and performs ordinary levels 2 and 3. Thus coarse IDs are exactly the flow cells; do not merge cells or restrict later codebooks.
COST: One-time graph construction O(E_train) and graph partition O((N+E_train)log N) approximately, plus initial cell means. Per step adds O(B·d) Lorentz distances/projections for B≈1024 items, so 24 steps/epoch × 3000 epochs =72,000 steps and ≈73.7M item/prototype distances, negligible against the baseline encoder and level-2/3 quantization; graph partition runs once.
HYPOTHESIS: Flow-cut cells collect items exchanged as next-items by the same training traffic, creating useful coarser first tokens, while unrestricted residual levels preserve item identity. Observe changed c1 assignments and first-prefix successor predictability, retain distinct level-3 triples, and judge solely by Stage3 test Recall@10; the target is >0.065.
RISKS: A hard flow partition can group items that are sequentially connected but semantically unlike; directed counts can be sparse, the partition can be dominated by hubs, and a single prototype per cell may underfit. If too many items share first token and residual levels cannot distinguish them, Recall falls; Lorentz geometry may offer no benefit versus Euclidean.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Popularity-adjusted directed adjacent-item transition mass on the training interaction graph.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: For each user’s training sequence only, count consecutive item pairs i→j; compute out_i and in_j over those same training transitions and weight each observed edge by n_ij/sqrt((out_i+1)(in_j+1)). Partition all training catalog items, including isolated items as singleton/least-loaded placements, into K0 nonempty cells. No validation or test event is used.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: The graph and cell assignment are built once from training transitions only; validation/test interactions never contribute to graph weights, partitions, codebook training, or SID export. Stage3 validation/test are consumed only by their frozen evaluation protocol.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Lorentz-model squared geodesic distance at fixed curvature −1, using hyperboloid lift L(x)=((1+||x||²)/(1−||x||²), 2x/(1−||x||²)) for ball-coordinate x; d=acosh(max(1,−⟨L(x),L(y)⟩_L)).
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: First-level code assignment and prototype update in the RQ-VAE; flow cell fixes the candidate prototype and the Lorentz distance supplies its actual quantization/reconstruction error and gradient. Later residual quantization remains baseline Euclidean.
COUPLING PATH: The train-only transition graph assigns each item to cell k; its cell selects prototype e_k; Lorentz geodesic error between the lifted item latent and lifted e_k drives the first-level quantization gradient, which changes residual input and later SIDs. Removing behaviour replaces cells with unconstrained nearest-code assignment; replacing geometry changes the cell’s curved quantization error and prototype gradient.
COUPLING EQUATION: k(i)=FlowPartition(W_train); L_i=L(exp_0(z_i)), L_k=L(exp_0(e_k)); c1(i)=k(i), L_q=Σ_i d_{−1}(L_i,L_k)²; r1(i)=z_i−e_k.
OBSERVABLES: The fixed flow partition changes first-token item memberships; curved loss changes first-code prototype gradients/updates. Record assignment-vs-baseline, training-only directed successor predictability by c1, third-level distinct triples, codebook gradient norms, and Stage3 test Recall@10. Diagnostics do not gate admission.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Ignore the graph partition and choose the nearest among all K0 prototypes by Lorentz distance (preserving prototype count and optimizer); specification/control only, not trained.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Preserve the exact graph-derived cells and memberships, but replace Lorentz distance/lift with squared Euclidean distance; specification/control only, not trained.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Use training-only flow-derived cells and Lorentz quantization error/prototype gradients; this is the single trained candidate.
SPEC EQUATIONS: n_ij=Σ_{u,t}1[i_{u,t}=i ∧ i_{u,t+1}=j] over training sequences; w_ij=n_ij/√((out_i+1)(in_j+1)); k=DirectedBalancedPartition(W,K0); x_i=clipball(z_i), x_k=clipball(e_k); q_i=acosh(max(1,−<L(x_i),L(x_k)>_L)); L1=Σ_i q_i²; r_i=z_i−e_{k(i)}; (c2,c3)=baseline_RQ(r_i). The partition minimizes directed cut Σ_{ij} w_ij 1[k_i≠k_j] subject to all K0 cells nonempty, deterministic tie-breaking; do not tune the balance constraint or other constants.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs are training sequences, training item embeddings, encoder latents z_i, configured K0 first-level prototypes/codebook size, and baseline level-2/3 quantizers. Outputs are graph cell map k(i), first SID c1=k(i), curved first-level loss/updates, residual and full three-level SIDs.
SAFEGUARDS: Add 1 only in graph degree denominators; omit unobserved edges; clip ball norm to (1−1e−5), compute Lorentz products/acosh in FP32 and clamp argument to at least 1; reject nonfinite edge weights and prototype updates; initialize empty-code edge cases through deterministic balanced assignment of isolated items using training-only item index. Keep all cells nonempty; apply no post-hoc SID merging. The graph-derived c1 is discrete and fixed; gradients flow through Lorentz distance/prototype updates, not through the partitioner.
END IDEA
