IDEA: transition-anchored-hyperbolic-codebook
TITLE: Allocate first-level codewords by directed transition neighborhoods in hyperbolic space
MOTIVATION: Ordinary itemwise reconstruction can spend first-level codewords separating visually or textually distinct items even when those items are interchangeable as next-item contexts; Stage 2 does not rank candidates, so prefixes should group items whose training-only successor neighborhoods overlap.
NOVELTY: This makes directed transition-neighborhood similarity directly alter the first-level discrete assignment through a hyperbolic pairwise codeword cost. It is not a behavior suffix on fixed assignments, a shell-calibrated item-to-code distance, a Busemann gate, or a tangent-space warp.
CHANGE: Precompute sparse, popularity-adjusted directed transition neighbors from training interactions, then add their expected hyperbolic codeword separation to each item's first-level assignment cost; alternate assignments and codebook updates with the usual RQ-VAE optimization. This changes which items share the first SID token, rather than only how an unchanged assignment measures reconstruction.
SPECIFICATION: Let the training-only directed count be C_ij and item popularity n_j. Keep each i's top M successors by a_ij = log(1+C_ij)/(log(2+n_j)); normalize a_ij over its retained successors to q_ij. With first-level encoder point z_i and codebook points c_k in the Poincare ball of curvature -κ, assign a_i = argmin_k [d_κ(z_i,c_k)^2 + λ Σ_{j in N(i)} q_ij d_κ(c_k,c_{a_j})^2], using the previous-iteration assignments a_j for neighbor targets. Update encoder/codebook using straight-through hard assignments and the same summed objective (stop-gradient through a_j within the minibatch/epoch); rebuild assignments synchronously once per epoch to avoid order dependence. Continue ordinary residual quantization at later levels.
COST: Store at most M sparse outgoing edges per training item. The extra term is O(E_M) per assignment refresh and O(BM) per minibatch update, where E_M ≤ 24556M; it adds one hyperbolic center-to-center distance per retained edge and does not require all item pairs or a dense Sinkhorn matrix. At the frozen scale (24 steps/epoch × 3000 epochs), there are 1,768,032,000 item-step opportunities, with edge work proportional to M times the items processed; assignment-refresh cost is paid 3000 times. For M=8, refreshes evaluate at most 196,448 edge terms each (589,344,000 across 3000 refreshes), plus minibatch edge terms; benchmark wall time against baseline because GPU batching and refresh implementation dominate.
HYPOTHESIS: If items with shared directed successors are useful next-item alternatives, this cost will make first-level prefixes more predictive of successors and increase Stage3 test Recall@10, ideally beyond 0.065; observe changed first-token assignments and held-out Stage3 Recall@10, not SID quality alone. Keep only if the both-on run improves Recall over the frozen 0.063354 baseline and the specified ablations show both factors matter.
RISKS: Popularity correction may over-amplify noisy rare edges; the previous-assignment target can reinforce early mistakes or collapse neighboring items into a few codes; λ may overwhelm reconstruction; transition regularization may optimize training graph structure that does not transfer to test-time next-item behavior. The sparse edge term may add substantial runtime if assignment refreshes are not batched.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Directed item-to-next-item transition counts, discounted by destination popularity.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: From training interactions only, sort each user's training events by timestamp (or the dataset's documented sequence order), count adjacent directed pairs C_ij, compute destination counts n_j, retain top M outgoing neighbors under log(1+C_ij)/log(2+n_j), and row-normalize to q_ij. Do not construct edges across users or infer edges from validation/test sequences.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: The graph, popularity counts, neighbor list, code assignments, and codebooks are constructed using the training split only. Freeze the resulting item-SID map before Stage 3 validation/test evaluation; no validation or test interactions, metrics, or derived transitions enter graph construction, refreshes, or training.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Squared Poincare geodesic distance d_κ(x,y)^2 on the ball of radius 1/√κ, with fixed κ>0 (initialize κ=1; report it explicitly).
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: First-level discrete assignment objective and its encoder/codebook gradients during RQ-VAE training; it acts on codeword-to-codeword transition-neighborhood cost, not merely item reconstruction distance.
COUPLING PATH: Training-only directed edges select successor assignments a_j; each item's candidate first-level codeword is then penalized according to the Poincare geodesic distances from that candidate codeword to those successor codewords. Removing the graph removes the neighbor-weighted term; replacing Poincare distance with Euclidean distance changes the actual grouping objective.
COUPLING EQUATION: J_i(k)=d_κ(z_i,c_k)^2 + λ Σ_{j∈N_train(i)} q_ij d_κ(c_k,c_{a_j})^2; a_i=argmin_k J_i(k), where q_ij is the popularity-adjusted behavior weight and d_κ is the curved Poincare distance.
OBSERVABLES: First-token assignment changes concentrated among items with overlapping training successor neighborhoods; training gradient/codebook updates include the sparse edge term; measure next-item predictability from SID prefixes and Stage3 validation/test Recall@10 under the existing evaluation protocol. The target is test Recall@10 > 0.065.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Set λ=0 (or q_ij=0), retaining the same Poincare RQ-VAE, training schedule, seeds, and Stage3 setup; this tests whether behavior edges contribute.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Keep the identical training-only graph, q_ij, λ, and optimization, but replace every d_κ(x,y)^2 in the coupled objective with squared Euclidean distance; this tests whether curvature contributes beyond behavior regularization.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Use training-only q_ij and the Poincare distance in J_i(k), train SIDs, freeze them, then evaluate Stage3 Recall@10; compare with behavior-off and geometry-off runs and the frozen 0.063354 baseline.
SPEC EQUATIONS: For each training user sequence, count adjacent transitions C_ij; define s_ij=log(1+C_ij)/log(2+n_j), N(i)=topM_j(s_ij), q_ij=s_ij/(Σ_{l∈N(i)}s_il+ε). At each epoch, with assignments a_j fixed from the prior refresh, compute J_i(k)=d_κ(z_i,c_k)^2+λΣ_{j∈N(i)}q_ij d_κ(c_k,c_{a_j})^2; choose argmin_k, update encoder/codebook via straight-through gradients on selected assignments and J_i(a_i), refresh assignments synchronously, then proceed to subsequent residual codebooks as in baseline. Use mini-batched edge lists; no dense N×N tensor.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs: training-only timestamp/order sequences, item IDs, encoder vectors z_i, first-level Poincare codebook {c_k}, κ, λ, and M. Output: first-level assignment a_i for every training item, updated encoder/codebook parameters, and ultimately frozen RQ-VAE SIDs for all catalog items. For catalog items absent from training edges, use N(i)=∅ and the standard reconstruction assignment.
SAFEGUARDS: Project all ball points to norm ≤(1-δ)/√κ (δ=1e-5); compute arcosh/log arguments with clamp ≥1+ε; use fp32 for distance and edge-weight accumulation; clamp denominators with ε=1e-8; normalize q over retained positive edges only; cap M and λ, clip gradients, and log the edge-term/reconstruction-term ratio and code usage to detect collapse. Resolve assignment ties deterministically. For items without outgoing training transitions, exactly recover the baseline first-level objective.
END IDEA
