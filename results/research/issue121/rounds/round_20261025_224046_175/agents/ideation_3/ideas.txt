IDEA: cohort_anchor_ball
TITLE: Successor-cohort anchors for a low-branching hyperbolic first token
MOTIVATION: The beam loses targets when the first token branches widely, but a purely content-driven first token can group items that are not useful next-step alternatives. Put items predicted by the same training-only predecessor cohorts in a small number of behaviorally meaningful first-level regions, and leave fine discrimination to later tokens.
NOVELTY: This changes the first-level codeword locations through cohort-derived Poincare anchors, rather than adding a behavior loss beside an unchanged quantizer. It is not the existing Euclidean behavior-conditioned quantization/ranking mechanism: behavior prototypes and item codewords are jointly compared by curved distance, with an explicit 32-way capacity and residual levels.
CHANGE: Build a smoothed successor-cohort vector per item from train transitions, map it to a fixed-dimensional tangent feature, and learn 32 cohort anchor points on a Poincare ball. The first token is assigned by a mixed content-to-codeword and cohort-to-anchor geodesic cost; subsequent three residual tokens reconstruct content, with T4 used only for deterministic collision extension.
SPECIFICATION: Let p(j|i)=(N_train(i,j)+alpha*pi(j))/(sum_k N_train(i,k)+alpha), where pi is the training item frequency distribution. Define z_i = normalize(log(1+sum_{h in top-M predecessors of i} p(i|h))) in R^d (zeros for no predecessors). Map cohort feature u_i=exp_0^c(A z_i) to the ball. Map encoder output x_i to y_i=exp_0^c(W x_i). For 32 first codewords q_k and trainable anchors a_k inside ball, assign k*=argmin_k [(1-lambda)d_c(y_i,q_k)^2 + lambda*d_c(u_i,a_k)^2 + rho*log(1+n_k)], where n_k is a stop-gradient EMA occupancy penalty; choose lambda=.35, rho calibrated once from train-only cost scales. Set r_i=log_{q_k*}(y_i), and quantize r_i with the existing Euclidean L2/L3 residual codebooks; reserve T4 as the deterministic collision-extension token. Train reconstruction plus the usual codebook/commitment terms through straight-through assignment; the anchor term's gradient updates A, anchors, and first codewords.
COST: Precompute sparse transitions and cohort vectors once; negligible storage O(E_train+24556*d). Each assignment adds 32 Poincare distance evaluations and one cohort feature lookup versus the 256-way baseline first-stage search, so the first-stage search is cheaper; expected epoch wall time within baseline or slightly lower. At frozen scale: 24 steps/epoch, about 1,024 items per step; each step adds about 32,768 vector-distance evaluations (O(32*B*d)), or 0.098 billion scalar-coordinate distance work per epoch at d=64; 3,000 epochs do not change the frozen schedule.
HYPOTHESIS: Successor-cohort similarity carries next-item predictability, and 32 curved anchors can place it in prefixes that remain beam-cheap without sacrificing later content detail. Keep only if the full coupled arm beats both controls on frozen Stage3 test_R@10, ideally >0.065, with prefix survival improved rather than merely lower SID entropy.
RISKS: Sparse/ambiguous transition profiles may group unrelated items; smoothing can make popularity dominate; 32 anchors may be too few; content/cohort cost scaling may overwhelm one signal; manifold optimization or residual definition may destabilize training.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Directed next-item transition distribution, with smoothed predecessor-cohort profiles.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: From the Stage2/training interaction split only, count consecutive user item pairs (i,j), producing N_train(i,j); aggregate incoming conditional distributions p(i|h) into z_i. Do not construct pairs across split boundaries.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: The matrix and item frequencies are computed solely from training interactions. Validation/test transitions are never loaded by SID code; held-out users/items with no train profile receive the zero profile and are quantized from content only.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Poincare-ball exponential map and squared Poincare distance d_c, fixed c=1 (negative sectional curvature -1).
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: First-level codeword assignment and joint anchor/codeword training; later residual levels are Euclidean in the tangent space.
COUPLING PATH: Training transition counts produce u_i; u_i and the content embedding y_i jointly determine the actual first-token argmin via a sum of curved distances; its selected codeword then determines all downstream residuals.
COUPLING EQUATION: k*(i)=argmin_{k in 1..32}{(1-lambda)d_c(y_i,q_k)^2 + lambda*d_c(exp_0^c(A z_i),a_k)^2 + rho log(1+n_k)}.
OBSERVABLES: On canonical 52,855 pairs with ten fixed negatives, predict L1 Hard ER to fall from the unconstrained high-branching regime (capacity bounds L1 support to at most 32; report exact ER under the fixed protocol). Predict gold-prefix survival depth 1 to rise and depth 2/3 survival to rise if cohorts are useful; depth 4 should be approximately unchanged because T4 is collision extension. T4 NLL should remain near constant. Stage3 test_R@10 should increase and exceed 0.065 to justify retention; report all metrics under frozen protocol.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Set lambda=0 and remove z_i/u_i/anchors from assignment; preserve 32 first codewords, hyperbolic content distance, residuals, epochs, and seeds. This tests whether train transitions add value.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Replace exp/log/Poincare distance with Euclidean coordinates and squared Euclidean distances for both content and cohort terms; preserve the behavior profiles, 32-way capacity, and schedule.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Use train-only cohort features and the c=1 Poincare mixed-distance assignment above, with 32 first groups, residual L2/L3, and deterministic T4 extension.
SPEC EQUATIONS: For each training item i, compute y_i=exp_0^1(Wx_i), u_i=exp_0^1(Az_i). Assign k by argmin_k (0.65 d_1(y_i,q_k)^2+0.35 d_1(u_i,a_k)^2+rho log(1+n_k)); r_i=log_{q_k}(y_i). Quantize r_i at L2 and residual at L3, then apply deterministic collision extension at T4. Optimize reconstruction+commitment/codebook losses with straight-through gradients; update n_k by EMA. Keep occupancy penalty small and fixed before final run.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs are train-only transition counts, item embeddings x_i, learned W/A, 32 ball codewords q_k, 32 ball anchors a_k, and curvature c=1. Outputs are four-token SID and scalar training objective; transitions for unseen items map to z_i=0.
SAFEGUARDS: Project ball points to norm <=(1-epsilon)/sqrt(c), epsilon=1e-5; clamp acosh/log arguments and distances; normalize/smooth counts with alpha>0; use float32 or float64 for distance accumulation; clip gradients; detach EMA occupancies; deterministic tie-breaking and collision extension; cap top-M incoming neighbors and use sparse storage.
END IDEA

IDEA: cohort_curvature_gate
TITLE: Transition-conditioned curvature gates the first-token geodesic ranking
MOTIVATION: Popular and long-tail items can have very different transition uncertainty. A fixed-curvature partition may spend first-token capacity on unstable branching for one cohort while under-separating predictable successors in another, causing beam attrition before later SID tokens can help.
NOVELTY: Behavior controls the curvature used in the first-stage distance itself, not a behavior-only auxiliary objective or a changed codebook size alone. Unlike cohort-anchor placement, this leaves a shared learned first-level codebook and changes the local geometry/ranking per item via its train-transition uncertainty.
CHANGE: Estimate each item’s transition entropy and confidence from train-only outgoing transitions; map these to a bounded negative curvature c_i and use the resulting item-specific Poincare geodesic distance to rank a fixed 64 first-level codewords. L2/L3 absorb remaining content residuals and T4 stays a near-constant deterministic collision token.
SPECIFICATION: Let counts N_ij be consecutive train transitions and p_ij=(N_ij+alpha*pi(j))/(sum_l N_il+alpha). Compute H_i=-sum_j p_ij log p_ij and confidence g_i=n_i/(n_i+tau), n_i=sum_j N_ij. Set c_i=clip(c_min+(c_max-c_min)*g_i*sigmoid(b0+b1*(H_i-h0)), c_min,c_max), with c_min=.25,c_max=2 (curvature -c_i); fix h0 to median train entropy. Map encoder output v_i=exp_0^{c_i}(W x_i), codeword q_k=exp_0^{c0}(e_k) with shared tangent prototypes e_k and c0=1, then compare after mapping both tangent coordinates into the same c_i ball: q_{ik}=exp_0^{c_i}(e_k). Assign k*=argmin_{k<=64} d_{c_i}(v_i,q_{ik})^2. The curvature gate is not a detached score multiplier: it changes the exponential map and distance, hence assignment and reconstruction gradients. Use ordinary tangent residuals for L2/L3.
COST: Precompute entropy/confidence once in O(E_train); each batch computes 64 Poincare distances and c_i map factors. Versus 256-way Euclidean L1, first-stage candidate count is one quarter, with a modest curved-distance overhead; estimate net epoch time no more than baseline, validate wall time. Frozen scale is 24 steps/epoch, B≈1,024: 65,536 distances/step, about 0.20 billion coordinate-distance operations/epoch at d=64, repeated for 3,000 epochs.
HYPOTHESIS: Transition uncertainty/confidence predicts which items need a tighter or more expansive curved neighborhood; curvature-dependent ranking will make first-token alternatives more predictable at beam width 20 while preserving enough discrimination in T2/T3. Keep if full coupling beats behavior-off and Euclidean controls in Stage3 test_R@10, targeting >0.065.
RISKS: Entropy estimates for sparse items are noisy; item-varying curvature can distort comparable distances or create inconsistent partitions; curvature may correlate with popularity without adding useful signal; 64 groups may still be too many for beam survival; optimization can exploit curvature instead of representing content.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Per-item outgoing next-item transition entropy and transition count (confidence).
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: Count consecutive item pairs per user in the training split only; compute smoothed outgoing p(j|i), entropy H_i and count n_i; no validation/test sequences.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: Entropy, confidence and smoothing prior pi are computed from training interactions only and frozen before SID training. Missing train transitions use n_i=0 and c_i=c_min; no validation/test event can affect curvature or assignments.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Poincare exponential map and geodesic distance with item-dependent c_i in [0.25,2], corresponding to negative curvature -c_i.
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: First-level soft/hard quantizer ranking and its straight-through training gradients; no post-hoc SID rewrite.
COUPLING PATH: Train transition entropy/confidence sets c_i; c_i determines the mapping of both item embedding and every candidate codeword into the ball and their geodesic distances; those distances select the first token, which conditions residual quantization.
COUPLING EQUATION: k*(i)=argmin_k d_{c_i}(exp_0^{c_i}(Wx_i), exp_0^{c_i}(e_k))^2, c_i=clip(c_min+(c_max-c_min)g_i sigmoid(b0+b1(H_i-h0)),c_min,c_max); both H_i and the curved operator are necessary for this assignment.
OBSERVABLES: On the canonical 52,855 fixed-negative pairs, L1 Hard ER should fall/hold within the 64-group capacity rather than approach 1; gold-prefix survival should improve at depth 1 and remain higher through depth 3, with depth 4 roughly flat. T4 NLL should stay near-constant. The decisive observable is frozen Stage3 test_R@10 increasing beyond 0.065; also inspect survival stratified by train transition confidence to confirm the gate acts as hypothesized.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Fix c_i=1 for every item while retaining Poincare distance, 64 codewords, and all training settings. This removes the train behavior’s influence on the curved ranking.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Replace the mappings and Poincare distance with Euclidean squared distance ||Wx_i-e_k||^2; retain the same transition-derived c_i computation only as a diagnostic logged covariate, not as a multiplier, to isolate geometric contribution. Include a matched Euclidean 64-way model whose assignment is independent of c_i.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Use train-only H_i,n_i to compute c_i and the item-specific Poincare maps/distance in first-token assignment, 64 groups, unchanged residual levels and fixed 3,000 epochs.
SPEC EQUATIONS: Compute p_ij=(N_ij+alpha*pi(j))/(n_i+alpha), H_i=-sum_j p_ij log p_ij, g_i=n_i/(n_i+tau), c_i=clip(.25+1.75*g_i*sigmoid(b0+b1(H_i-h0)),.25,2). Let v_i=exp_0^{c_i}(Wx_i), q_ik=exp_0^{c_i}(e_k). Set k=argmin_{1..64} d_{c_i}(v_i,q_ik)^2 and r_i=log_{q_ik}^{c_i}(v_i); quantize r_i then residual at L2/L3, T4 collision extension. Optimize standard reconstruction and commitment losses via straight-through hard assignment.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs: train-only N_ij and pi, embeddings x_i, 64 tangent prototypes e_k, bounded curvature gate parameters b0/b1 and h0, and L2/L3 codebooks. Outputs: four-token SID, per-item curvature diagnostic, and training objective.
SAFEGUARDS: Bound c_i in [.25,2], clamp ball norms to (1-epsilon)/sqrt(c_i), epsilon=1e-5; use stable exp/log maps with small-norm series; clamp acosh arguments to >=1+1e-7 and distance squares; smooth counts, define zero-count fallback; constrain b1 or regularize c_i toward c0=1; use float32+stable accumulation, gradient clipping, deterministic ties and collision extension.
END IDEA
