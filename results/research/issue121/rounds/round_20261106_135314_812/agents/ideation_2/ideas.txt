IDEA: curvature-depth-residual-sinkhorn-v1
TITLE: Behaviour-set radial depth in a fixed-curvature Poincare ball as the residual-level Sinkhorn cost, leaving level 1 and the balanced assignment untouched
MOTIVATION: The previous round (round_20261106_020541_888, curvature-stratum-sinkhorn-v1) showed that relaying a behaviour cohesion offset onto the COARSE level buys level-1 separation (Hard ER 16.39 -> 6.36, all 256 codes used) but destroys the FINE level: only 955 distinct level-2 prefixes against the baseline's 7414 and a weighted 3-level collision of 0.771 against the reference 0.065, so the candidate was refused at the owner collision gate and never reached Stage 3. The real problem is therefore not how to re-partition the first level but how to keep the second and third level's resolution while behaviour still informs the quantiser. This idea addresses exactly that: cohesive items should be resolved FINELY (their residual compared at high angular sensitivity so the residual codebook must spend distinct codes on them), while weakly-attached items are resolved coarsely and share codes. In a hyperbolic ball this is a depth manipulation, not a re-assignment, so curvature is used to protect fine-level resolution instead of redrawing the coarse partition.
NOVELTY: Different from the mechanisms already tried here in three ways. (1) Location: it acts only on residual levels 2 and 3, level 1 is bit-identical to the baseline, so the coarse partition cannot be relaid out at all. (2) Form of the behaviour term: it is not an additive code offset (the thing that collapsed the L2 prefixes last round) but a per-item RADIAL SCALE on the item's own hyperbolic image, so it has no preferred code and cannot push a whole prefix group onto one residual code; Sinkhorn column marginals stay uniform and the balanced assignment rule is untouched. (3) Form of the geometry: curvature is not a penalty or a distance baseline, it is the mechanism that converts behaviour into resolution - the conformal factor 2/(1-c||x||^2) makes the same angular separation cost more at large radius, so raising an item's radius sharpens discrimination among residual codes, an effect that a Euclidean temperature cannot reproduce.
CHANGE: At level l in {2,3} the Euclidean residual vector is mapped into the Poincare ball of fixed curvature -1, its radius is multiplied by a per-item factor kappa_i derived from how much of its parent-prefix group's train-split transition mass lands on that item, and the squared Poincare distance to the also-mapped residual codes, divided by a measured per-level scale, becomes the cost matrix of the baseline's balanced Sinkhorn plan; the plan, its marginals and the argmax assignment rule are unchanged.
SPECIFICATION: Notation: level l in {2,3}; residual encoder output r_i in R^d for item i (N=24556 training items); codebook C_l = {c_k} in R^d, k=1..256; fixed curvature c=1. (A) Hyperbolic embedding map h(v) = tanh(sqrt(c)*||v||)/(sqrt(c)*max(||v||,eps)) * v, so ||h(v)|| < 1/sqrt(c). (B) Behaviour: from the train split only, T[j,i] = directed transition counts inside sessions (j before i within the session window); for the current epoch's stop-grad level-(l-1) assignment, G_l(p) = {j : prefix_{l-1}(j) = p}. rho_i = sum_{j in G_l(p_i)} T[j,i] / max(sum_{j in G_l(p_i)} sum_{i'} T[j,i'], eps). Group-relative standardisation with no free constant: s_i = sigmoid((rho_i - mu_i)/(mu_i + eps)) with mu_i = mean_{j in G_l(p_i)} rho_j; kappa_i = 0.5 + 0.5*s_i in (0.5,1). (C) Cost: xt_i = kappa_i * h(r_i), e_k = h(c_k), C_ik = ( d_P(xt_i, e_k) / D_l )^2 with d_P(a,b) = (2/sqrt(c)) * artanh(sqrt(c) * || (-a) (+) b ||) and D_l an EMA (momentum 0.9) of the mean over items of d_P(xt_i, e_{k_i^prev}) from the previous epoch's balanced assignment. (D) Plan: P = Sinkhorn(C/eps_s, row marginal 1/K, column marginal 1/N), identical routine and identical eps_s as the baseline; assignment k_i = argmax_k P_ik. (E) Level 1 uses the baseline cost matrix and the baseline Sinkhorn unchanged. (F) Switches in code: behaviour_off sets s_i = 1 so kappa_i = 1; geometry_off sets c -> 0 and replaces d_P by the Euclidean distance on kappa_i * r_i vs c_k (radial scaling survives, curvature does not).
COST: About 1.02-1.05x baseline wall clock, no extra parameters and no extra epochs. Per step at the frozen scale (24556 items / 24 steps = 1023 items per step): two residual levels x 1023 x 256 = 5.24e5 Poincare distance evaluations (~20 FLOP plus one artanh each, 10-20 MFLOP total, well under 1 ms on one GPU), one log-domain Sinkhorn of exactly the size the baseline already builds per residual level, and an O(N) radius/cost reduction. Once per epoch: a group-by over 24556 items to rebuild G_l(p) and a sparse gather over the train transition counts (seconds). One-time: construction of T from the train split (seconds to a minute, off the training loop). Stage 2 cost is dominated by the transformer encoder and Stage 3 is untouched, so total wall clock over 3000 epochs on four cards stays within a few percent of the frozen baseline.
HYPOTHESIS: If behaviour-driven radial depth really restores fine-level resolution instead of re-partitioning it, then with the coupling on and one run: (i) the number of distinct level-2 prefixes per epoch rises well above the 955 measured in round_20261106_020541_888 and approaches or exceeds the baseline's 7414, (ii) the weighted 3-level collision stays below the owner's 0.35 gate (the failed round was 0.771), (iii) level-1 Hard ER stays at the baseline value because level 1 is untouched, and (iv) Stage 3 test Recall@10 exceeds 0.065. If the L2 prefix count stays near 955 or the collision near 0.77, the metric-modulation route is falsified as a rescue of the fine level; if prefixes recover but Recall@10 stays at or below 0.065, curvature-driven resolution is not what limits the generative recommender.
RISKS: The behaviour term may be too weak once it enters only through a scalar radius: rho differences inside a prefix group can shrink to noise and kappa_i may cluster near 0.5+0.5*sigmoid(0), making the coupling nearly a no-op.; Curvature-depth sharpening could overshoot and make cohesive items saturate a few residual codes, lowering prefix diversity rather than raising it.; D_l estimated from the previous epoch's assignment could lag and either flatten or explode the cost scale in the first epochs.; Sinkhorn with a level-dependent cost scale may need more iterations than the baseline to reach uniform marginals, silently changing effective temperature.; Transition counts are sparse for long-tail items, so rho_i is dominated by noise exactly for the items that most need fine residual resolution.; An arctanh near the boundary of the ball can produce inf/NaN costs and poison the plan for a whole step.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Train-only prefix-conditional directed transition mass: how much of the incoming transition mass of an item's parent-prefix group actually lands on that item (a behaviour-conditioned assignment weight built from directed item transitions).
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: Built once from the Stage-2 training split only: for every training session, every ordered pair (j,i) of items within the session window contributes 1 to T[j,i] (sparse CSR). Per epoch, after the stop-grad level-(l-1) assignment is known, items are grouped by parent prefix p; rho_i is the column mass of column i restricted to the rows in G_l(p_i), normalised by the total mass of those rows, then standardised by the group mean and mapped through a sigmoid. No validation or test row is ever read; nothing in the loop depends on any Recall.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: The behaviour matrix is built exclusively from interaction rows of the Stage-2 training split; Stage-2 validation/test rows and all Stage-3 sequences are never touched by the SID construction. Prefix groups come from the model's own current quantiser assignment, not from any label or metric. The only feedback into the cost is the model's previous-epoch assignment (for the D_l scale) plus train-split counts, so no validation/test interaction can influence which SID an item receives, and Stage 3 sees a SID table frozen before it starts.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Fixed negative curvature c=1 Poincare ball, d_P(a,b) = (2/sqrt(c)) artanh(sqrt(c)||(-a) (+) b||), with the radial map h(v) = tanh(sqrt(c)||v||)/(sqrt(c)||v||) * v and the ball's conformal factor 2/(1-c||x||^2) acting as the resolution amplifier. Both the item residual and the residual codebook are mapped with h; no trainable curvature is introduced.
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: Inside the quantisation step of the residual levels 2 and 3, as the distance that fills the Sinkhorn cost matrix the baseline already computes; level 1 and all Euclidean losses of the RQ-VAE (reconstruction, commitment) are untouched.
COUPLING PATH: A single pathway: train-split transition counts -> parent-prefix group -> per-item rho_i -> radius multiplier kappa_i -> the item's hyperbolic image kappa_i * h(r_i) -> Poincare distance to h(c_k) -> normalised cost matrix -> the baseline's Sinkhorn plan -> level-l code and its codebook gradient. The behaviour term is the radius kappa_i and the curved term is d_P; if rho is set to a constant (behaviour off) kappa becomes 1 and the whole cost matrix changes; if c is set to 0 the distance changes to Euclidean and the whole cost matrix changes. Neither side survives removal, and removing either changes the assigned residual code and the resulting prefix statistics.
COUPLING EQUATION: C_ik = ( d_{P,c}( kappa(rho_i) * h(r_i), h(c_k) ) / D_l )^2 with kappa(rho_i) = 0.5 + 0.5 * sigmoid( (rho_i - mean_{G(p_i)} rho) / (mean_{G(p_i)} rho + eps) ), i.e. behaviour term kappa(rho_i) contracts/expands the item's radius and the curved term d_{P,c} turns that radius into angular resolution (behaviour off: kappa == 1; geometry off: d_{P,c} == ||.||, c -> 0).
OBSERVABLES: Per epoch: distinct level-2 prefixes (failed round 955, baseline 7414), distinct level-3 prefixes, weighted 3-level collision rate (failed round 0.771, gate 0.35, reference 0.065), level-1 Hard ER (must stay at the baseline 16.39 because level 1 is untouched), per-prefix entropy of the residual code distribution, effective kappa spread (diagnostic that the behaviour term is not degenerate), and codebook gradient norm per residual code. Final judgement: Stage 3 test Recall@10 against 0.065.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Same code with s_i := 1 for every item, so kappa_i = 1 and the residual cost is the plain squared Poincare distance between h(r_i) and h(c_k) divided by D_l; the transition matrix is still built but never read. Switch exists in code, not trained.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Same code with c -> 0: h becomes a bounded identity and d_P becomes the Euclidean distance, so the cost is || kappa_i * r_i - c_k ||^2 / D_l, i.e. the behaviour radius survives but curvature no longer converts depth into resolution. Switch exists in code, not trained.
ABLATION BOTH ON: The full coupling: kappa_i from train-split prefix-conditional transition mass multiplies the item radius inside the c=1 Poincare distance that fills the residual-level Sinkhorn cost, everything else at baseline. This is the only arm that is run, once, for 3000 Stage-2 epochs on four cards, followed by the frozen Stage 3 protocol.
SPEC EQUATIONS: eps=1e-8; c=1; for l in (2,3): r = residual_input(l) [N,d]; h(v) = tanh(sqrt(c)*||v||)/(sqrt(c)*max(||v||,eps))*v; e_k = h(c_k) [K,d]; rho = prefix_group_column_mass(T, G_l(prefix_{l-1})); mu = segment_mean(rho, prefix_{l-1}); s = sigmoid((rho - mu)/(mu + eps)); kappa = 0.5 + 0.5*s; xt = kappa[:,None]*h(r); d2_ik = ((2/sqrt(c))*arctanh(clip(sqrt(c)*||(-xt_i) (+) e_k||, 0, 1-1e-7)))**2; D_l = 0.9*D_l_prev + 0.1*mean_i sqrt(d2_{i,k_i_prev}), floored at 1e-3; C = d2/D_l**2; P = log_sinkhorn(C/eps_s, r=1/K, c=1/N, iters=baseline_iters); k_i = argmax_k P_ik.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs: the level-l residual encoder output r (N x d; stop-grad on kappa and on prefix assignments), the level-l codebook c_k (d-dim, as the baseline keeps it), the train-split sparse transition matrix T, and the previous epoch's level-(l-1) assignment. Outputs: the level-l code index k_i for every item, the balanced plan P (used exactly as the baseline uses it), and the scalar diagnostics D_l and the kappa distribution.
SAFEGUARDS: Clip sqrt(c)*||(-a) (+) b|| to [0, 1-1e-7] before arctanh; floor every norm denominator at eps=1e-8; keep kappa strictly inside (0.5,1) so ||xt|| <= tanh(||r||) < 1 and no point ever reaches the boundary; clamp D_l to at least 1e-3 and to at most the 99th percentile of the epoch's raw distances to stop a one-epoch scale blow-up; run Sinkhorn in the log domain with max-subtraction and the baseline's iteration count; if any cost entry is non-finite, fall back for that step to kappa=1 and c=0 for the affected rows only and log the event; stop-gradient kappa and the prefix assignment so the encoder cannot inflate cohesion to widen its own metric; build T once from the train split and assert that no validation/test row index enters it.
END IDEA
