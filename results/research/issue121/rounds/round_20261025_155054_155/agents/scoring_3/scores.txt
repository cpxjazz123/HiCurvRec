SCOPE: transition_geodesic_residual | IN_SCOPE | Uses training-only directed adjacent-item transitions with smoothed targets, explicitly maps residuals and codes through Poincare operations, and couples the target shift directly to geodesic code assignment and residual recurrence. Both behaviour-off and Euclidean geometry-off comparisons are specified, as are measurable assignment/Recall effects, numerical controls, inputs, and cost. The tangent-shift/subtraction construction is implementable, though it is an approximate rather than fully intrinsic residual quantizer and the stated log/exp round trip makes the exact effect of the curved representation worth verifying.
RATIONALE: transition_geodesic_residual | The residual itself and code ranking are jointly altered by a concrete transition-derived target and curved distance, making the mechanism meaningfully distinct from auxiliary regularization. Directed targets are relevant to next-item prediction and the coarse-to-fine weighting offers plausible downstream value. Implementation is moderately involved: multimodal successors are averaged, tangent residual subtraction is approximate, and the described log_0(exp_0(v)) round trip mathematically returns v (up to numerical effects), so curvature's effective role may principally be in code distance. Expected Stage3 benefit remains uncertain and requires controlled ablations.

SCOPE: transition_busemann_gate | IN_SCOPE | Computes a training-only directed successor signal and uses it with confidence in a fixed-negative-curvature Poincare/Busemann score that changes first-stage code assignment. It names behaviour-off and Euclidean/geometry-off controls, assignment and Stage3 effects, clipping/smoothing safeguards, and computational cost. The formula as initially written is singular at the boundary, but the specification supplies a bounded Poisson-kernel-style alternative and clarifies the score sign, so a usable curved directional potential is present.
RATIONALE: transition_busemann_gate | Directional boundary potential is a distinct and testable way to make successor behaviour affect code ranking, rather than appending a behaviour code or leaving assignments unchanged. The training-only signal is prediction-relevant, and first-stage-only application limits disruption while offering a useful mechanism contrast. Implementation is feasible but requires careful validation of the stable potential, boundary handling, and sign convention; the averaged successor direction can obscure branching, and lambda selection must remain training-only. Stage3 gains are plausible, not assured.

SCOPE: branch_curvature_gate | IN_SCOPE | Computes training-only outgoing branching entropy and uses it to set bounded negative curvature inside Lorentz lifts and code distances at every residual level, directly changing assignment geometry. It specifies Euclidean geometry-off and behaviour/geometry interventions, observable assignment/gradient and Recall effects, entropy smoothing, curvature bounds, numerical safeguards, and cost. The per-item curvature distance and residual assignment are sufficiently explicit to implement.
RATIONALE: branch_curvature_gate | This has the strongest direct coupling: removing the behaviour signal removes item-specific curvature, while removing curved geometry removes the Lorentz distance computation. Branching entropy is a meaningful training-only behaviour statistic and the design tests a different mechanism from directional target shifts or Busemann preference. Practical implementation is moderate rather than trivial because item-varying curvature shares one codebook, entropy is unreliable for sparse items, and Lorentz distance calculations need stable numerical handling. Adaptive geometry could improve assignments useful to generation, but global codebook distortion and Stage3 transfer are substantial uncertainties.
