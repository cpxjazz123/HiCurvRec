IDEA: cohort_curved_capacity
TITLE: Cohort-conditioned Lorentz assignment with a beam-budgeted first level
MOTIVATION: A text-near item may be a plausible next item for multiple, behaviorally distinct cohorts; assigning all such items by text-only distance can make T1 either uninformative or too hard for the Stage3 beam to rank.
NOVELTY: Unlike a cohort pair-margin on code vectors, this makes per-item next-transition cohort distributions alter the actual first-level nearest-code assignment cost. A fixed small T1 capacity is integrated into that behavior-conditioned curved assignment, not tested as an isolated codebook-size change.
CHANGE: Form a train-only cohort profile for each item from users' preceding-history vectors, then combine its mismatch-to-code cost with Poincare geodesic distance to assign T1 among 32 effective groups. Keep T2/T3 responsible for residual discrimination and deterministic T4 solely for collision extension.
SPECIFICATION: Let p_i be the smoothed distribution of training transitions into item i across K cohorts; let q_c be the smoothed cohort distribution of items assigned to T1 code c, updated by minibatch EMA. Set C(i,c)=d^2_{P,-κ}(z_i,e_c)+λ KL(p_i||q_c), κ>0. Assign hard c_i=argmin_c C(i,c) subject to per-minibatch balanced capacity via 5 Sinkhorn iterations on exp(-C/τ), then update q_c using assigned items. Use 32 first-level codes. Target bit budget: T1 ≤5 bits (32 groups); T2 and T3 together carry most remaining discrimination (aim 2–4 bits each empirically); T4 has NLL near 0 and is used only to resolve collisions.
COST: Precompute transition cohort counts once, O(E_train·d) plus O(E_train·K); each step adds O(B·32·d) Poincare distances and O(B·32·K) KL/Sinkhorn operations, roughly 5–15% over baseline depending on embedding dimension, with cached profiles. At frozen 24,556 items/24 steps/epoch/3,000 epochs (about 1,023 items per step), this is approximately 31.5M item-code distance evaluations and 31.5M·K cohort terms per epoch, or 94.5B distance evaluations over the run; those are batched GPU operations, not additional per-item graph traversals.
HYPOTHESIS: If training-transition cohort mixture is predictive of which next-items share a rankable prefix, behavior-conditioned geodesic assignment will change T1 membership and shift discrimination out of T4; it is worth keeping only if beam survival improves and Stage3 test_R@10 exceeds 0.065 with both-on outperforming both controls.
RISKS: Sparse items yield noisy cohort profiles; capacity balancing may over-regularize popularity skew or conflict with semantic reconstruction; minibatch Sinkhorn capacity may not match global occupancy.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: User-history cohort distribution of directed next-item transitions.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: On Stage0 TRAIN sequences only, for each adjacent (history context → next item i), average frozen Stage1 vectors of up to the preceding five items, cluster contexts into K=8 deterministic k-means cohorts, and accumulate smoothed cohort counts for i. Normalize counts with Dirichlet smoothing α=1 to obtain p_i. No validation/test examples participate.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: Freeze p_i and cohort definitions computed exclusively from training interactions before Stage2. SID construction and assignment consume only p_i, frozen item embeddings and Stage2 parameters; exclude all validation/test histories, targets and aggregates.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Squared Poincare-ball geodesic distance on curvature -κ, κ=1, after exp-map of encoder and code vectors from the origin; project to radius at most 1-ε.
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: In the T1 assignment logits and hard assignment itself, rather than in an unrelated auxiliary loss.
COUPLING PATH: Training cohorts determine p_i; p_i changes the KL term in each curved-distance assignment cost; that cost changes Sinkhorn weights and hard T1 IDs, feeding subsequent residual quantization and the exported SID.
COUPLING EQUATION: C(i,c)=d^2_{P,-κ}(exp_o(z_i),exp_o(e_c)) + λ_beh KL(p_i^train || q_c^train); A=Sinkhorn(exp(-C/τ)), c_i=argmax_c A_ic. Both the behavior term and curved term jointly determine assignment.
OBSERVABLES: On the canonical 52,855 valid pairs with ten fixed negatives, predict L1 Hard ER to decrease from the saturated 1.0 toward a non-degenerate value while remaining useful; gold-prefix survival should rise at depth 1, remain higher at depths 2 and 3 under width-20 search, and stay high at depth 4. T4 teacher-forced NLL should decrease/remain near zero rather than absorb discrimination. Stage3 test_R@10 should improve over controls and target >0.065. Report actual values; these are directional hypotheses, not acceptance thresholds.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Replace each p_i with the global cohort prior, retaining κ, 32 codes, capacity procedure and all other settings; expect changed assignments and prefix survival versus both-on.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Replace Poincare distance with matched squared Euclidean distance, retaining p_i, 32 codes, temperatures and capacity; expect changed assignments/prefix survival versus both-on.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Use train-derived p_i and the stated curvature/cost for the 32-capacity T1; compare under identical 3,000-epoch Stage2 and frozen Stage3 protocol.
SPEC EQUATIONS: For x_i encoder output, map z_i=exp_o(x_i), code e_c=exp_o(v_c), with d_P(x,y)=κ^{-1/2} arcosh(1+2κ||x-y||²/((1-κ||x||²)(1-κ||y||²))). Compute C_ic=d_P(z_i,e_c)^2+λ KL(p_i||q_c). Apply entropic capacity assignment A=Sinkhorn(exp(-C/τ), row mass 1, column mass B/32); select argmax A for hard IDs, use straight-through assignment. EMA-update q_c from assigned p_i. Continue standard Euclidean residual RQ at T2/T3; T4 remains the existing deterministic collision-extension token, with no learned signal assigned to it.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs are train-only transition contexts, frozen Stage1 item vectors, Stage2 encoder and code vectors, p_i, q_c, κ, λ, τ and minibatch item IDs. Outputs are balanced soft T1 weights, hard T1 IDs and gradients/EMA updates to encoder/code vectors and cohort prototypes; remaining SID positions follow baseline residual quantization plus deterministic extension.
SAFEGUARDS: Clamp ball norms below (1-ε)/sqrt(κ), use float64 for distance denominators then cast logits, clamp KL probabilities to ≥1e-8, center costs before exp, subtract row maxima, clamp Sinkhorn exponent range, and fall back to the prior for low-support p_i. Keep λ and τ fixed from train-only calibration; log assignment occupancy and finite-loss checks.
END IDEA

IDEA: transition_hyperbolic_tree
TITLE: Geodesic transition-tree routing for behaviorally aligned prefixes
MOTIVATION: The beam loses the gold path when high-branching next-item distinctions are placed at an unpredictable prefix level; directed successor structure can define a coarse routing hierarchy aligned with what histories actually precede targets.
NOVELTY: Rather than pairwise source-code contrast or simply shrinking a codebook, this builds a coarse-to-fine hierarchy by routing transition-neighborhood prototypes along hyperbolic geodesics and uses the routing score directly in T1 assignment. The hierarchy's branching budget explicitly preserves T4 as a collision token.
CHANGE: Construct popularity-adjusted successor neighborhoods from training transitions and route their item representations to a 16-way T1 geodesic prototype tree. Trainable tree-child decisions are behavior-conditioned by the neighborhood signature, while T2/T3 encode residual distinctions.
SPECIFICATION: For each item i calculate outgoing transition distribution r_i(j) from training (u,i→j), with edge weight count(i,j)/sqrt(freq(i)freq(j)); retain top 32 neighbors and normalize. Build a fixed 8-dimensional signature s_i as weighted mean of their frozen Stage1 vectors (zero for no neighbors). For each of 16 trainable T1 prototypes a_c in the Poincare ball, define score S_ic=-d_P(exp_o(Wx_i),a_c)^2/τ + γ cos(s_i,g_c), where g_c is a trainable Euclidean tangent vector mapped by exp_o to ball prototype b_c; to make coupling geometric rather than appended, use the full score S_ic=-d_P(exp_o(Wx_i),a_c)^2/τ - γ d_P(exp_o(U s_i),b_c)^2/τ. Assign by argmax; train W,U,a,b with reconstruction plus soft assignment cross-entropy to the routing target obtained by nearest child in the same score. Capacity budget: T1 exactly 16 effective groups (≤4 bits), T2/T3 carry discrimination (target combined ≥8 bits as measured empirically); T4 remains deterministic and near-constant NLL.
COST: One-time sparse transition graph aggregation O(E_train + E_train·d) and top-32 selection. Per step adds two Poincare score matrices of shape B×16, O(B·16·d), plus existing quantization; at 1,023 items/step this is about 16.4k prototype distances per branch, ~32.8k total per step, 0.8M per epoch, 2.36B over 3,000 epochs; expected 5–12% wall-clock overhead, no test-time work.
HYPOTHESIS: If items linked by high-quality directed transitions form predictable coarse neighborhoods, curved transition routing will put useful distinctions at T1 without creating excessive beam branching; keep only if depth-1 survival rises or holds while depth-2/3 survival and Stage3 test_R@10 improve, reaching >0.065.
RISKS: Sparse outgoing edges and popularity normalization may produce unstable signatures; train transitions can be non-stationary; 16-way routing may collapse or over-group semantically distinct targets. The score's two terms can compete unless γ is calibrated.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Popularity-adjusted directed next-item transition neighborhood.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: Count adjacent item transitions (i→j) within TRAIN sequences only; estimate p(j|i) with add-one smoothing, reweight by inverse square-root endpoint frequencies, retain top 32 outgoing neighbors, normalize, and compute s_i as their weighted mean of frozen Stage1 item vectors. Compute before Stage2 and freeze.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: Graph counts, endpoint frequencies, neighbor lists and signatures use only TRAIN rows and training-period histories. Exclude validation/test transitions and all their statistics from graph construction, code assignment, hyperparameter choice for SID generation and collision extension.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Poincare geodesic distance at fixed negative curvature -κ=-1, applied after origin exponential maps to the item point, transition-signature point and trainable child prototypes.
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: T1 routing logits and the behavior-signature branch used to choose T1; the resulting hard routing controls the first SID digit.
COUPLING PATH: Train-only successor graph yields s_i; exp-map and geodesic distance turn s_i into a curved behavior-routing score against trainable prototypes; the joint score chooses T1 and hence changes all downstream residual IDs.
COUPLING EQUATION: S_ic=-d^2_{P,-κ}(exp_o(Wx_i),a_c)/τ - γ_beh d^2_{P,-κ}(exp_o(U s_i),b_c)/τ; c_i=argmax_c S_ic. The train-only behavior signal and curved operator both enter the same routing score.
OBSERVABLES: Predict L1 Hard ER on canonical 52,855 valid pairs plus ten fixed negatives to become non-saturated (directionally below 1.0); gold-prefix survival should be higher at depths 1, 2 and 3 under width-20 search, with depth 4 no worse. T4 teacher-forced NLL should fall or remain near zero, not rise as discrimination shifts there. Stage3 test_R@10 should exceed both controls and target >0.065.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Set every s_i to the global mean signature (equivalently γ_beh=0), retaining 16 codes and Poincare item routing; predicts different T1 assignment distribution and prefix survival.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Replace both Poincare distances with squared Euclidean distances on matched tangent vectors, retaining transition graph, γ, 16-way capacity and training schedule; predicts different assignments/prefix survival.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Train with frozen popularity-adjusted train successor signatures and both Poincare distance terms; run the frozen Stage3 protocol unchanged.
SPEC EQUATIONS: Let n_ij be train adjacent counts and f_i=sum_j n_ij. Set w_ij=(n_ij+1)/sqrt((f_i+|V|)(f_j+|V|)); retain top-32 j, normalize to π_ij, s_i=Σ_j π_ij e_j. Map z_i=exp_o(Wx_i), h_i=exp_o(Us_i). For c∈{1..16}, score S_ic=-d_P(z_i,a_c)^2/τ-γd_P(h_i,b_c)^2/τ. Train with L=L_RQVAE+η CE(softmax(S_i), stopgrad(softmax(S_i/τ_target))) plus baseline reconstruction/commitment; hard T1 ID is argmax S. Use T2/T3 baseline residual quantizers and existing deterministic T4 extension. Compare behavior-off and Euclidean replacements at the same capacity and seeds.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs are TRAIN interaction sequences, frozen Stage1 vectors, Stage2 encoder, 16 pairs of child prototypes and hyperparameters κ,τ,γ,η. Outputs are frozen sparse graph/signatures, T1 hard IDs and gradients to routing encoders/prototypes; T2/T3 residual IDs and deterministic T4 yield the fixed four-token SID.
SAFEGUARDS: Smooth counts and ignore zero-degree signatures via global-mean fallback; cap edges to 32; normalize signatures; project mapped points to radius ≤1-ε; compute acosh arguments with clamp ≥1 in float64; clip gradients; stop-gradient routing targets to avoid chasing logits; log per-code occupancy and verify T4 NLL stays near zero. Set γ,τ,η from train-only statistics and do not tune SID construction on validation/test interactions.
END IDEA
