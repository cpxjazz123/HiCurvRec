IDEA: cohort_ball_budget
TITLE: Cohort-conditioned Poincare assignment with a 32-group first-level budget
MOTIVATION: A 256-way first token is expensive for a width-20 decoder, yet a constant first token wastes prefix capacity. This mechanism tests whether a small first-level vocabulary can be both cohort-predictable and discriminative, leaving the later levels to resolve items.
NOVELTY: Unlike an independent transition loss or a codebook-size-only ablation, training-history cohort transitions move each item's point in the curved assignment computation before its first-level code is selected. The 32-way budget is explicit and the behavior signal controls the curved group choice.
CHANGE: Replace the 256-way L1 assignment by 32 prototypes in a Poincare ball; add a cohort-derived tangent offset to each item's encoder point, map it onto the ball, and assign by geodesic distance. Keep the existing fixed-width four-token SID and deterministic collision-extension token; train L2/L3 for remaining discrimination and regularize T4 toward near-constant use.
SPECIFICATION: For each training item i, compute transition-cohort vector b_i (definition below), project it with trainable W_b, and set z_i=exp^c_0(u_i+alpha W_b b_i), where u_i is the L1 encoder output and c=1. For 32 trainable ball prototypes p_k, q(k|i)=softmax_k(-d_c(z_i,p_k)^2/tau). Train with the existing RQ-VAE objective plus lambda_bal KL(mean_i q(.|i)||Uniform(32)); use straight-through argmin_k q(k|i) for hard SID export. The balance term prevents a nominal 32-codebook collapsing to a few effective groups. Set alpha initially 0.2, tau=0.1 and tune only on the frozen training protocol; T4 commitment is to remain a collision extension, not an information-bearing residual level.
COST: 32 rather than 256 L1 distances reduces that part of assignment work; added work is one sparse cohort-vector projection and 32 Poincare distances per item. At 24,556 items per epoch: about 785,792 prototype distances/epoch, 18.8M over 24 steps, and 2.36B over 3000 epochs (plus cohort projection); this is small relative to the encoder and baseline 256-way L1 search. Four-card 3000-epoch run; no extra interaction data or Stage3 cost.
HYPOTHESIS: If cohort-conditioned curved grouping makes next targets predictable without overbranching, the both-on arm will improve gold-prefix survival at depths 2–3 and width-20 coverage enough to yield test_R@10 > 0.065. Required directional measurements: L1 Hard ER on the canonical 52,855 valid pairs/ten fixed negatives should rise from the single-code construction toward useful discrimination, not saturate at 1.0; depth-1 survival should remain high, depth-2 and depth-3 survival should increase, and depth-4 survival should not be the sole gain. T4 teacher-forced NLL should stay low/near-constant rather than absorbing information. Report all outcomes; these are falsifiable predictions, not guaranteed thresholds.
RISKS: Cohort counts may be sparse or popularity-dominated; 32 groups can overcompress item distinctions; balance can oppose the true transition distribution; curvature or tangent offsets may saturate near the ball boundary. A low T4 NLL target could inadvertently make T4 useless for collision extension. Improved prefix coverage may not translate to Stage3 ranking.
BEHAVIOR SIGNAL: Training-only directed next-item transition cohort profile, representing which training user cohorts tend to transition into item i.
BEHAVIOR COMPUTATION: From the training split only, form directed counts C_ab over consecutive (history item a, next item b) events, grouped by user-history cohort g. Define cohort for each event from the preceding training-only history using a fixed hash of its most frequent category/embedding cluster (clusterer fit on training items only). For each item i, b_i[g]=log(1+sum_a C^g_ai); normalize b_i to unit L1 mass and retain top 16 cohort coordinates. No validation/test events or histories are read.
LEAKAGE BOUNDARY: Compute C and cohort assignments exclusively from the training split, persist the resulting item-indexed b_i table, and expose it only to Stage2 training. SID export for every item uses its fixed training-derived b_i; validation/test interactions are never inputs to fitting, assignment, hyperparameter choice, or SID construction.
HYPERBOLIC OPERATOR: Poincare exponential map exp^c_0 and squared Poincare geodesic distance d_c^2 with fixed negative curvature -c=-1.
HYPERBOLIC LOCATION: L1 assignment path during each Stage2 training step and deterministic SID inference/export; cohort tangent offset is applied before the exponential map, and the resulting ball point is compared to the 32 curved prototypes.
COUPLING PATH: b_i is projected into the encoder tangent representation, added to u_i, mapped by exp^c_0, and scored against Poincare prototypes; changing b_i changes z_i and hence the actual L1 assignment, while replacing Poincare operations changes the assignment score itself.
COUPLING EQUATION: z_i=exp^c_0(u_i+alpha W_b b_i); q(k|i)=softmax_k[-d_c(z_i,p_k)^2/tau]; k_i=argmax_k q(k|i). Both b_i and d_c are in this single assignment pathway.
OBSERVABLES: Compare L1 Hard ER on the canonical 52,855 valid pairs with ten fixed negatives; gold-prefix survival at depths 1,2,3,4 under width-20 beam; teacher-forced NLL(T4); and Stage3 test_R@10. Predicted: L1 ER becomes discriminative but not maximally branching; depth-1 remains high, depths 2–3 survival and width-20 coverage rise, T4 NLL remains low, and test_R@10 exceeds 0.065 if the hypothesis holds.
ABLATION BEHAVIOR OFF: Replace b_i with the same constant zero vector for every item; retain 32 Poincare prototypes, curvature, and all other settings. The cohort-directed offsets disappear, so assignment/prefix structure should change.
ABLATION GEOMETRY OFF: Matched Euclidean assignment with 32 prototypes: use u_i+alpha W_b b_i, squared Euclidean distance to Euclidean prototypes, and the identical temperature, balance, losses, and optimizer. The curved map and distance are removed; prefix structure should change relative to both-on.
ABLATION BOTH ON: Training-only b_i offsets plus exp^c_0 and Poincare distance to 32 prototypes, with the stated balancing and T4 constraint; evaluate under the frozen Stage3 protocol.
SPEC EQUATIONS: Let h_i be the 16-sparse normalized cohort histogram, u_i encoder output, and P={p_k}_{k=1}^{32} ball prototypes. Compute v_i=u_i+alpha W_b h_i; z_i=exp^c_0(v_i); logits l_ik=-d_c(z_i,p_k)^2/tau; q_i=softmax(l_i); hard k_i=argmax q_i. Optimize L_RQVAE + lambda_bal KL(mean_i q_i || Uniform_32) and existing reconstruction/commitment losses. Quantize residuals with the unchanged L2/L3 stages; preserve the deterministic fourth collision-extension token and monitor its NLL.
SPEC INPUTS OUTPUTS: Inputs are training item embedding/encoder vector u_i, fixed training-only sparse cohort histogram h_i, trainable W_b and 32 ball prototypes. Outputs are soft assignment q_i for loss/gradient and hard L1 token k_i for SID construction; later SID tokens remain produced by the existing residual quantizer.
SAFEGUARDS: Clamp tangent norms and ball norms to (1-epsilon)/sqrt(c); implement stable exp/log and acosh distance with epsilon clamps; compute distances/softmax in float32; normalize sparse histograms with zero-vector fallback; clip gradients; use tau floor 0.05 and monitor prototype occupancy, assignment entropy, boundary saturation, L1 ER, prefix survival, and T4 NLL. Fixed c=1 avoids curvature instability; no batch may include validation/test-derived behavior.
END IDEA
