# Adversarial mathematics review

**Scope.** Line references below are to `proposals.md`. This is an operator-level review, not a judgement about novelty or likely metric gains. Under the frozen protocol, neither proposal may change the latent dimension, codebook cardinalities, token count, data/splits, or training/evaluation recipes.

## Mechanism 1 — Signed geodesic-triangle-area assignment

**Verdict: REVISE. Do not join as written.** The intended area signal is meaningful in a chosen oriented totally-geodesic 2-plane, but the proposal does not define such an orientation for general triples in 32 dimensions, and its claimed invariants/gradient story fail at important degeneracies.

### Domain and definition (lines 11–17)

- `q=Exp_p(Log_p(x))` is mathematically `x` for points in the ball (line 11, `α=1`), so this is not a distinct residual target. That identity is fine away from numerical boundary issues, but makes the stated operator simply a triangle on `(p,x,z)`.
- Three non-collinear points in a constant-curvature space determine a totally geodesic 2-plane. They do **not**, in dimension 32, inherit a canonical orientation from “an ordered orthonormal 2-frame at p.” A single fixed frame at `p` does not orient every other 2-plane. One can project the frame onto the triangle plane, but that projection can be linearly dependent or zero, and its orientation flips/discontinues at the corresponding locus. Thus the sign in lines 15 and 19 is not defined globally as stated. An orientation on the ambient tangent space does not by itself orient every 2-plane either.
- Collinear triples (including `p=q`, `p=z`, `q=z`, and repeated points) have zero area by convention, but the signed area as a function of the vertices is generally not differentiable across the collinear locus: the plane orientation/sign changes there. Repeated items are not a special remedy; they land exactly on this singular set. At `p=q`, the angle formula includes an undefined angle at a zero-length side unless area zero is supplied separately.
- “Degenerate base” is not handled. If the host is at/near the ball boundary, the conformal factor `λ_p=2/(1-c||p||²)` blows up, and tangent coordinates, log/exp, and gradients become ill-conditioned. The open ball excludes the boundary; clamping changes the operator and can create zero/altered gradients. No admissible margin from the boundary is specified.
- The distance term `d²` is finite for strict interior points. Its formula has `artanh` singularity at norm `1/sqrt(c)`; it is well-defined for interior points, but has poor conditioning near the boundary. The angle-defect expression `(π-θp-θq-θz)/c` is valid only for a nondegenerate triangle with a consistently defined angle convention. It is not a numerically safe definition at zero sides/collinearity.

### Score and gradient (lines 13, 21)

- The hard score is finite for strict interior, nondegenerate inputs if a plane orientation is supplied. It is not a globally differentiable score over the quantiser's stated domain: signed area is non-smooth/ambiguous at collinearity and orientation-chart singularities; triangle angles are also ill-conditioned as a side vanishes or the triangle degenerates. The hard `argmin` is itself discontinuous at code ties, as expected, but the proposal separately claims gradients through a soft assignment.
- Softening `-τ logsumexp(-S/τ)` does not cure a non-differentiable `S_k` at degenerate triangles. Ties between candidates make the hard assignment discontinuous, while the soft loss is smooth only wherever each candidate score is smooth. The stated straight-through/selected-code path does not define derivatives of the area at these singularities.
- `p` is the current host and is used in the score and in `q`; line 21 says no gradients to `p` “as a separately learned basepoint,” but does not say whether gradients flow through the host-producing earlier stages. If they do, all the singularities above feed upstream; if not, this is a stop-gradient design choice that needs specification. “No separately learned basepoint” is not equivalent to stop-gradient through `p`.

### Invariants and counterexamples (lines 15, 23)

- (i) Zero area for collinear triples is a valid convention. Sign reversal under swapping `q,z` holds only if a coherent orientation convention for each triangle plane is fixed. The proposed frame rule does not provide one globally; a projected-frame convention can flip at its singular locus. With an arbitrary per-triangle sign choice, the swap invariant is not guaranteed.
- (ii) Agreement with Gauss–Bonnet is tautological only if area is defined from the same oriented triangle and its interior angles. It is not an independent validation of the proposed signed extension in 32-D. Angle sums give unsigned area; the sign needs the missing orientation rule. Near degeneracy, “within numerical tolerance” is especially fragile.
- (iii) The **unsigned** area magnitude and geodesic distances are invariant under common hyperbolic isometries. The signed area is invariant only under orientation-preserving isometries with a transported orientation; a reflection reverses it. “Orientation transformed consistently” in line 23 does not rescue invariance if the fixed frame at `p` is held fixed rather than transported. Explicitly distinguish orientation-preserving from orientation-reversing isometries.
- (iv) The area tends to zero as `c→0` for a fixed nondegenerate Euclidean-limit triangle, since curvature times area is the angle defect and hyperbolic area approaches Euclidean area. But the *assignment* need not become the stated harmless control: `β` and score scaling are not specified as functions of `c`, and the area may vanish while the distance term still decides the same nontrivial partition. Conversely, if `β` is rescaled or the area term normalized by `c`, the limit can retain Euclidean triangle-area information, which is not “area vanishes.” State exactly what is held fixed and compare assignments against the frozen control.
- A simple counterexample to “area must affect assignment”: choose all `z_k` on one geodesic ray from `p` (area zero) or choose `β` large enough that distance dominates. The validation variance condition does not prevent either behavior on training/test inputs, nor does “at least half the variance” ensure area changes the argmin. Lines 17/25 correctly propose an assignment-agreement falsifier, but the threshold and split must be registered without test leakage.

### Capacity / hidden-form audit

Three 256-way choices and three indices are preserved on paper (lines 19, 23). However, the auxiliary soft area loss and any extra learned orientation/frame parameters are not specified in the frozen recipe. A learned frame could add capacity; a fixed arbitrary frame produces coordinate dependence. `α=1` makes the target exactly `x`, so this is a code-assignment scoring change rather than a new residual geometry. It is not Euclidean reformulation, but the flat-limit control is not operationally defined well enough to establish that it differs from an ordinary Euclidean assignment in the actual limiting experiment.

### Minimal revision

Define a canonical, isometry-compatible orientation for each triangle plane or explicitly restrict the mechanism to a fixed oriented 2-D subspace (and admit the resulting coordinate dependence); specify behavior/regularization on collinear, repeated, and near-boundary cases; state whether gradients pass through `p`; and give a fixed-`β`, fixed-normalization `c→0` control with finite-difference/autograd checks away from and near degeneracy. If no globally coherent signed convention is intended, use unsigned area and stop claiming sign reversal/handedness.

## Mechanism 2 — Non-associative gyro-composition quantization

**Verdict: REJECT as written.** The score mixes points and tangent vectors in incompatible spaces, includes an unused transported code vector, and applies a basepoint-`o` gyration to a point at host `p`. These are definition-level failures, not merely implementation details.

### Domain and operator consistency (lines 29–40)

- Möbius addition in line 31 is defined for ball points. Its denominator is positive for strict interior inputs, but boundary points are excluded and near-boundary arithmetic is ill-conditioned. Thus “all points remain strictly inside” (line 44) is an invariant to enforce, not something guaranteed by the formula without bounded inputs/clamping.
- The prose defines `v_k=PT_{o→p}(Log_o(z_k))` and `u_k=Exp_p(v_k)` at line 33, but neither `v_k` nor `u_k` appears in the score at lines 35–36. Instead, the score re-transforms `T_s Log_o(z_k)` and exponentiates at `o`. The purported host-tangent code semantic is therefore not the code operation actually scored. Also, `T_s` is applied before the stated transport in the prose, but no compatible domain/codomain convention for `T_s` is specified.
- `G_k` in line 35 is a point-valued composition followed by `Log_p`, hence a tangent vector at `p`. The first term `||G_k||²_{g_p}` is well-typed and nonnegative.
- The second term is not well-typed as written. `gyr[p, Exp_o(...)]` is the usual Möbius gyration acting on ball points/vectors in the origin chart; `Exp_p(r)` is a ball point representing `x`, not a tangent vector in a stated domain of that gyration. Subtracting `gyr[...] Exp_p(r) - Exp_p(r)` is an ambient coordinate difference, not a tangent vector at `p`; applying `g_p` to it does not make it a Riemannian tangent difference. In particular, gyration at `o` fixes `o`, not the host `p`, and is not the claimed rotation in `T_p`.
- The definition `gyr[a,b]w=-(a⊕b)⊕(a⊕(b⊕w))` is a gyrogroup expression with convention-sensitive domains. Its output is a ball point when `w` is a ball point; it cannot silently be substituted as a linear map on `T_p`. If inputs approach the boundary, intermediate compositions and inverse/addition operations are numerically unsafe.
- A degenerate host `p=o` is not itself singular for standard Poincaré log/exp/PT, but exposes the mismatch: `PT_{o→o}` is identity and the code tangent is clear, while the score's second term still has no specified tangent interpretation. At a host near the boundary, `λ_p` and all host-tangent norm calculations blow up. Repeated items give `r=0`; the first term remains defined, but the second term as written still compares incompatible point coordinates.

### Finiteness, differentiability, and limits (lines 35–46)

- For strict interior points, standard `Log_p` and `Exp_p` have removable zero-vector limits (so `r=0` is ordinarily well-defined), but implementations often encounter `0/0` in direction-normalized formulas and need explicit stable branches. At the boundary, the Poincaré conformal factor and `artanh`/log expressions diverge. No parameterization or margin guarantees strict interior codewords/hosts throughout optimization.
- Assuming the second term were repaired, the first term is differentiable for interior points except for the discrete argmin boundary; the softmin is smooth wherever its scores are. The present second term has no well-defined differential because its operands/types are unspecified. A straight-through estimator cannot fix an undefined forward score.
- As `c→0`, Möbius addition tends to vector addition and gyration to identity only under a coherent scaling of coordinates/tangent vectors and fixed bounded inputs (line 44). The proposal does not specify that scaling, nor the control's `γ` scaling. Even if gyration tends to identity, the first term becomes `||code + residual||²` (with the displayed composition order), which is not automatically the frozen Euclidean residual quantizer's score `||residual-code||²`; sign/target convention matters. It may select a different partition. The claimed harmless control is therefore not established.
- “Gyration is metric-preserving at the appropriate tangent space” and “transport preserves tangent norms” (line 44) are true only when the correct map and tangent metrics/spaces are used. Parallel transport is an isometry between the specified Riemannian tangent spaces. The score does not use its transported code vector; and gyration's isometry property does not validate subtracting two ball coordinates and measuring the result with `g_p`.
- Association order does differ generically for non-collinear inputs in a nonzero-curvature gyrogroup, but that fact alone does not establish that the particular score's second term measures the associator. Test explicit triples, including collinear and zero cases; collinear inputs can have trivial gyration, and `r=0` makes any residual-rotation distinction vanish.

### Invariants and counterexamples (lines 38, 44)

- (i) Identity laws for Möbius addition hold for valid interior points. They do not imply the score uses the declared `u_k` or transport.
- (ii) The flat limit statement is an operation-level limit, not an assignment-level result. Set `γ>0` fixed: the gyration term may vanish but the first-term limit is a sum-norm score, whose argmin need not match the frozen control. For example, in one dimension with residual `r=1`, candidate vectors `v=1` and `v=-2`, minimizing `|v+r|²` picks `-2`, while minimizing `|r-v|²` picks `1`.
- (iii) Metric preservation is not verified by the displayed coordinate-difference expression. It is a counterexample to the stated invariant to take a nonzero host `p` and a point `x≠p`: the origin-based gyration moves `x` around `o`, whereas a host-tangent rotation should fix the host/basepoint. The two outputs are not a pair of vectors in `T_p` under the stated definitions.
- (iv) Generic non-associativity holds, but the composition expression in `G_k` is not itself a comparison of the two association orders: it explicitly uses one order. The gyration term is intended to encode the difference, but currently is not the corresponding tangent displacement. The demanded numerical verification must compare both orders with matching conventions, rather than infer non-associativity from the formula.
- (v) PT norm preservation is standard only for the explicitly transported `v_k`; it is irrelevant to the implemented score because `v_k` is unused. A learned invertible `T_s` is not generally norm-preserving; “bounded and invertible” does not imply an isometry and can change code-vector scales.
- (vi) Möbius addition of interior points stays interior in exact arithmetic. Exponentials of unconstrained tangent vectors also yield interior points mathematically, but finite precision and code/host parameterization can approach the boundary arbitrarily closely. Clamping can change gradients/assignments; reporting only clamp fraction does not make a material clamp harmless.

### Capacity / hidden-form audit

The nominal 256 codes and three emitted indices remain (lines 40, 44). But learned invertible `T_s` is an extra learned linear/nonlinear transform with unconstrained capacity and could absorb a refuted Euclidean reparameterization or change codebook geometry; “bounded” is insufficient without a fixed parameterization, norm/singular-value bounds, and parameter budget. The score's flat-limit first term is a sum rather than a residual difference and therefore does not satisfy the proposal's own Euclidean-control requirement. This is not a valid demonstration of a genuinely hyperbolic contribution until the corrected score is compared to a matched frozen Euclidean control.

### Minimal revision

Rewrite the score with explicit types and a single consistent chart: define the code tangent in `T_p`, define the associator displacement as the difference between two valid compositions in that same tangent space (using `Log_p` after each), and measure it with `g_p`. Remove the unused `u_k` or use it. Specify the basepoint/domain of gyration and an operation that fixes the intended host. Fix `T_s` capacity and bounds. Finally derive the `c→0` score algebraically and make it reduce to the frozen residual score, then test repeated/zero residuals, collinear triples, `p=o`, and near-boundary points with finite-difference gradients. Until then, reject rather than implement an ambiguous operator.

## Overall disposition

Neither candidate is currently suitable for `APPROVE_FOR_JOIN_E`. Mechanism 1 has a potentially repairable but globally undefined signed-area convention and degeneracy-gradient problems (**REVISE**). Mechanism 2's central score is ill-typed and does not implement its stated transport/gyration semantics (**REJECT**). No metric threshold can compensate for an undefined or inconsistent operator; after mathematical repair, each still must pass the exact conjunctive frozen thresholds and demonstrate a non-negligible hyperbolic contribution.
