# Adversarial prior-art and novelty review

## Scope and standard

This is a mechanism-level novelty screen, not a legal novelty opinion or an exhaustive literature search. I treat `literature.md` as a targeted scan: its one verified close match is **HiHPQ**, DOI `10.1609/aaai.v38i5.28261`, arXiv `2401.07212`, *ABSTRACT_ONLY*. The record establishes hyperbolic product quantisation/codebook attention for unsupervised image retrieval, but not its detailed assignment equations. The scan reports no recommendation-specific discretisation match; that is not evidence of absence. Statements below beyond that record are marked **recollection** or **UNKNOWN**, not presented as verified citations.

The frozen 32-D / three 256-way codebooks / three integer SID tokens, fixed data/splits/recipes, and strict conjunctive ER, Recall@10 and NDCG@10 gates are constraints on feasibility, not novelty. Passing them would not itself distinguish either mechanism from prior art.

## Candidate 1 — Signed geodesic-triangle-area assignment

### Kill attempt / closest work

**Closest verified work in the supplied scan:** HiHPQ (DOI `10.1609/aaai.v38i5.28261`; arXiv `2401.07212`; **ABSTRACT_ONLY**). It already places hyperbolic geometry inside a product-quantisation/codebook mechanism for retrieval. The abstract does not say whether it uses signed simplex area, triangle angle defect, orientation-sensitive assignment, or the proposed host-relative score. Therefore it is strong evidence against claiming the broad idea “hyperbolic geometry for retrieval quantisation” as new, but it does **not** verify occupation of this exact operator.

**Other pipeline uses:** In the proposal, geodesic distance is already used as the locality/tie-breaking term, while the area is the distinctive assignment operator. The residual host, log/exp maps and codeword selection/update are also part of the quantisation path. Reusing familiar distance-based assignment plus an auxiliary differentiable softmin makes the overall construction look like a geometric replacement/augmentation of a codebook metric. An area score is not automatically obvious from a curvature sweep, but triangle area/angle defect is a standard hyperbolic geometric quantity; using it as a ranking score for candidate codewords is a relatively direct transfer once the three points (host, target, code) are present. **Recollection (not verified citation):** oriented hyperbolic triangle area and Gauss–Bonnet angle defect are classical geometry, and area-based geometric scores occur in non-quantisation settings. I have no verified identifier here for an earlier recommender or quantiser using this exact signed assignment.

### Specific technical vulnerabilities

1. **The claimed signed area needs a precise definition in 32-D.** The three points determine a totally geodesic 2-plane and the *unsigned* area of their triangle. An orientation of that plane is needed for a signed area. “An ordered orthonormal 2-frame at `p`” does not by itself orient every plane spanned by arbitrary `Log_p(q), Log_p(z)` unless a concrete convention maps that frame to each plane. Swapping `q,z` reverses the vertex order, but the sign convention must be made coordinate/isometry consistent. The stated isometry invariant is not established by merely naming a frame; reflecting an isometry reverses orientation unless the orientation rule is transformed accordingly. If sign depends on ambient coordinates/frame, the score may be chart-dependent rather than an intrinsic scalar.
2. **The score can be degenerate in the very dimension and geometry proposed.** For each fixed host and target, the triangle area is a scalar orientation/area statistic. It can tie many codewords (especially coplanar/near-collinear or nearly co-directional candidates), and maximizing signed area alone can favor large angular excursions rather than useful residual reconstruction. The distance term then decides ties or dominates in practice. A variance-share criterion is not enough: large area variance need not change argmins or correlate with retrieval utility.
3. **Area is coupled to the same geometry as distance.** Both are functions of the same triangle. Saying the distance is only a regularizer does not make the mechanism distinct if ablation shows assignments are effectively those of the distance term, or if area mostly reweights angular separation equivalent to a metric/feature transformation on the observed support. The required assignment-agreement and zero-area controls are essential, but even changed assignments do not establish novelty.
4. **Optimization/definition ambiguity:** signed area changes sign with orientation and the score uses `-A`; a globally fixed orientation convention is required across batches, stages and isometric transformations. The softmin term must be reconciled with the frozen reconstruction/quantization recipe: it is an added objective, so “unchanged recipes” needs operational detail (weight, schedule, normalization) to be reproducible and not an undeclared recipe change.

### Distinguishability

If implemented intrinsically and shown to alter decisions when the distance component is held fixed/ablated, this is **not merely a curvature sweep**: area is a three-point, orientation-sensitive quantity, unlike changing `c` in a distance quantizer. It is also not a simple metric swap in principle. In practice, however, the proposal has not yet made the signed 32-D area convention sufficiently unambiguous, and it must show assignment changes beyond distance reweighting. It is not a Euclidean reparameterisation if nonzero-curvature area truly controls assignments; the flat-limit test alone does not prove that it does so on actual data.

### Verdict: **ADJACENT**

Closest work: **HiHPQ**, verified only at abstract level, already occupies hyperbolic retrieval product quantisation broadly. Precise difference claimed here: host-relative, signed geodesic triangle area in the hard candidate assignment, rather than the abstract-described hyperbolic product quantizer/codebook attention. That exact difference is **not verified as unoccupied**. Reclassify as **OCCUPIED** if full-text HiHPQ or another primary source shows oriented simplex-area assignment; do not claim “first hyperbolic quantiser for retrieval.”

## Candidate 2 — Non-associative gyro-composition quantization

### Kill attempt / closest work

**Closest verified work in the supplied scan:** HiHPQ, same identifier and **ABSTRACT_ONLY** limitation. Its abstract already establishes hyperbolic product quantisation for retrieval, but does not identify Möbius addition, gyration, operation order, or residual composition. Thus broad domain/mechanism overlap is verified; exact gyro-composition assignment overlap is **UNKNOWN**.

**Other pipeline uses:** The proposed score uses Möbius addition, exp/log maps, parallel transport and gyration *inside assignment*. The proposal also uses log/exp and transport to construct the residual/code representations, and the selected code still passes through the existing residual quantizer decode/update and straight-through reconstruction path. Moving a known hyperbolic operation from representation/decoding into the assignment decision is a real pipeline-location change, but it does not by itself make the application non-obvious: a natural way to compare or compose hyperbolic residuals and codewords is to use the manifold operation in the quantizer. Reuse elsewhere in the same pipeline weakens any assertion that gyration is an independent semantic signal unless the gyration term demonstrably changes selected codes and improves the frozen outcomes.

**Recollection (not verified citation):** Möbius gyrovector arithmetic, gyrations, and their non-associativity are established hyperbolic-geometry machinery, and gyrovector operations have been used in hyperbolic representation learning. I cannot verify from the supplied literature an identifier for their use in a recommender's quantizer or in a retrieval code-assignment score. Hyperbolic residual/vector quantisation is also explicitly listed as a nearby idea in `literature.md`, without a verified source.

### Specific technical vulnerabilities

1. **The score may cancel the claimed order effect.** In exact gyrovector algebra, gyration is the correction relating the two association orders; the first term scores a composed point and the second scores the gyration-induced displacement. The proposal must show these are not redundant functions of the same composition/residual mismatch. Ablating the gyration term and measuring assignment agreement is necessary but insufficient if `G_k` already encodes essentially the same rotation.
2. **Operator/convention ambiguity:** The displayed `gyr[a,b]w = -(a⊕b) ⊕ (a⊕(b⊕w))` needs checking against the chosen left/right gyroassociative convention and the tangent-space type of `w`. Standard gyration is a linear map on the gyrovector space; the expression must specify the relevant tangent/basepoint identification. Likewise, `gyr[p,b] Exp_p(r)` must be type-correct under the selected convention, and subtracting `Exp_p(r)` must use a common vector space and metric. As written, it risks mixing ball points, tangent vectors at `p`, and ambient coordinates.
3. **Transport claims require care.** Parallel transport preserves Riemannian norms, but the separately learned invertible `T_s` need not preserve norms. Applying `T_s` in the origin chart then exponentiating is not equivalent to transporting the original code vector unless the map is defined and constrained appropriately. The formula defines both `v_k` by transport and then appears to use an origin-chart transformed code in the score; clarify which path is operative. The invariant “transport preserves tangent norms” does not validate the learned transform.
4. **Potential curvature/scale confound:** The first score term is a squared norm of a log of a composed point; the second is a squared displacement. Their relative scale depends on curvature, point magnitudes, and `γ`. A nonzero gyration statistic or a `c→0` change does not establish that gyration, rather than a curvature-dependent effective metric/scale or the first term's altered distance, drives the assignment. Report component variance, assignment ablations, and matched-distance controls.
5. **Order semantics are asserted, not supplied.** Three SID stages are sequential, but code/residual order sensitivity is not automatically meaningful behavioral semantics. Unless order is fixed by the existing residual recipe and the learned code meanings remain stable, the operation can act as an arbitrary partition perturbation. Do not infer downstream recommendation value from non-associativity alone.

### Distinguishability

In the ideal mathematical limit, nontrivial gyration is not just a curvature sweep or ordinary metric swap: it encodes the discrepancy between composition orders, and the flat limit removes that discrepancy. But the **full assignment score** can still reduce empirically to a curvature-dependent distance/scale reparameterisation if the gyration component is negligible/redundant or assignments are controlled by `G_k`. The proposal's `c→0` control is necessary, not sufficient. It must additionally compare against matched distance-only and gyration-ablated scores under the fixed curvature and demonstrate meaningful decision changes on actual residual/code pairs.

### Verdict: **ADJACENT**

Closest work: **HiHPQ** (verified abstract-level overlap in hyperbolic retrieval quantisation; exact operator **UNKNOWN**). Precise difference claimed: order-sensitive Möbius composition with an explicit gyration penalty in residual-stage code assignment. The supplied scan does not establish this exact mechanism as occupied, but neither does it support a strong novelty claim: gyrovector geometry and hyperbolic quantisation are each established ingredients, and their placement together at assignment is a plausible, adjacent combination. Reclassify as **OCCUPIED** if a primary source documents gyro/gyration-based quantizer assignment; **ABSTRACT_ONLY/UNKNOWN** remains the honest status until targeted full-text search.

## Overall disposition

Neither candidate earns a **NOVEL** verdict on the present record. Both are **ADJACENT**, with HiHPQ as a verified broad retrieval/quantisation neighbor and the exact operator-level prior art unresolved. Candidate 1 has the more fundamental definition/orientation vulnerability; Candidate 2 has the more severe operator typing, convention and redundancy vulnerabilities. Before spending the frozen evaluation budget, resolve those mathematical definitions, perform targeted full-text searches for (i) signed hyperbolic simplex/triangle-area quantisation and (ii) Möbius gyrocomposition/gyration in vector quantisation or recommendation, then run the proposal's mechanism-specific ablations. Do not treat threshold success, a nonzero curvature effect, or changed assignments alone as evidence of novelty.
