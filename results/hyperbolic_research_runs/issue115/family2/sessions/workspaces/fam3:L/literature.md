# Literature scan: hyperbolic operations at discrete SID choice

## Scope and screening rule

The question is whether published work places a genuinely hyperbolic operation in the rule that chooses a discrete code/token, rather than merely using hyperbolic embeddings elsewhere. “Overlap” below describes the operator/method actually stated in the inspected source, not a claim that a paper has tested this repository's protocol. The frozen protocol and closed-family exclusions are taken as supplied; none is proposed for reopening. Source access labels mean: **FULL_TEXT** = full paper/preprint text was accessible for inspection; **ABSTRACT_ONLY** = only abstract/metadata was inspected; **UNKNOWN** = could not establish the relevant detail.

### Verification notes

- arXiv identifiers, titles, dates and abstracts for HyperVQ and HRQ were checked against the arXiv Atom records; the linked arXiv full-text records are available. HyperVQ is v2; the cited title and identifier are stable across versions.
- HiHPQ DOI/title/year and the abstract wording were checked against DOI-resolved OpenAlex metadata. This was an abstract-level check, not a full-text audit.
- These three are verified evidence of substantial neighboring prior art. This is a targeted scan, not an exhaustive novelty or patentability search. No claim is made that the following ideas are wholly unprecedented; they are candidate distinctions not occupied by the inspected operator descriptions.

## Candidate mechanism ideas not occupied by the inspected descriptions

All candidates below are **ideas only**, not implementation-ready declarations and not claims of expected metric gains. Each would need a pre-registered finite-precision definition and artifact-only occurrence-rate measurement before implementation, plus proof it leaves the frozen dataset/splits/recipes and exactly-three-codebook/three-integer-SID constraints untouched.

1. **Curvature-aware angular-margin choice.** Choose among the existing 256 candidates using a signed margin between the candidate's hyperbolic decision boundary and the item, rather than nearest-distance or MLR probability. This differs in proposed operator from HyperVQ's hyperbolic MLR, although its decision-hyperplane setting is nearby prior art. Before testing: specify the model/curvature, boundary-distance formula and sign convention; finite-precision domain; denominator/norm clamps and epsilons; deterministic tie tolerance and fallback. Measure the fraction of assignment decisions whose winner changes versus the frozen baseline, without training.
2. **Hyperbolic Voronoi solid-angle balancing as a choice penalty.** Add a fixed, precomputed penalty based on each code's hyperbolic Voronoi-cell measure to the point-to-code assignment objective, aiming to discourage pathological occupancy while retaining one code per level. This is not the same as changing the codebook count or adding tokens. Prior art includes hyperbolic codebooks (HyperVQ/HiHPQ), so the proposed distinction is specifically a cell-measure correction at assignment. Define the finite code-domain, measure approximation, clipping, epsilon, comparison tolerance and degenerate-cell behavior; first compute how often the correction can flip an assignment from frozen artifacts.
3. **Geodesic bisector-crossing consistency across the three choices.** For a candidate triplet of codes, evaluate whether the two successive item-to-code geodesic segments cross or approach the corresponding pairwise bisectors in an inconsistent order, and use that as a joint choice regularizer while still emitting exactly three existing integer tokens. This is not the LOTV/tie-break proposal: it must be a primary, non-negligible objective term, not a rare-tie fallback. Specify the finite-precision geodesic/bisector predicates, boundary epsilon, clipping and lexicographic tie behavior; estimate the eligible triplet/decision frequency from the frozen assignment stream before proposing an experiment.
4. **Hyperbolic triangle-defect assignment score.** For an item and candidate code in a level-conditioned context, score the signed defect formed by three hyperbolic distances (item-to-candidate, candidate-to-context anchor, item-to-anchor), so choice uses triangle geometry rather than radius, a level set, a transported residual identity, or a determinant tie-break. This broad concept risks collapsing to a monotone transform or an already-used distance objective; derive and test that it is non-identity/non-monotone in the actual candidate domain before implementation. Declare curvature, all distance/norm clamps, epsilons, comparison tolerance and behavior for coincident/near-coincident points; measure nontrivial score/winner-change rate from artifacts first.

## Decision

The clearest unoccupied *distinction in this small inspected set* is not “hyperbolic quantization” generally: that is already occupied. Any follow-up must identify a specific new assignment operator, demonstrate that it is not algebraically an identity/reparameterization of the frozen objective, state its artifact-computable activation rate, and give its finite-precision contract before coding. Nothing here establishes that any mechanism beats all three frozen thresholds; that requires the unchanged Stage2/Stage3 evaluation.
