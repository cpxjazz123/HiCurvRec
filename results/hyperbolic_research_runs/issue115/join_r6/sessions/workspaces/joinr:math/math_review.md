# Adversarial mathematical review

Scope: proposals in `proposals.md`; line numbers below refer to that file. `literature.md` (lines 7–17) establishes only an abstract-level retrieval/PQ precedent and supplies no mathematical validation for either operator.

## Mechanism 1 — signed geodesic-triangle-area assignment

**Verdict: REJECT as written.** Minimal revision: replace the proposed signed scalar “area” score with a precisely defined oriented 2-form/area in a specified oriented 2-plane, restrict the domain or define a continuous degeneracy rule, and state how orientation is made invariant under isometries. Reassess after fixing those issues.

1. **Well-definedness/domain (11–17).** `q=Exp_p(Log_p(x))=x` for interior points, so the stage target construction itself is fine if all inputs are strictly interior. But a unique geodesic 2-simplex does not exist as a nondegenerate 2-simplex for collinear triples; declaring its area zero handles the scalar value but not the claimed orientation/sign or differentiability there. In dimension 32, the three vertices determine a geodesic triangle in their totally geodesic span when noncollinear; its orientation still requires an oriented plane. The “ordered orthonormal 2-frame at p” does not generally orient every plane spanned by the two log vectors: projecting that fixed frame into a varying plane can vanish or change sign. Swapping q,z reverses the plane orientation convention only if the convention is defined consistently for that plane. Repeated points and p=q or p=z are degenerate and require explicit treatment. A degenerate base (e.g. p at/near the ball boundary) makes the metric factor and Log/Exp numerically singular; the open ball excludes the boundary, but the proposal explicitly expects boundary-near quantizer inputs and gives no margin/domain bound.

2. **Finite/differentiable score (13, 15, 21).** On a compact subset strictly inside the ball, distance squared is finite and smooth, including at coincident points (though implementations of `artanh`/norm need stable limits). The oriented area is not smooth at collinear triples under the stated sign-by-plane construction: the plane normal/sign is undefined there and can jump under arbitrarily small perturbations. The angle formula is also numerically ill-conditioned near degeneracy; angle derivatives can diverge. At the base itself, `Log_p(p)=0` is mathematically defined by continuity, but formulas dividing by `||p⊕(-q)||` or tangent norms need that explicit limit. The straight-through path does not cure singular derivatives in the soft assignment; gradients through the area term can be undefined at collinearity/repeated vertices. The `c>0` ball boundary is excluded, but as `c||x||²→1`, conformal factor and distance/logarithm become unbounded, so finite gradients are not guaranteed.

3. **Invariants (23).** (i) Collinear area has zero magnitude, but signed area reversal under swapping q,z is not guaranteed by the fixed-frame prescription when the plane orientation is ambiguous; near a collinear triple a tiny perturbation can flip sign. (ii) Gauss–Bonnet gives the *unsigned* area from angle defect for a geodesic triangle; an oriented area requires multiplying by a separately defined orientation sign. It cannot validate the sign convention as written. (iii) Hyperbolic isometry preserves unsigned area, but orientation-reversing isometries reverse oriented area. Thus the score is not invariant under “all common isometries” if orientation is transformed consistently (that instead preserves a chosen oriented quantity only for orientation-preserving maps / transformed frame). The statement conflates invariant scalar area with orientation-sensitive area. (iv) In the flat limit the area tends to zero for fixed bounded configurations, so the remaining score is `β d²`, ordinary nearest-neighbour distance assignment. This may be a harmless control only if the actual Stage-2 quantizer control is that same distance assignment; not established here. (v) Count is nominally unchanged.

4. **Flat limit/control (17, 23–25, 50).** The Gauss–Bonnet angle defect is order `c` for a fixed Euclidean-scale triangle, and division by c yields a finite Euclidean triangle area, not zero. The *curvature integral* `K·area = -c·area` vanishes as c→0; the geometric area itself approaches Euclidean area. Therefore the explicit claim “area vanish” is false if `A_c` means area as defined by the angle-defect formula. If instead it means integrated curvature, it is not area and the formula/sign in line 17 is wrong by a factor/sign. This is a central control failure, not a wording issue.

5. **Capacity/refuted forms.** Three 256-way choices and three tokens are retained (11, 19, 23). However, this is not inherently a novel geometric partition if the area contribution is negligible or if the chosen oriented area is replaced by a local tangent-space wedge/determinant; the latter risks a transported-residual-frame reformulation (one of the already refuted families). The distance term can dominate and reduce it to an ordinary distance quantizer; the proposed variance check is necessary but insufficient to establish non-equivalence.

## Mechanism 2 — non-associative gyro-composition quantization

**Verdict: REJECT as written.** Minimal revision: provide one internally consistent convention for tangent vectors, transport, Möbius gyration and the score; define the score in a single tangent space (with units), and use a composition whose non-associativity actually affects the selected-code term rather than a potentially identically-zero gyration comparison.

1. **Well-definedness/domain (29–38).** The Möbius addition formula is defined for points strictly inside the ball; its denominator is positive there. But line 33 defines `u_k` using parallel transport, then line 35 ignores `u_k` and instead uses `Exp_o(T_s Log_o(z))` directly in a host-based composition. `T_s` is said to act on residual vectors without specifying which tangent space it maps from/to; “invertible” alone does not make it a metric- or radius-preserving map. Most importantly, `gyr[p,b]` is a linear map on the tangent space at the origin in standard gyrovector notation, whereas `Exp_p(r)` is a point in the ball, not a tangent vector. Applying gyration to it is type-invalid under the stated definition. The displayed gyration definition is a point-valued associator expression unless the argument `w` and output convention are carefully typed; it is not by itself an implementation specification. The score then subtracts `Exp_p(r)` from the gyrated result and measures that Euclidean coordinate difference with `g_p`, despite those objects not being tangent vectors at p. Collinear/repeated inputs do not fix this type mismatch. Degenerate host near boundary also makes `Log_p`, `Exp_p`, and metric scaling ill-conditioned.

2. **Finite/differentiable score (35–42).** For interior points, Log/Exp and Möbius operations are smooth away from implementation singularities; standard limits define Log at its base and gyration at zero. But the formula as written is not a well-typed Riemannian scalar, so its claimed finite/differentiable gradient is not established. If implemented literally with denominators based on `1-c||p||²`, boundary-near hosts produce arbitrarily large factors. Invertibility of learned `T_s` does not bound its operator norm, so code points can be pushed arbitrarily close to the boundary. Softmin cannot repair these singularities. `γ` may be fixed positive, but no boundedness/conditioning constraint is imposed on transport.

3. **Invariants (44).** (i) Identity is correct for Möbius addition on the ball. (ii) The flat limit of addition is vector addition and gyration becomes identity, assuming the inputs and scaling remain bounded as c→0. (iii) Gyrations are isometries in the appropriate gyrovector/tangent metric, but “at the appropriate tangent space” does not establish that the line-36 difference is measured in that metric; as written its operands are points. (iv) Non-collinear inputs generically produce nontrivial gyration, but not for every non-collinear triple (e.g. special symmetric configurations); the test must say generic and quantify over actual samples. Moreover in the formula, `gyr[p,b] Exp_p(r) - Exp_p(r)` is not a valid tangent displacement. (v) Parallel transport preserves Riemannian norms, but line 35 bypasses the transported `v_k`; and the learned `T_s` can change norms, so the asserted transport invariant does not apply to the effective code semantic. (vi) Möbius sums of interior points remain interior, but unconstrained `T_s` can map `Log_o(z)` to arbitrarily large tangent vectors; Exp maps finite vectors to interior, with boundary saturation, while numerical clamps may be material. Thus strictly interior mathematically is possible, but finite margin is not guaranteed. (vii) nominal code/token counts are unchanged.

4. **Flat limit/control (44–46, 52).** With bounded coordinates and a corrected formula, gyration vanishes and the first term becomes the norm of ordinary composed vectors. That is not automatically the frozen Euclidean residual quantizer: it includes code-plus-residual composition and a potentially learned `T_s`, and minimizing `||code + residual||²` can favor the opposite code direction rather than nearest residual reconstruction. The claimed control “reproduces assignments” is therefore not implied. Line 52 correctly notes associativity in the flat limit, but the proposal has not shown that its score's Euclidean limit is the existing control or that the gyration term changes assignments.

5. **Capacity/refuted forms.** The 256×3 code capacity is retained (42, 44). Nonetheless, line 33's transported tangent semantic is exactly vulnerable to the already-refuted transported-residual-frame family; line 35 then changes the construction to an origin-chart Möbius composition, making the proposal internally inconsistent about which mechanism is tested. A learned invertible `T_s` can also absorb arbitrary linear reparameterizations, obscuring whether gains come from curvature or a learned codebook transform. The gyration term is not proof against a Euclidean reformulation until it is correctly typed and shown to be non-negligible in the actual assignment score.

## Cross-candidate decision

Neither candidate is ready for a join. Mechanism 1 has a false flat-limit claim and an unresolved orientation/differentiability definition; Mechanism 2 has type/domain inconsistencies in its central score and an unspecified Euclidean control. These are mathematical blockers independent of whether the strict ER, Recall@10 and NDCG@10 thresholds are eventually met. Frozen dimensions, codebook sizes, token count and recipes should remain untouched in any revision.
