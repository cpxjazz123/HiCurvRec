# Adversarial prior-art review

## Scope and standard

This review treats `proposals.md` as two proposed **assignment operators**, not as claims that any geometry-aware quantizer is novel. The local scan in `literature.md` is explicitly targeted rather than exhaustive. Its only verified primary-source match is HiHPQ (arXiv:2401.07212; DOI:10.1609/aaai.v38i5.28261), and that match is **ABSTRACT_ONLY**. I do not claim a comprehensive search or verified novelty. Statements below marked **recollection** are not citation-checked.

The frozen 32-dimensional latent, three 256-entry codebooks, three integer SID tokens, data/splits/recipes and strict conjunctive ER / Recall@10 / NDCG@10 thresholds are constraints, not novelty evidence. Neither mechanism gets credit for those constraints or for merely beating a baseline. The five already-refuted families in the request are excluded from consideration.

## Candidate 1 — Signed geodesic-triangle-area assignment

**Prior art status.** `literature.md` verifies HiHPQ, *Hierarchical Hyperbolic Product Quantization for Unsupervised Image Retrieval* (2024), arXiv:2401.07212 / DOI:10.1609/aaai.v38i5.28261, **ABSTRACT_ONLY**. The abstract establishes hyperbolic product quantization, codebook attention and quantized contrastive learning for image retrieval; it does **not** establish signed triangle area as its assignment score. It is the closest verified quantization/retrieval work in the supplied scan, but the precise proposed operator is not shown there. Whether signed hyperbolic simplex area has appeared in another quantizer or recommender is **UNKNOWN** on this evidence. **Recollection, unverified:** hyperbolic triangle area and angle-defect/Gauss–Bonnet quantities are standard tools in hyperbolic geometry and appear in geometric representation/learning contexts; that does not establish their use as a discrete code-assignment objective.

**Pipeline-location test.** The proposal uses area in the **assignment** itself, while the ordinary reconstruction/quantization losses and residual decode/update remain frozen. That is a meaningful location relative to work using hyperbolic geometry only in an encoder or ranking loss. But moving a known geometric quantity from an auxiliary loss or representation to the assignment argmin is not, by itself, a strong conceptual distinction: nearest-code assignment is already an optimization over codewords, and replacing its score with another invariant is a direct, fairly obvious operator substitution once area is chosen. The local HiHPQ abstract already occupies the broader “hyperbolic operation inside quantization/codebook” territory; it does not settle this exact score.

**Is it more than a curvature sweep, metric swap, or reparameterization?** In principle, yes: a signed three-point simplex area is not a radial distance, and a nonzero area term can change assignments through orientation and the joint host/target/codeword configuration. It is not just changing curvature if fixed nonzero curvature is used and the area contribution changes decisions. The proposal's own controls (area ablation, flat limit, score-variance share and assignment agreement) are appropriate necessary checks, not proof of novelty. A major definition problem weakens the claim: in 32 dimensions there is no canonical signed orientation for every triangle plane from one ordered 2-frame at `p`. A 2-frame orients one plane, not all planes spanned by arbitrary pairs of tangent vectors. Under general isometries/reflections the sign convention cannot simultaneously be both globally canonical and invariant as claimed without extra structure. Define an explicit orientation field/structure and test its symmetry consequences, or the claimed invariant signed operator is under-specified. The unsigned area remains a possible operator, but is not this signed proposal.

**Kill tests / decision.** First reject if the orientation is not well-defined and reproducible, or if the area ablation leaves assignments unchanged / its contribution is negligible. Even if those tests pass, compare against distance-only, unsigned-area, and a simple tangent-space wedge/angle control; otherwise gains may be attributable to a generic directional tie-break rather than specifically curved area. Require all three frozen thresholds; an ER-only gain is failure.

**Verdict: ADJACENT.** Closest verified work: HiHPQ (ABSTRACT_ONLY), which occupies hyperbolic product quantization but has no verified triangle-area assignment. The exact signed-area argmin is not established as occupied by the supplied literature, yet its use is an adjacent geometric scoring substitution, with a substantive orientation-definition gap. Do not call it NOVEL absent a broader full-text search and a precise orientation definition; do not call it OCCUPIED based only on HiHPQ's abstract.

## Candidate 2 — Non-associative gyro-composition quantization

**Prior art status.** The same verified closest work is HiHPQ, arXiv:2401.07212 / DOI:10.1609/aaai.v38i5.28261, **ABSTRACT_ONLY**. It proves that hyperbolic product quantization and codebook operations are already present in retrieval, but its abstract does not mention Möbius gyration, order-sensitive residual composition, or this score. Exact prior art for this assignment rule in recommendation/quantization is **UNKNOWN** from `literature.md`. **Recollection, unverified:** Möbius addition, gyration and parallel transport are standard hyperbolic/gyrovector operations and have been used in hyperbolic representation-learning models. That makes the ingredients established mathematics, not a new geometric primitive; it does not verify a quantizer using this particular composition penalty.

**Pipeline-location test.** The proposal places composition and a gyration-dependent term at the **assignment** decision; it also uses `Log`, `Exp` and transport to construct representations/targets and sends gradients through them during training. Those latter operations are already familiar representation/encoding machinery. Their presence in the encoder or loss does not make order-sensitive code assignment identical, but it makes the design a composition of known operators at a new pipeline site rather than a new primitive. Given a residual quantizer, evaluating code-plus-residual in both orders and using their discrepancy to choose a code is a direct and foreseeable extension of residual composition. HiHPQ makes the broad quantization context adjacent, not the exact operation verified.

**Is it more than a curvature sweep, metric swap, or reparameterization?** The intended gyration effect is genuinely curvature-dependent and, if nonzero on data and assignment-changing, cannot be reproduced by simply rescaling curvature or swapping a distance metric. However, as written the score does not securely demonstrate that distinction. `G_k` is the logarithm at `p` of a composition that includes the candidate code and residual; its norm is already an order-sensitive composition residual. The added gyration penalty may be redundant with that first term, or may merely reweight a geometry-induced discrepancy. The decisive ablations are: first-term-only, gyration-term-only, reversed composition order, and flat limit, with per-example assignment changes and score correlations. If the gyration term is functionally redundant, this is a metric/score reparameterization, not evidence for a distinct mechanism.

There are also mathematical/implementation issues that must be fixed before interpreting results. The definition of `gyr[a,b]w` is written using Möbius addition but the operands and tangent spaces need to follow one consistent gyrovector convention. The proposal defines `v_k` by parallel-transporting a code tangent to `p`, then defines `u_k`, but the score instead applies `Exp_o(T_s Log_o(z_k))`; the transported `v_k`/`u_k` are unused. The second score term subtracts `Exp_p(r)` (a point in the ball) from a gyrated `Exp_p(r)` while declaring a tangent norm at `p`; point subtraction is not a tangent vector. Use the gyration's tangent action on `r` in `T_p` (or explicitly log both points into the same tangent space) and define the metric/norm there. The notation for `T_s` also needs a declared domain/codomain and a construction ensuring its output remains a valid tangent vector. These are not cosmetic: as written the score is not an unambiguous Riemannian scalar.

**Kill tests / decision.** Correct the score before running. Verify gyration's metric preservation and nonzero magnitude on actual pairs, test all ablations/order reversals, and quantify assignment changes beyond the first composition residual. Reject for near-identity gyration, redundancy, clamp/boundary problems, or failure of any strict frozen metric. A benefit from a generic learned transform `T_s` must not be attributed to gyro non-associativity; include a matched control with `T_s` but identity gyration.

**Verdict: ADJACENT.** Closest verified work: HiHPQ (ABSTRACT_ONLY), for hyperbolic quantization in retrieval; standard gyrovector operations are recalled as established representation geometry, but no precise matching assignment paper was verified. This proposal may be distinct from a curvature sweep if the corrected gyration term demonstrably changes assignments, but its ingredients and residual-composition rationale are adjacent, and the current formula has unresolved type/consistency errors. It is not supportable as NOVEL yet, nor is exact occupation established by the supplied scan.

## Overall disposition

Neither candidate merits approval as novel on the present record. Candidate 1 is an adjacent assignment-score construction with an unresolved global orientation definition. Candidate 2 is an adjacent composition-based construction whose score needs mathematical repair and a redundancy audit. The targeted literature scan is insufficient to upgrade either to NOVEL or to declare the exact operators OCCUPIED. Before spending a frozen-protocol run, establish well-defined operators and do a full-text search around hyperbolic product quantization, gyrovector quantization, and geometric/area-based code assignment. Any eventual run must preserve the frozen setup and pass all three strict thresholds; passing those thresholds would establish empirical utility under this protocol, not prior-art novelty.
