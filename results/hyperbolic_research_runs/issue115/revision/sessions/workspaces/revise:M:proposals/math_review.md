## Mechanism 1 — Signed geodesic-triangle-area assignment

### Domain, well-definedness, and regularity

- **Lines 11–15:** For `α=1`, `q=Exp_p(Log_p(x))=x` wherever `Log_p(x)` is defined. In the open Poincaré ball this is mathematically defined for every interior `p,x`; it is not defined at the ideal boundary. The stated “clamp only for numerical safety” does not define what happens to exact/out-of-domain boundary inputs, and near-boundary distances/logs are increasingly ill-conditioned. A real quantizer must state its admissible domain and clamp/projection rule.
- **Lines 15, 17:** Three vertices in `H^32` determine a totally geodesic 2-simplex when noncollinear, but they do **not** inherit a canonical signed orientation merely from an ordered 2-frame at `p`. A 2-frame supplies an orientation only to its own 2-plane. To sign the area of an arbitrary plane spanned by `Log_p(q), Log_p(z)`, one needs an explicit orientation rule on that plane (for example, a chosen ambient orientation plus an oriented bivector). The signed projection onto the declared frame can be zero for a nonzero triangle whose plane is orthogonal to that frame; it is not the triangle's signed area. As stated, `A_c` is underdefined in dimension 32.
- **Lines 15, 23(i):** Repeated points and collinear triples have zero unsigned area. But the *signed* area near collinearity depends on the orientation convention. With the natural determinant/bivector convention it changes sign across the collinear locus and is generally nondifferentiable there (locally it behaves like a signed determinant times a nonzero factor, or like `sign(det)*area` with an absolute-value cusp, depending on the convention). The proposal explicitly includes degenerate triples but gives no smooth extension or gradient rule. Thus the claimed soft-assignment gradient is not guaranteed to exist for all candidates/items; in particular `q=p`, `z=p`, repeated codewords, and collinear triples are ordinary possible training states.
- **Lines 13, 21:** `d_c(q,z)^2` is smooth at coincident interior points when implemented as squared Riemannian distance, but formulas using `artanh(||(-q)⊕z||)` need a zero-norm limiting branch. The area term's angle formula has angle-coordinate singularities at degenerate triangles even though unsigned area tends to zero. The score is therefore not globally differentiable on the stated domain. The softmin does not smooth a nonsmooth `S_k`.

### Claimed invariants and counterexamples

- **Line 23(i), swapping:** Swapping `q,z` reverses orientation only after an orientation of the triangle's plane has been coherently defined. A fixed ambient frame's projected determinant can vanish for a noncollinear triangle, so the current definition does not establish the claimed sign reversal for the full geometric area.
- **Line 23(iii), common isometry:** The *unsigned* area and distances are invariant under hyperbolic isometries. A **signed** area is preserved only by orientation-preserving isometries, and changes sign under orientation-reversing isometries if orientation is transported. The clause “with orientation transformed consistently” makes the assertion conditional, not invariance under a fixed frame. Counterexample: reflect the whole configuration in an orientation-reversing hyperbolic isometry while retaining the original ambient frame; area sign reverses and hence `S` changes. The proposal must narrow the invariant to orientation-preserving isometries or transform the orientation data as part of the input.
- **Line 23(ii):** Gauss–Bonnet is valid for a nondegenerate geodesic triangle, with area `(π−sum angles)/c`; it does not by itself fix the signed orientation or supply stable/differentiable angles at degeneracy. “Within numerical tolerance” is not a definition for the degenerate cases.
- **Lines 17, 23(iv), 25:** In the flat limit the area tends to zero for a fixed bounded configuration, but this does not imply the *assignment* depends on curvature. If the distance term has a unique nearest code, then for sufficiently small `c` the same code remains selected by continuity, and replacing area by zero leaves assignment unchanged. The proposed variance condition can be met by scaling `β` small while area is still too small to alter any argmin. That is a concrete failure mode already acknowledged by the assignment-agreement falsifier; it means the stated curvature-limit invariant is only a term-level statement, not a decision-level one.
- **Line 23(i), “zero for collinear”:** A zero area does not imply a smooth zero-gradient extension. Perturb one vertex to either side of a collinear configuration: the signed area has opposite signs, so a conventional signed-area implementation has a cusp or branch transition there.

### Flat limit, capacity, and refuted-form risk

- **Lines 13, 23(iv), 50:** For fixed vertices, area is `O(c)` as `c→0` (angle defect vanishes at the same rate as `c`), so its flat limit is zero and the score tends to `β||q−z||²`. This is a harmless Euclidean nearest-code control **only if** `β` remains finite and fixed. If `β` is re-tuned with `c` (not allowed by the declared fixed protocol), or area is rescaled by `1/c`, the limit need not vanish. State the fixed-parameter convention in the control.
- **Lines 17, 25:** Requiring area to contribute half the *variance* is not a robust contribution criterion: variance is sensitive to units, outliers, and `β`; it does not establish a meaningful argmin effect. Report score components and per-stage assignment agreement as already requested, but do not claim the variance test alone validates a curvature-driven assignment.
- **Lines 19, 23(v):** The operator chooses one of 256 entries at each of three stages, so it does not overtly change code capacity or token count. It is nevertheless not fully specified whether codewords `z` are ball points while the existing residual decoder/update expects Euclidean vectors; a map between the two is needed to rule out an implicit representation/capacity change.

**Minimal disposition:** Keep only after providing a coordinate-free signed-area definition valid in arbitrary dimension, a declared orientation policy, a differentiable or explicitly subgradient-safe treatment of degeneracies, and corrected isometry claims. Test exact repeated/collinear cases and near-boundary points, not only generic triangles.

## Mechanism 2 — Non-associative gyro-composition quantization

### Domain, typing, and well-definedness

- **Lines 31–32:** The displayed Möbius addition formula is defined for points strictly inside the ball; its denominator is positive there. The open ball is closed under Möbius addition, so the composition itself stays interior in exact arithmetic. The proposal nevertheless asks for all inputs while providing no policy for boundary points, where the denominator/domain assumptions fail, or for finite-precision overflow near the boundary.
- **Lines 33–35:** `v_k` is defined by parallel-transporting `Log_o(z_k)` to `T_p`, and `u_k=Exp_p(v_k)` is then introduced, but **neither `v_k` nor `u_k` is used in the score**. Instead line 35 applies `T_s` at the origin and exponentiates there. This is a different operator from the stated transported-code construction. `T_s` is said to be an invertible tangent transport, but an arbitrary invertible linear map is not a parallel transport and need not preserve norms. The claims of transported semantics and invariant (v) do not follow.
- **Lines 35–36:** `Log_p(...)` returns a tangent vector at `p`, so the first norm in `S_k` is meaningful under `g_p`. But `Exp_p(r)` is a **point in the ball**, not a tangent vector. `gyr[p, Exp_o(...)] Exp_p(r)` is consequently also a point (under the displayed gyrovector convention), and subtracting these two ball coordinates and measuring their difference with `g_p` is not a Riemannian tangent norm. The second score component has no stated mathematical type/meaning. This is fatal to the score as written.
- **Lines 36, 38, 44(iii):** Standard Möbius gyration acts as an isometry in the gyrovector model, but the formula's `w` must be interpreted in the gyrovector/vector convention appropriate to that model. The proposal feeds it `Exp_p(r)`, a point based at `p`, then compares coordinate points using a tangent metric at `p`. “Metric-preserving at the appropriate tangent space” does not specify the missing identifications. A valid alternative would apply a properly defined linear tangent-space gyration to a vector and compare vectors in the same tangent space.
- **Lines 33, 40:** Parallel transport `PT_{o→p}` is path-dependent in curved geometry; on the simply connected constant-curvature ball a path must still be specified (usually the unique radial geodesic). The score bypasses it, so the semantic narrative cannot justify the actual score.
- **Lines 35–36:** With interior inputs, the first composition and its `Log_p` are defined. At `p=x`, `r=Log_p(x)=0` is defined by continuity, but implementations must handle the zero-vector limit. `Log_p` becomes singular at the ideal boundary. Gyration/addition are smooth in the open ball, but not at the excluded boundary.

### Gradient and claimed invariants

- **Lines 36, 42:** If repaired to use smooth interior operations, the first component can be differentiable, except for usual zero-norm implementation issues (the squared norm itself is smooth). The *current* second component is an ill-typed coordinate difference, so no claim of a geometrically meaningful gradient follows. Hard `argmin` is nondifferentiable at ties; the softmin relaxation gives gradients only to a defined smooth score and does not fix this.
- **Line 44(i):** The stated identity `u⊕0=0⊕u=u` is correct for interior `u` and origin `0`; it does not validate arbitrary boundary inputs.
- **Line 44(ii), 46:** As `c→0`, the first component becomes `||T_s Log_o(z_k)+r||²` in the flat chart (with the specified `Exp/Log` limits), while the gyration correction tends to zero. This is an ordinary Euclidean **composition/residual comparison**, not necessarily the original quantizer's control. It can still change assignments substantially: choose two code vectors on opposite sides of `−r`; the nearest code under `||v_k+r||²` need not equal the baseline nearest-code assignment. Thus “gyration tends identity” does not imply “c→0 reproduces assignments,” contrary to the falsifier at line 46. The flat control must compare the full operator against a predeclared Euclidean counterpart, not demand baseline agreement.
- **Line 44(iii):** Gyration is an isometry in the gyrovector metric under the proper action, but this is not the same as preserving the Poincaré tangent metric `g_p` on a point `Exp_p(r)`. No such invariant is established for the expressions currently written.
- **Line 44(iv):** Non-associativity is genuine for generic non-collinear gyrovector inputs, but “numerically verify” is not an invariant proof. Collinear inputs, zero inputs, or special symmetric configurations associate; those are not counterexamples to the word “generic,” but they show the test must not require a difference for every pair.
- **Line 44(v):** Parallel transport preserves Riemannian tangent norms when used as actual Levi-Civita parallel transport along a specified path. The learned `T_s` is only constrained to be invertible, which does **not** preserve norms: e.g. `T_s=2I` is invertible and doubles norms. Also the score does not use the declared `PT` result. The invariant is false as written.
- **Line 44(vi):** Exact interior Möbius operations preserve the ball, but an unconstrained learned `T_s` followed by `Exp_o` remains interior in exact arithmetic; numerical clamping can still materially change the operator. The claimed invariant needs a numerical tolerance and boundary policy.

### Flat limit, capacity, and refuted-form risk

- **Lines 35–38, 52:** The first score's flat limit is a Euclidean residual-composition score. The second term, even if repaired, vanishes as gyration tends to identity. Therefore the claimed genuinely hyperbolic contribution must be demonstrated in the *actual argmin*, not inferred from use of Möbius syntax. As currently defined, the purported gyration contribution is not mathematically valid.
- **Lines 33–35:** Since `T_s` can be any learned invertible map and its norm is unconstrained, it can absorb anisotropic rescaling/rotation into code semantics. This is hidden reparameterization freedom, not a curvature invariant; it does not alter the nominal 256×3 token capacity, but can make the comparison confounded. If “transport” is intended, constrain it to the appropriate isometry/parallel transport and specify whether it is learned or fixed.
- **Lines 33, 40:** `u_k` being unused means the mechanism actually scored is not the one described in the semantics. This is not merely notation: replacing the origin-exponential composition with host-tangent composition changes the operator and its flat control. Resolve before implementation or evaluation.

**Minimal disposition:** Reject this version. A revision needs a single consistent convention for points versus tangent vectors; a path-defined transport; a correctly typed gyration action on a vector in a declared tangent space; an explicit admissible open-ball domain/numerical policy; and a flat-control definition that compares with its true Euclidean limit. Then re-review the revised equations before running the frozen experiment.
