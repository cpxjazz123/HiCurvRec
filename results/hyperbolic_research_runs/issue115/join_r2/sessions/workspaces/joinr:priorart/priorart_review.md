# Adversarial prior-art and novelty review

## Scope and evidence standard

The review treats `proposals.md` as the two candidate designs and `literature.md` as a bounded, targeted scan—not as an exhaustive search. The only verified paper-level match in that scan is HiHPQ (DOI `10.1609/aaai.v38i5.28261`, arXiv:`2401.07212`), whose status is explicitly **ABSTRACT_ONLY**. Its abstract establishes hyperbolic product quantization, hyperbolic codebook attention, and quantized contrastive learning for image retrieval; it does not disclose enough detail to establish overlap with either proposed operator. No recommendation-specific discretization match was verified in the scan. Statements below about classical geometry are recollection, not a newly verified paper search.

These are technical novelty assessments, not claims of legal novelty or an exhaustive prior-art opinion. Neither candidate has experimental evidence here, and neither should be approved without the frozen-protocol tests and mechanism-specific falsifiers.

## Candidate 1 — Signed geodesic-triangle-area assignment

### Kill attempt

**Closest verified work:** HiHPQ, DOI `10.1609/aaai.v38i5.28261` / arXiv:`2401.07212` (**ABSTRACT_ONLY**). It already places hyperbolic structure in product quantization/codebook attention for retrieval. This occupies the broad territory “hyperbolic geometry participates in retrieval quantization”; it does **not**, on the available abstract, establish a signed geodesic triangle-area code-assignment score, a residual-host triangle, or signed-area-driven SID selection. No specific recommender paper with that operator is verified in `literature.md`.

**Other pipeline locations / obviousness:** Hyperbolic distances and geodesic constructions are familiar representation-learning and metric-learning ingredients (recollection; no specific paper verified here). They are commonly used to encode or rank representations, but that does not by itself disclose using the *oriented curvature integral over (host, target, codeword)* as the assignment criterion. Conversely, once a design already has a hyperbolic host, residual ray, and candidate rays, testing a geodesic-simplex quantity as a directional compatibility score is a fairly direct geometric extension. The proposal's area-plus-distance objective is therefore not made non-obvious merely by moving a familiar geometry operator from representation/ranking into assignment. The relevant distinction is whether signed area actually controls the chosen code, not whether the implementation computes an area somewhere in the loss.

**Is it more than a curvature sweep, metric swap, or reparameterization?** In principle, yes. A signed, three-point, orientation-sensitive area term is not a rescaled distance or a change of curvature parameter; it can distinguish configurations that have the same pairwise radii/distances but opposite orientation. If implemented and shown to materially change assignments, that is a real operator-level distinction. But the proposal has a serious specification/falsifiability issue: in dimensions above two, the triangle lies in a 2-plane, but “orientation fixed once by an ordered orthonormal 2-frame at `p`” does not give a globally consistent sign for arbitrary candidate planes in 32 dimensions. A fixed 2-frame only orients its own plane; for other planes, a rule (for example, a specified bivector/pseudoscalar pairing and its degeneracy behavior) is needed. Also, the claimed common-isometry invariance requires carrying the orientation convention with the isometry; it is not invariance under arbitrary reflections with a fixed external orientation. Until that is defined, “signed area” may be ambiguous, discontinuous, or effectively unsigned in much of the latent space.

The Gauss–Bonnet angle-defect formula is standard hyperbolic geometry (recollection), not by itself a new quantization contribution. At fixed curvature, triangle area is a deterministic function of the triangle's angles; the proposal must show it produces partitions not reducible to an angle/distance heuristic or a changed embedding geometry. Its own required ablation—zeroing the area term and measuring assignment agreement—is essential. The variance condition alone is insufficient: a numerically large area contribution can still be redundant with distance or fail to change the argmin.

**Novelty verdict: ADJACENT.** Closest work is HiHPQ (verified identifier, **ABSTRACT_ONLY**) for hyperbolic quantization in retrieval. The exact signed host-relative triangle-area assignment is not established by that source, but it is an adjacent geometric scoring construction built on standard hyperbolic geometry, not a clearly isolated new hyperbolic quantization principle. No verified recommendation-specific precedent was found; absence in this targeted scan is not evidence of absence. To strengthen a novelty claim, define a valid high-dimensional orientation/sign rule and demonstrate nonredundant, material assignment changes against distance-only, unsigned-area, and flat-limit controls.

## Candidate 2 — Non-associative gyro-composition quantization

### Kill attempt

**Closest verified work:** Again, HiHPQ, DOI `10.1609/aaai.v38i5.28261` / arXiv:`2401.07212` (**ABSTRACT_ONLY**), because it already uses hyperbolic product quantization/codebook machinery in retrieval. Its abstract does not say that Möbius addition, gyration, or order-sensitive residual composition determines assignments. `literature.md` does not verify a recommender or quantizer using this exact operator. Möbius gyrovector addition and gyration are established hyperbolic-geometry machinery (recollection; no particular source checked here), so the primitive itself should not be represented as novel.

**Other pipeline locations / obviousness:** Möbius operations and hyperbolic distances appear in hyperbolic representation maps, aggregation/composition, and potentially losses/ranking (general recollection, not verified source-level claims). Moving such an operation from representation construction or a geometry-aware loss into the quantizer's candidate score is a pipeline-location change, not automatically a new operator. Given residual quantization, testing composition order and its gyration correction is a natural way to make curvature affect code selection. The potentially distinguishing feature is the *assignment rule's explicit dependence on order-induced gyration*, with a measured ablation showing that it—not ordinary distance after a coordinate transform—changes the selected code.

**Is it more than a curvature sweep, metric swap, or reparameterization?** The intended mechanism could be: Möbius addition is non-associative, gyration captures the resulting rotation, and the score depends on composition order. That is conceptually distinct from a curvature sweep or replacing Euclidean distance with hyperbolic distance, provided the gyration term is non-negligible and affects argmins. However, as written, the score is vulnerable to collapsing into a reparameterized distance comparison: `G_k` is the log of a composed point, and the added gyration discrepancy can be small, redundant, or act like a smooth correction that does not alter assignments. The proposal itself identifies the right falsifiers (gyration magnitude, remove-gyration assignment agreement, and `c→0` assignment agreement); these must be reported, not assumed from the equations.

There is also a formulation concern: `gyr[p, Exp_o(T_s Log_o(z_k))]` is applied to `Exp_p(r)`, while the proposal separately defines a transported host-tangent code vector and `u_k`; the score instead constructs the code point through an origin exponential and composes it with `p`. The tangent spaces and gyrovector convention for this gyration action must be made explicit and checked against the implementation. The stated “transport preserves tangent norms” invariant does not validate the score's use of transport or the basepoint conventions. A convention mismatch could make the claimed non-associative effect an artifact, rather than a well-defined intrinsic assignment mechanism.

**Novelty verdict: ADJACENT.** Closest work is HiHPQ (verified identifier, **ABSTRACT_ONLY**) at the level of hyperbolic quantization for retrieval; classical gyrovector addition/gyration is an established geometric toolkit (recollection, not a verified paper citation). No inspected source establishes this exact order-sensitive SID assignment, but the proposal is a direct application of known hyperbolic composition machinery to residual code selection. It is more distinguishable from metric swapping than a curvature-only variant *if* the order/gyration term demonstrably changes assignments in the curved setting and vanishes in the flat control. Otherwise it is only a reparameterized distance quantizer. No recommendation-specific prior art was verified by the bounded scan.
