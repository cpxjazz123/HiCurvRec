# Adversarial mathematical review

Scope: this review tests the operators as written in `proposals.md`; it does not infer implementation conventions that are not specified there. `literature.md` (especially lines 5, 11, and 16–18) supports the general relevance of hyperbolic quantisation to retrieval, but is abstract-level evidence for image retrieval only. It provides no mathematical validation of either proposed operator or evidence for recommendation gains.

## Mechanism 1 — signed geodesic-triangle-area assignment

**Verdict: REVISE (not approvable as specified).** The core area-plus-distance idea is mathematically meaningful in a two-dimensional oriented hyperbolic plane, but the signed area operator and its claimed gradient behavior are not fully defined in the stated 32-dimensional domain.

1. **Well-definedness and domain (proposal lines 11–15).** For strict interior points `p,x,z`, the Poincaré ball is a Hadamard manifold: the geodesic segments and the geodesic triangle as an *unoriented* simplex are unique. With `α=1`, `Exp_p(Log_p(x))=x`, so `q=x` (lines 11, 19). The distance is defined for these points. The problem is the *signed* area in dimension 32: a 2-plane has two orientations, and a fixed ordered 2-frame at `p` does not canonically orient every plane spanned by `Log_p(q),Log_p(z)`. Projecting the frame or choosing a complement would be extra data and can become singular when the projection loses rank. “Orientation fixed once” therefore does not define a scalar sign for all candidate triples as written (line 15).

   Counterexamples/edge cases: if `q=p` or `z=p`, one spanning vector is zero and the plane/sign is undefined; the area magnitude is zero, but a signed orientation rule still needs a convention. If `p,q,z` are collinear (including repeated points), the area is zero by the stated convention, but the oriented sign on either side of the collinear locus flips. Points arbitrarily close to the ball boundary remain valid only while strictly interior; exact boundary points are excluded, and “clamp only for numerical safety” (line 7) does not specify how the operator is extended to them. A degenerate base `p` is not a geometric exception in the Poincaré ball, but if “degenerate” means a host coincident with target/code, it produces the zero-vector cases above.

2. **Finiteness and differentiability (lines 13, 15, 21).** For strict interior inputs, `d_c` is finite; its square is smooth, including at coincidence. The area magnitude is finite for finite `c`, bounded by the hyperbolic triangle angle-defect bound. But signed area is not differentiable across the collinear locus under the natural `sign × area-magnitude` definition: the sign changes while the unsigned area approaches zero linearly in transverse displacement, yielding an absolute-value-type cusp. At a zero spanning vector, the plane orientation is also undefined. Thus soft assignment does not make the score differentiable there; log-sum-exp is smooth only if its input scores are. This contradicts an unqualified gradient-flow claim through triangle area (line 21). The exact singular sets include collinear triples (including repeated/ coincident vertices) and any point where the proposed 32-D orientation convention loses rank. Near the ball boundary the conformal factor diverges, and numerical clipping can introduce additional nondifferentiable boundaries.

3. **Invariants (lines 17, 23).** (i) Zero area for collinear triples and sign reversal under swapping `q,z` are valid for an actually oriented plane and a consistent signed simplex; but neither follows from the 32-D frame prescription. (ii) Gauss–Bonnet is correct for a nondegenerate geodesic triangle in curvature `-c`, with unsigned interior angles and the orientation sign separately attached. At degeneracy it is only a limiting statement, not a test of sign convention. (iii) The *unsigned* area and distance are invariant under common hyperbolic isometries. The signed area is invariant only under orientation-preserving isometries; a reflection reverses it. “Orientation transformed consistently” can preserve a value only if the orientation structure is transformed as extra state, not under an arbitrary common isometry with a fixed ambient convention. In particular, the score is not reflection-invariant as a scalar score: `-A` changes sign while the distance does not. (iv) The area term tends to zero as `c→0` for fixed bounded configurations, but that does not establish that assignments depend on curvature: distance remains and can determine every argmin. The variance condition in line 17 is not a proof that area affects selected assignments; a high-variance term can leave all argmins unchanged. (v) Code/token counts are bookkeeping invariants, not consequences of the area operator.

4. **Flat limit/control (lines 17, 23–25).** In the flat limit the area term vanishes, but the score becomes `β||q-z||²`; this is an ordinary Euclidean nearest-code assignment. That is not necessarily the proposal's frozen control unless its metric and target are explicitly identical. Also, assignment continuity at `c=0` is not guaranteed at distance ties: an arbitrarily small curved area perturbation can flip a tied or nearly tied argmin. State how ties are handled and compare the full assignment against the exact `c=0` control, not only the area variance.

5. **Capacity/refuted-form check.** No code-count change is explicit (lines 19, 23), but the area score is only one signed scalar derived from two rays, and the proposed “orientation” may effectively add a learned/fixed frame or an implicit coordinate convention. The frame is especially consequential in 32-D and could silently make assignment coordinate-dependent. It is not a radius-only score if correctly defined, but line 50 overstates the distinction: a 2-D determinant/wedge is a coordinate expression of oriented area, and in higher dimensions no single signed scalar exists without extra orientation structure.

**Minimal revision:** restrict the area operator to an explicitly oriented 2-D totally geodesic subspace (and specify how all points are mapped into it), or replace signed area with a precisely defined 32-D invariant such as unsigned simplex area and revise the sign-based claims. Specify behavior at coincident/collinear triples, use a smooth regularization if gradients are required there, define boundary handling and tie-breaking, and demonstrate argmin changes against the exact Euclidean control. Recheck that any added frame/subspace does not introduce extra learned capacity or a role-layout equivalent.

## Mechanism 2 — non-associative gyro-composition quantization

**Verdict: REJECT as written; REVISE from the operator definition.** The first score term can be made well-defined on the open ball, but the second term mixes a gyrovector-space operation with tangent vectors and a tangent metric at the wrong basepoint. The stated invariants do not repair that type error.

1. **Well-definedness and domain (proposal lines 29–38).** Möbius addition is defined for interior ball points and maps interior points to interior points. For strict interior `p,x,z`, `Log_p(x)` is a tangent vector and `Exp_p(r)=x`; `Log_o(z)` is defined. If `T_s` is a linear map on `T_o`, its output is a valid finite tangent vector and `Exp_o` returns an interior point. Thus the nested additions and `Log_p` in the first term are defined (barring numerical overflow/clamping).

   The second term is not well-typed as written: `gyr[p,b]` is a gyrovector linear map acting on a ball-coordinate vector (or an origin-based gyrovector), whereas `Exp_p(r)` is a *point* in the ball, not a tangent vector at `p`. Subtracting that point from `Exp_p(r)` after gyration is not a tangent-space difference at `p`, and measuring it with `g_p` does not make it one. A gyration is not intrinsically a map on `T_p` in these coordinates. The statement that `v_k=PT_{o→p}(Log_o(z_k))` and `u_k=Exp_p(v_k)` (line 33) is dead notation: neither `v_k` nor `u_k` occurs in the score (lines 35–36). This leaves the asserted transported semantics disconnected from the actual first term.

   Repeated items (`x=p`) give `r=0`, which is valid for the first term, but the second remains undefined by the type mismatch. Collinear triples are not inherently singular for Möbius addition/gyration; however, if all points coincide or a gyration input degenerates, any claimed “rotation angle” must have a defined zero convention. A degenerate host coincident with the origin is valid: `Log_o`, `Exp_o`, and addition remain defined; the mismatch persists. Exact boundary points are outside the domain. Interior points approaching the boundary can cause very large tangent norms and numerical instability, and clipping changes the stated operator.

2. **Finiteness and differentiability (lines 35–42).** On the open ball, with finite parameters, the first term is smooth: `Log_p(y)` is defined even at `y=p` and equals zero there; unlike a sphere log map, there is no cut locus in the Poincaré ball. The displayed second term has no well-defined finite/differentiable value until its arguments are repaired. After repair, expected sensitive regions include near-boundary points (large `λ_p`, large `Log` norms), any explicit normalization/angle calculation at a zero vector, and the softmin-to-hard-argmin transition (the latter is not differentiable at ties, although a softmin itself is). “Bounded and invertible” for `T_s` does not bound its condition number unless specified uniformly; invertibility alone does not prevent large gradients.

3. **Invariants (line 44).** (i) The identity laws for Möbius addition hold for points in the ball. (ii) The `c→0` addition limit is vector addition and gyration tends to identity, for fixed finite inputs. (iii) Gyrations are orthogonal/isometric in the gyrovector metric convention at the origin; this does not imply preservation of the Riemannian tangent metric `g_p` on a vector at `p`, as line 36 assumes. Parallel transport is norm-preserving when correctly defined between tangent spaces, but the score does not use the declared transported `v_k` at all, so invariant (v) is irrelevant to the operative score. (iv) Non-associativity is generic for suitable non-collinear inputs, not guaranteed for every non-collinear triple; special symmetries and zero/collinear inputs yield coincident association orders. The proposal must quantify “generic” and test actual pairs. (vi) Closure of Möbius addition plus `Exp` keeps exact mathematical outputs interior, but numerical clamping and finite precision require a specified policy. (vii) Again, code/token count is bookkeeping.

4. **Flat limit/control (lines 35–46).** As `c→0`, with the displayed first term, the composed point becomes `p + T_s z + r`; applying the Euclidean log at `p` gives `T_s z+r`, so the first score becomes `||T_s z+r||²` (up to the chosen limiting metric convention). That is a Euclidean residual-composition quantizer, not a harmless zero-operation baseline: it can change assignments relative to a nearest-residual or reconstruction control. The second term tends to zero only if it is repaired as a curvature-induced difference and the limit is taken consistently. Consequently “if `c→0` reproduces assignments, kill it” is not sufficient: identical assignments may occur despite a nonzero curved term, and differing assignments may arise from the Euclidean composition/reparameterisation itself.

5. **Capacity/refuted-form check.** The fixed three 256-way codebooks and three tokens are preserved in the text, but the learned invertible `T_s` adds a full per-stage linear reparameterisation (potentially 32×32 parameters per stage). A norm or isotropy constraint is needed if it is not intended to add capacity. More importantly, line 33 explicitly transports origin code semantics to the host, and line 40 calls this the code's canonical semantic; that is structurally close to the already-refuted transported-residual-frame family. The unused `PT` path makes it unclear whether the actual mechanism is transported-frame quantisation or Möbius composition. The Euclidean limit is a residual-plus-code vector comparison, also close to the refuted Euclidean/reparameterised form. Lines 52–53 therefore do not establish that the operative gain is uniquely hyperbolic.

**Minimal revision:** define the gyration penalty in one space with compatible types—for example, specify an explicit differential/isometry mapping on `T_p` and compare tangent vectors there, or compare ball points using a stated Riemannian distance. Remove the unused `v_k/u_k` or use them consistently in the operative score; state the exact gyrovector convention and metric. Constrain/report `T_s` parameter count and conditioning, and include a matched `c=0` control with the same `T_s`, score, and optimization. Explain why the revised operator is not the refuted transported-frame form. Until these are done, no candidate run can test the stated mechanism.

## Overall decision

Neither candidate is ready for an `APPROVE_FOR_JOIN_E` decision. Mechanism 1 is potentially testable after fixing the 32-D orientation/sign definition and its nonsmooth degeneracies. Mechanism 2 has a fundamental score type error and a likely confound from unused transport notation and learned reparameterisation. Any revised candidate must still obey the frozen protocol and clear **all three** strict thresholds; mathematical plausibility or the abstract-only retrieval precedent in `literature.md` is not evidence of that outcome.
