# Adversarial mathematical review

Scope: this review uses the definitions in `proposals.md` literally. A mathematical operator being numerically implementable after clipping is not the same as its being defined on the declared domain. The frozen 32-D/3×256/three-token protocol and strict conjunctive thresholds are noted, but cannot repair operator defects.

## Mechanism 1 — Signed geodesic-triangle-area assignment

**Verdict: REVISE. Do not approve as written.** The area objective is promising only after its orientation and degeneracy conventions are made mathematically unambiguous, and the claimed gradient behavior at degeneracies is withdrawn or regularized.

1. **Well-definedness on the full input domain (proposal lines 11–17).** The Poincaré ball is geodesically convex, so for three strictly interior points the geodesic simplex exists; collinear triples can consistently be assigned unsigned area zero. But a *signed* area is not defined by the prescription in line 15 in 32 dimensions. The plane spanned by the two tangent vectors has its own orientation, while an ordered 2-frame at `p` does not orient every 2-plane in a canonical way. One possible sign is the determinant of the two vectors' projections onto that frame, but it vanishes for non-collinear vectors whose plane projects degenerately, and changes sign across that zero set. The phrase “orientation fixed once … and the sign taken in the plane spanned” does not select a globally continuous sign. Repeated vertices and collinear triples have zero area, but do not fix this ambiguity nearby. Inputs on the ball boundary are excluded by “same Poincaré ball” only if that means the *open* ball; state this explicitly. The angle-defect formula is valid for nondegenerate geodesic triangles, not a definition of a globally signed, differentiable area in dimension 32.

2. **Finiteness/differentiability and exact singularities (lines 11–17, 21).** For strictly interior points, `d_c(q,z)^2` is finite, including `q=z`; distance squared is smooth at coincidence in a normal neighborhood. The area is finite for interior vertices, but its signed version under the proposed frame rule is discontinuous or nondifferentiable when the projected orientation determinant crosses zero. Triangle angle derivatives are singular/ill-conditioned as a triangle collapses (collinearity, repeated vertices); the area itself has a zero extension there, but generally has no unique derivative for the stated signed convention. `Log_p(x)` and `Exp_p` are smooth for interior points; no `Log`-at-base singularity occurs for `x=p` (the log is zero). As points approach the boundary, conformal factors and `artanh` diverge; clamping may prevent numerical infinities but changes the operator and its gradients. Softmin does not remove nonsmoothness in the area term.

3. **Invariant counterexamples (lines 23(i–iii)).** Collinear area zero holds for the unsigned simplex, but the signed extension is unspecified; a sign convention based on a fixed 2-frame can assign zero or unstable sign to non-collinear planes with zero frame projection. Swapping `q,z` reverses an orientation defined by the ordered pair, but this is not guaranteed by an unspecified “sign taken in the plane.” Gauss–Bonnet gives unsigned angle defect (in a hyperbolic plane); it does not independently establish the proposed sign. Isometry invariance of signed area fails if the fixed frame at `p` is held fixed under an isometry rather than pushed forward. “With orientation transformed consistently” can make it true, but that is an extra transformation rule and must be operationally defined. Distance isometry invariance itself is fine.

4. **Flat limit/control (lines 17, 23(iv), 50).** For a fixed nondegenerate triangle of bounded size, hyperbolic area tends to Euclidean area as `c→0`, not to zero. The angle-defect formula has a numerator also tending to zero, with the quotient tending to the Euclidean triangle area. Thus the assertion that curvature-integrated area vanishes in the flat limit is false: Gaussian curvature tends to zero while the simplex area stays finite, and their ratio/angle defect has a finite limit. Consequently the proposed c→0 control is not the stated one. If the intended signal is *curvature times area* or angle defect, define that quantity explicitly and reassess its units and limit.

5. **Capacity/reparameterisation audit (lines 11–23).** The declared 256 choices and three emitted indices are unchanged; no direct capacity increase is specified. However, each codeword remains a free 32-D point and the score's area orientation requires an extra global frame convention, which is a new structural parameter/choice. The area plus distance objective is not merely a norm comparison at nonzero curvature, but its asserted “non-Euclidean-only” distinction is invalid because area has a nonzero Euclidean limit. The validation variance gate (line 17) is a diagnostic, not a proof of a hyperbolic contribution.

**Minimal revision:** Define an intrinsic orientation for each relevant 2-plane (or abandon signed area and use a specified unsigned area), explicitly restrict all points to the open ball, and either exclude/regularize degenerate triangles with a differentiable formula or stop claiming gradients there. Correct the c→0 claim/control: test the difference from Euclidean triangle area, or use a separately justified curvature-dependent statistic. Then recompute all invariants and retain the frozen threshold test unchanged.

## Mechanism 2 — Non-associative gyro-composition quantization

**Verdict: REJECT as written; REVISE only if the score is rebuilt with type-correct operations.** The second score term is not a well-typed tangent-space quantity, and the flat control reduces to a Euclidean residual code assignment.

1. **Well-definedness on the full input domain (proposal lines 29–38).** Möbius addition is defined for points in the open ball and maps such points to the open ball. `Log_p(x)` is a tangent vector; `Exp_p(r)` is a point. In line 36, `gyr[...] Exp_p(r)` is therefore (under the stated standard gyration) a point, and subtracting `Exp_p(r)` is a coordinate difference of points, not a tangent vector at `p`. Applying `||·||_{g_p}` to that difference is not defined as a Riemannian tangent norm. If gyration instead acts on tangent vectors, its argument must be a tangent vector (e.g. `r`), not `Exp_p(r)`. Also line 33 defines transported `v_k` and `u_k`, but neither is used in the actual score; the score instead exponentiates `T_s Log_o(z)` at `o`, adds it to `p`, and never uses the declared parallel transport. This makes the semantic/operator description internally inconsistent. The `T_s` domain is `T_o` in the formula, so calling it a tangent transport is misleading; invertibility alone does not ensure the claimed boundedness unless a bound is specified. Interior `x,p,z` are required; boundary points are outside the domain.

2. **Finiteness/differentiability and exact singularities (lines 33–42).** For strictly interior inputs, the first `Log_p` is finite, including `x=p`; the composition result remains interior and `Log_p` of that result is finite, including when its argument equals `p`. There is no intrinsic `Log`-at-base singularity. Boundary approach causes `Log`/`Exp` conditioning and conformal-factor blowup; clipping makes the effective map nonsmooth at the clip. The second term as written has no valid metric norm, so it is not a finite scalar score in the claimed Riemannian sense. After a type-correct repair, the smooth maps are differentiable on the open ball, but any hard argmin is discontinuous at code-score ties; a softmin can smooth the selection, not fix invalid score typing or boundary singularities. Parallel transport and gyration need consistent tangent-space basepoints.

3. **Invariant counterexamples (lines 44(i–vi)).** `u⊕0=0⊕u=u` is true for Möbius addition with the stated convention. The c→0 addition/gyration limits are true for fixed bounded inputs. Gyration is an orthogonal/metric-preserving map in its proper tangent/vector setting, not justification for applying it to a point and taking an arbitrary coordinate difference. The “two association orders differ” claim is only generic, not universal: it fails for zero inputs, collinear/special configurations, and cases where the residual is fixed by the gyration; the invariant must say “there exist/generic inputs” and specify the tested quantity. Parallel transport preserves Riemannian tangent norms when correctly defined, but line 33's transported vector is unused, so this invariant does not validate the scored operator. Strict interior closure follows absent clipping, but any clamp rate changes the map and gradients.

4. **Flat limit/control (lines 35–46, 52).** With fixed interior coordinates and `c→0`, `⊕` becomes vector addition, `Exp`/`Log` become their flat limits, and gyration becomes identity. The first term then becomes, up to the stated tangent metric convention, a squared Euclidean residual-plus-code comparison: `||code + residual||²` (with `p` cancelling). This is not harmlessly “the same assignments” in general; it is a Euclidean residual quantizer and may change assignments relative to the baseline, depending on the code sign/decoder convention. It is nevertheless exactly a Euclidean reformulation of the scored composition in the limit, contrary to the “WHY NOT EUCLIDEAN” claim. The c→0 control must compare against this explicit Euclidean control, not assume it reproduces baseline assignments.

5. **Capacity/reparameterisation audit (lines 33–46).** Three codebooks of 256 entries and three output indices are nominally preserved. But `T_s`, if learned independently per stage, adds a learned invertible linear reparameterisation of each code semantic; its parameter count/bounds are unspecified. More importantly, in the flat limit the first score is a Euclidean residual-plus-code norm, and the allegedly curvature-specific second term vanishes. This is a hidden Euclidean residual quantizer plus a curved perturbation, not a mechanism that cannot be represented by Euclidean composition. The unused `PT`/`u_k` further mean the claimed transported-code construction is not actually the assignment operator.

**Minimal revision:** Specify a single consistent type convention: use gyration on a tangent vector and measure its change in the appropriate tangent metric, or map both points back to a common tangent space before subtraction. Reconcile/remove the unused `v_k,u_k,PT` definitions and state exactly where each transport acts. Bound and parameterize `T_s`. Replace the universal association invariant with a generic-input test and publish the explicit Euclidean c→0 score/assignment control. Keep the frozen code/token counts and three strict metric gates; no performance result can waive these mathematical fixes.
